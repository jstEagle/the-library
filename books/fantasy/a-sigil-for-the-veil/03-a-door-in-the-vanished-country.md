## 3. A Door in the Vanished Country

*Cyrus*

He had been running for three days when Sera found him.

Not with torches and zealots at her back, as he'd half expected. Not with the cold apology of a captain cutting loose dead weight. She came alone, out of the snowfall on the high shoulder of Mount Cael, walking toward his fire with her hands open at her sides, the way you approach a wounded animal or an old friend who has reason to bite.

Cyrus's hand went to his knife anyway. It was the only honest thing left in him.

"Before you speak," he said, "know that I watched them burn Hallowmere. I stood on that ridge and I watched, because you told me to run, and I ran. So whatever you're about to say—"

"It was staged." Her breath came out white. "The betrayal. All of it."

The words landed like stones dropped down a well; he listened for the bottom and heard nothing.

"You sold me," Cyrus said. "You put my name in their mouths. You gave them our route, our signals, everything. I saw the ledger page, Sera. Your handwriting."

"I know what I wrote." She crouched across the fire from him, close enough that the heat touched her face but no closer. "I needed it to be real. The Ashborn don't read faces, Cyrus — they read *certainty*. If they suspected for one heartbeat that we were lying, they'd have burned every village between here and the coast looking for you, and then they'd have burned us. But a traitor? A traitor they understand. A traitor they reward. They gave me a map, Cyrus." She pulled something from her coat and held it out across the flames. "They gave me the way into the vanished country. Because they think I'm delivering you to them."

He didn't take it. His hands wouldn't move. Three days of running had carved him hollow, and inside the hollow there was nothing but the memory that wasn't his — the door, the erased nation, his own face standing before it like a man come home.

"And are you?" he asked quietly. "Delivering me?"

Sera met his eyes across the fire, and whatever she saw there made her flinch. Good, some bitter thing in him thought. Let her see what she made.

"I'm delivering you to yourself," she said. "It's the same road. That's the whole horror of it."

* * *

*Sera*

She had lied to better men than Cyrus Brightwater and slept soundly after. This was different. Watching him study the Ashborn map by firelight, tracing the borders of a country that hadn't existed in two hundred years — Vhalen, drowned off every chart, struck from every treaty — she felt the lie she'd lived for a week begin to curdle into something else. Truth, maybe. Truth was always worse.

"The pass is here," he said at last. "Through the Cinder Teeth. But Sera — these mountains were never Vhaleni soil. The country ended south of the river. Why would its door be here?"

"Because countries don't end where maps say they do." She fed a branch to the fire. "They end where people stop remembering them. And somebody wanted Vhalen remembered somewhere very specific, behind a veil, in a place no one would go."

Cyrus looked up. In the firelight his eyes had that quality she'd been pretending not to notice for six years — the quality of a window lit in an empty house. As if someone were home, but not the someone who'd built the house.

"When did you first suspect?" he asked. "What I am."

"Two winters ago. You cut a memory from a smuggler outside Greyharrow — a good cut, clean, no scar. And afterward you couldn't remember your mother's voice. You laughed it off, said you'd never known it." She held his gaze. "Nobody laughs off a thing like that, Cyrus. Unless they've got nothing underneath the laugh."

He was quiet a long time. Snow ticked against the rocks around them like slow applause.

"Then let's go meet whoever built me," he said.

* * *

*Cyrus*

The Cinder Teeth took two days and gave back nothing but wind and black stone, and then, on the third morning, the world changed.

There was no border. No wall, no gate, no shimmering ward. There was only a moment, crossing a frozen stream, when the light went strange — too gold, too warm for the season — and Cyrus looked up and saw a city.

It stood in a valley that no surveyor had ever charted: towers of pale stone hung with banners that moved without wind, streets laid out in patterns like script, windows glowing amber in buildings that had no right to stand after two centuries of abandonment. And none of it was real. He knew it the way he knew his own name — which, he understood now, was not a reliable form of knowing at all. The city was memory. Dead Vhalen, remembered so fiercely and so long that the remembering had taken on architecture. Ghosts of streets. Echoes of a nation, standing like buildings.

"They're still in there," Sera breathed beside him. "The memories. Look —"

And he looked, and he saw them: figures moving in the golden streets, translucent as breath on glass. A woman shaking out laundry that would never dry. Children chasing a hoop down a lane that led nowhere. A bell ringing in a tower, soundless, forever. The dead nation going about its dead afternoon, over and over, at the bottom of a valley like a cupped hand.

"The zealots' maps said the door is beneath the bell tower," Sera said. "In the old palace quarter. Are you ready?"

"No," Cyrus said honestly. "But I've spent my whole life readying other people for things I never expected to need myself. Seems fair it works both ways."

They descended together.

* * *

*Sera*

The city accepted them the way deep water accepts a stone. The ghost-figures didn't see them — or pretended not to — but the memories pressed close, thick as fog, and Sera felt them tugging at her own past. Once she turned sharply because she smelled woodsmoke and river water, the exact smell of her grandmother's house in Thornfell, and for a vertiginous moment she could not remember whether the house had been real or whether this place was already feeding on her, showing her own memories back to her dressed as streets.

"Cyrus." She caught his sleeve. "Stay with me. Whatever it shows you, stay with me."

But he had stopped walking. He was staring at a fountain in a small square, and his face had gone the color of the snow on the peaks above them.

"I know this square," he said. "Not from maps. From *inside*. I stood here. I talked to someone — a woman with grey braid — she was telling me—" His hand rose to his temple, to the old ache behind his eye where he kept the stolen god-memory sealed. "Sera. These aren't strangers' memories. Some of these are *mine*. They were mine before they were anyone's."

The truth arrived then, the way floods arrive — not all at once but everywhere at once. She took his arm and walked him through it, piece by piece, because someone had to and because she loved him and love, she had learned, was mostly the willingness to say terrible things gently.

"You were made," she said. "From cut memories. Someone gathered them — from Vhalen, from the dying god, from a hundred minds — and built a vessel to carry the sigil to this door. And then, Cyrus—" her voice caught and she hated it— "and then the crew found you half-frozen in a smuggler's wagon with no past at all, and instead of asking what you were, we just... decided you were ours."

He was crying. She had never seen him cry, not over cuts, not over wounds, not over anything.

"All those years," he said. "Every birthday we invented for me. Every story you told about my childhood to make me feel real."

"We weren't lying to you." She gripped his shoulders, forced his eyes to hers. "We were building you. Memory by memory, year by year. Whoever made you gave you a purpose, Cyrus. We gave you a *life*. Do you understand the difference? Their version of you ends at that door. Ours doesn't have to end anywhere."

Above them, soundless, the bell in the tower began — impossibly — to ring.

* * *

*Cyrus*

The bell's toll went through him like a hook through silk, and the seal behind his eye tore open.

The god's memory unfolded across the ghost-city like ink in water, and Cyrus finally saw it whole: the door beneath the tower, not stone but absence, a seam in the world where the veil had been stitched shut by hands that had since gone to dust. And standing before the door in the oldest layer of the memory — his own face. Younger. Unfinished. A sketch of himself, waiting.

The voice that had answered him that first night on the mountain spoke again, and now it did not whisper. It filled the golden streets, shook the phantom banners, set the ghost-figures kneeling in their endless afternoon.

***Vessel. You have brought my name home.***

"I'm not your vessel," Cyrus said, and his voice cracked on it, grief and fury braided together. "I'm the cook's bad influence. I'm Sera's second-in-command. I owe Finn eleven crowns. I am *theirs*, and they chose me when you would only use me."

***And yet you stand at my door.***

Behind him he heard boots on the ghost-stones — Sera, and behind Sera, impossibly, Finn and Marisol and old Tam, breathless, frost-bearded, having followed the Ashborn's staged trail all the way into a country that didn't exist. His family. Chosen, choosing, chosen.

The bell rang again. Beneath the tower, the seam in the world began, slowly, to open.
