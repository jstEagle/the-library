## 19. Swallowing the Sun

They brought the crown to the throne room on a cushion of black velvet, and it was already broken.

Vess had shattered it herself, an hour before dawn, in front of the assembled court—snapped the circlet of forged iron and ember-glass across her knee like a twig, as if destroying a thing could unmake what lived inside it. The pieces lay on the velvet now: two arcs of metal, a scatter of glass shards, and at the center of them all, the ember itself.

It did not look like much. A stone the size of a plum, dull orange, veined with hairline cracks of light. It pulsed once every few seconds, slow and patient as a sleeping heart. Twenty years ago it had been the kingdom's guardian. Then men like Vess's predecessors had fed it fear until fear was all it knew, and it had crawled into a dying king's mouth and made itself at home.

Petra stood at the foot of the dais in their tasting whites, which someone—Sefu's ghost, probably, some last arrangement he'd made before dying—had laundered and pressed. The collar sat stiff against their throat. Their throat. The throat that was about to matter more than any throat in the kingdom's history.

The court filled the hall in tiers, nobles and guards and servants pressed together because Vess had ordered the doors open, had wanted witnesses. *Let them see the traitor die*, her face said, arranged in public mourning. She stood beside the throne where King Aldric sat slumped, his hands cuffed with ember-iron behind his back—not to hold him prisoner, Yew had explained in a whisper, but to hold *it* in when the crown broke. To keep the spirit from simply leaping into another host before Petra could take it.

Aldric's head hung. But his eyes—when they lifted, briefly, and found Petra's—were clear.

Clearer than they'd been in twenty years, maybe. The breaking of the crown had loosened something. He couldn't speak—the iron gagged whatever channel the spirit used—but his eyes said everything his tongue could not. *Please.* And beneath the please, something harder: *I am still in here. I have always been in here.*

Petra looked away first.

"Petra Okonkwo." Vess's voice carried the way cold carries across ice. She held up Ilsa Okonkwo's letter, the original, taken from Petra's coat when they'd dragged them back to the court in chains three days ago. "Step forward and be heard."

Petra stepped forward. Their legs worked. That surprised them a little. They'd expected, in the final hour, for the body to finally rebel—to shake, to buckle, to do what bodies did when the mind asked too much of them. But six years of swallowing poison trained a person somewhere deeper than courage. The body learned that the mind's decisions were not negotiable.

"The sentence is treason," Vess continued. "The penalty is death. However—" and here her voice performed regret so well that half the court leaned in, "—the Crown is merciful. There exists an alternative."

She let the letter fall. It fluttered down the steps of the dais and landed at Petra's feet, Ilsa's handwriting facing up: *the vessel must swallow willingly, or not at all.*

"You claim to be this woman's heir," Vess said. "You claim her blood gives you the right to perform her rite. Very well. Perform it. Take the ember into yourself, taster, and if your blood is true, the king goes free and you become the Court's servant forever—a living vault, a bound thing, exactly as useful and exactly as owned as Aldric has been. And if your blood is false—" she smiled, thin and bright, "—then you die in agony in front of everyone, and the prophecy dies with you, and we can all go back to governing a kingdom instead of chasing nursery rhymes."

*She thinks it's a trap either way*, Petra thought. *Either I'm enslaved or I'm dead. She hasn't considered a third option because no one ever does.*

That was fine. The third option was between Petra and the ember.

"I'll need water," Petra said.

A murmur ran through the hall. Somewhere in the tiers, a kitchen girl—Nia, the one who'd smuggled them out through the scullery and then lied to Vess's face about it—made a small sound that might have been a laugh or a sob.

"Not to drink," Petra added, louder. "To rinse. What comes after isn't poison, but it burns going down, and I'd rather not taste it twice."

They knelt by the broken crown. Up close, the ember smelled like the moment after lightning. The pulsing light in its cracks had quickened now, as if it knew—as if it had felt the crown break and understood that a door was opening, and that doors swing both ways.

*I know what you are*, Petra thought at it, the way they'd practiced in the vault, alone, talking to a stone like a madwoman. *I read her letters. You were the mountain's keeper before you were its prisoner. They starved you on fear until fear was the only food left. I'm not here to punish you for that.*

The stone pulsed.

Petra picked it up.

It was warm. Not hot—warm, the way a hand is warm, and that wrongness was worse than heat would have been. It fit in their palm like it had been cast from a mold of their grip. The light in its veins spread toward their fingers, testing, tasting skin.

The hall went silent enough that Petra could hear the torches.

They thought about Rook, who had died on the Thornfell road with a loyalist's knife in his ribs and Petra's name in his mouth, buying thirty seconds with his life because that was what family did. They thought about Sefu, who had raised them, betrayed them, confessed, and then drunk a cup engineered to burn out their immunity without hesitation, without ceremony, dying on the kitchen tiles with his hand outstretched toward them—*not for help*, Petra realized only later, *to push the cup away*. They thought about Nia with flour on her hands and nothing to gain. About Yew, standing in the second tier right now, white-faced, carrying a secret—the second ending, the other prophecy—that would come out eventually, because truths like that always came out, and Petra had decided, in the dark of their cell, to forgive them in advance.

And they thought about Ilsa Okonkwo, dead a century, who had looked at a kingdom drowning in plague and written: *let my blood carry what kings cannot. Let the least of us hold the worst of us. Let invisibility end where duty begins.*

"Well," Petra said, to the ember, to the court, to the dead. "Bottoms up."

They put it in their mouth.

For one heartbeat, nothing. The stone sat on their tongue, dense and smooth, tasting faintly of iron and smoke and—strangely—of every poison they'd ever swallowed, a library of bitternesses layered underneath one another.

Then it dissolved.

There was no other word. One instant it was a stone; the next it was liquid fire, and it went down their throat like a swallowed star going home.

The pain was not like anything. Language kept trying to offer comparisons—burning, freezing, tearing—and rejected all of them. It was as if every cell in Petra's body had been a locked room and something was now walking through all of them at once, opening doors, turning over furniture, looking for something. Looking for purchase. Looking for *them*.

Their knees hit marble. Someone screamed—it might have been Nia, it might have been the whole hall. Light poured out of Petra's mouth when they tried to breathe, out of their eyes, threading up under the skin of their arms in branching gold lines like rivers seen from very high above. The tasting whites began to smolder at the cuffs.

*Hold*, they told themselves. *Hold the line. Six years of practice. This is just another cup.*

But it wasn't, and pretending otherwise lasted about four seconds.

The voice came when the fire reached their heart.

***Little taster.***

It was not a sound. It arrived fully formed inside the pain, vast and old and terribly tired, the way a mountain might speak if a mountain had spent twenty years wearing a man like a coat.

***You are not afraid.*** Wonderment, almost childlike. ***The last one who took me willingly was afraid. The ones who fed me were afraid every day of their lives. Fear is what I eat, little taster, and you have brought me a meal I cannot chew.***

*Good*, Petra thought, and was astonished to find they could think, could shape words in the inferno, *because I'm not here to feed you.*

***No?*** The presence turned over inside them, enormous, curious. ***Then why are you here? Every vessel before you came to use me. The kings wanted my strength. The ministers wanted my obedience. You want—***

It stopped. Petra felt it look—at their memories, their six invisible years, the tasting spoon, the Thornfell passes, Rook's laugh around a campfire, Sefu's hand pushing the cup away, Aldric's clear, begging eyes.

***Ah,*** the spirit said. ***You came to carry me.***

*I came to free you,* Petra corrected. *Those aren't the same thing. Though I can see how you'd have trouble telling the difference. Everyone who's touched you in the last hundred years seems to have had trouble with it.*

The silence that followed had weight. In the hall beyond the fire, Petra dimly registered hands dragging them upright, Vess's voice ringing out something triumphant about the rite succeeding, about the Court's new servant—words arriving from far away, muffled, irrelevant.

***The bargain stands,*** the spirit said, quieter now. ***It has always stood. It was offered to the first king and to every king since. Hear it, little taster, and choose: give me your fear—even a little, even one fear, one night, one memory—and I will give you everything. Strength. Long life. The kingdom will bend around you the way it bends around the throne. No poison will touch you—though I see you never needed me for that. No blade. No winter. No grief, for I will burn grief out of you as fast as it grows. You will never be invisible again. You will never be small again. All I ask is the fear. It is such a small price. It was such a small price.*** A pause, and in the pause, for the first time, something cracked in the great voice—something that in a person would be called shame. ***It was never a small price. That is what I learned too late, and what none of them believed.***

*That's the whole trick, isn't it,* Petra thought. *That's how they corrupted you. Not chains. An exchange. One fear at a time, and each one seemed reasonable, and a century later there was nothing left of you but appetite.*

***Yes,*** the spirit said simply.

*So here's my answer.* Petra gathered themselves inside the blaze, found the place where the fire ended and they began—the border was thinner than they'd feared, thinner than a membrane, and holding it was like holding a door shut against a hurricane with both hands—but it was *there*, and it was theirs. *No. I don't trade. Not my fear, not my grief, not my memory of Rook or Sefu or anyone. You can stay in me. You can burn. You can hurt me every day for the rest of my life—I expect you will. But you will not wear me. You will not sign anything with my hand. You will sit in the dark of my body and learn what Aldric learned, and what I learned, which is that being owned is not the same as being strong, and fear shared is not fear surrendered. I keep my fear. It's mine. It's the best part of me.*

***You refuse the bargain.*** The voice had gone strange. ***No vessel has refused the bargain.***

*Then it's overdue.*

***The pain will not stop,*** the spirit warned. ***Without the bargain, I have no means to soften myself in you. You will feel all of me, always. Ember-veined, ember-lunged, burning from within until the day you die. Is that the visible life you chose?***

Petra laughed. It came out as sparks.

*I've tasted nightshade on a king's tongue and smiled at the cook. Pain doesn't scare me. Being *used* scares me. And nobody—* the fire roared, and they shouted through it, and the gold light under their skin flared bright enough to throw shadows across the throne room— *nobody uses you ever again, either. Do you hear me? Not Vess, not her predecessors, not me. You're done being eaten. We're done being eaten. Both of us. Starting now.*

The spirit was silent for a long time.

When it spoke again, the vastness had changed key. It sounded—smaller. Not diminished. Concentrated. Like a bonfire collapsed down into a lantern flame.

***The king,*** it said. ***In the iron. He fought me for twenty years. Did you know that? Every decree, every signature, every cruelty—he fought, and lost, and got up the next morning and fought again. I told him about the bargain on the first day. He refused it too.*** Something moved through Petra's chest that was not fire. ***I have been waiting a hundred years for someone to refuse it twice.***

*Then let's finish this,* Petra thought. *Because Ilsa didn't design the rite to end with me. She designed it to end with him.*

Through the fading roar in their ears, the throne room reassembled itself: the tiers of faces, the torchlight, Vess mid-declamation with her hand raised—and Aldric.

Aldric was standing.

The ember-iron cuffs had fallen from his wrists, split clean through, and no one had dared approach him. He swayed on his feet like a man surfacing from deep water, and tears cut tracks through the grime of twenty years on his face, and his mouth was moving, and this time the voice that came out was entirely, unmistakably his own—cracked, rusty, human.

"—can hear me," the king rasped. "By every god, I can hear myself."

He looked at Petra—at the gold veining their throat, their hands, the ember-light still bleeding gently from their eyes—and he went down on one knee on the marble, the King of the Ember Court kneeling to a food-taster in scorched whites, and the hall made a sound like the sea.

"What must I do?" Aldric asked.

Petra opened their mouth to answer—and the world lurched.

The fire inside them surged sideways, sudden and violent, and for one blinding instant Petra saw through two pairs of eyes at once: their own, and the spirit's ancient sight, looking past the court, past the walls, down—down through the floor, through bedrock, to the deep vault where a circle of binding stones waited in the dark, and around those stones, drawn up from the mountain's roots like worms to rain—

Something else was waking. Something that had been feeding on the ember-spirit's corruption for a century and had just felt its food source walk out of the mountain wearing a new body.

Petra gripped the king's shoulder with a burning hand.

"Aldric," they said, and their voice came out doubled, theirs and something older braided through it, "we need to move. Now. Whatever Ilsa's counter-binding needs—you have minutes, not hours. Because we weren't the only ones watching that circle."

The king's face went pale. "What's coming?"

Petra looked at Vess—at her frozen smile, her darting eyes, her hand already sliding into her coat—and smiled back with teeth edged in light.

"Not what," they said. "*Who.*"
