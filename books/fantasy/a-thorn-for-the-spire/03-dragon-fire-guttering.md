## 3. Dragon-Fire, Guttering

**Ada**

We left the valley at dawn, when the mist still owned the ridgeline and the border stones were soft with frost. Mira walked beside me, not behind — she'd refused to be carried, refused the rope I'd offered, refused everything except my hand, which she'd taken without asking and hadn't let go since.

The moment we crossed the line of standing stones, she made a sound I'd never heard from her. Not pain. Recognition.

*Vessha,* she said, and pointed at the sky.

I felt it too. Like walking out of deep water. The valley had pressed on me for six years — a numbness I'd mistaken for peace. Out here, the world had weight again. My brand ached beneath its wrapping, and somewhere far off, something answered her word in a voice like a banked fire.

"That's him," I said. "Isn't it. The last one."

Mira looked up at me with those too-old eyes and spoke First Tongue until she ran out of breath, then switched to the broken common she'd been learning from me, word by stubborn word.

"He holds. He is tired. He is *so* tired, Ada."

I know how that feels, kid, I didn't say.

Behind us, on the valley's rim, riders gathered. I'd counted nine fires two nights back. They weren't coming in — they couldn't; their magic died at the border same as anyone's — but they didn't need to come in. They just needed us to come out.

So we did. Straight toward them, then east along the ridge where the trail narrowed to goat-width, then down into the ash country where nothing grows because nothing has grown there since the Spire was raised. Three days of hard travel. Mira never complained. She hummed instead — a low, looping melody in the First Tongue, and I began to understand it wasn't a song. It was a countdown.

* * *

**Mira**

The dream comes every time she closes her eyes now, awake or asleep, it doesn't matter which.

A hall of wings. A table of green stone. Nine voices speaking as one, and one of the nine going quiet mid-sentence — cut off, folded away, *unmade*. Her father's voice. She knows it even though she was an egg when he stopped being a voice and started being silence.

The humans call it betrayal. She has learned their word for it. But in the dream it does not feel like betrayal. It feels like a door closing so that something behind it could survive. Her father closed the door from the inside, and the thing behind it was her.

She wakes with ash in her mouth. Real ash. They are crossing the burn country, and Ada walks ahead of her with her sword loose in its scabbard and her shoulders set like a wall, and Mira loves her in the way the covenant loves: completely, and at cost.

Her hands are doing the thing again. Light under the skin, gold threading through the veins of her wrists like sap rising wrong. She pulls her sleeves down. Ada mustn't see yet. Not yet. There are things Mira has to say first, and the First Tongue is failing her in the human tongue, and she needs more days.

The dragon-fire in her dreams has guttered to a candle.

She counts her own heartbeats instead of sheep. She is down to four hundred a night.

* * *

**Ada**

The Spire rose out of the ash on the fifth day, black and crooked as a thorn, exactly as its name promised. It wasn't built. It had *grown* — some ancient act of will given bone, taller than any tower men ever raised, and around its base wound the church camp: white tents, white banners, and the sigil I knew better than my own heartbeat. My god's mark. The closed eye inside the open hand.

They were waiting for us. Of course they were. A woman in vestments stepped out before I could choose violence, hands open, empty.

"Ada Nakamura." She said my name like a debt collector. "The Church of the Silent Hand welcomes you home."

"I'm not home. I'm passing through."

"The Voice can be restored." She said it gently, the way you tell someone a dying parent is suffering. "You've felt it, haven't you — the silence, all these years. That silence was a wound He took for us. For *you*, especially. And it can be undone." Her eyes slid to Mira. "One small death. One seal, released. Your god speaks again. Your oath means something again. You go back to who you were."

And there it was. The bargain I'd have taken, once. Six years ago I'd have knelt in the ash and done it with tears in my eyes and called it devotion.

Mira's hand found mine. Small. Burning hot. Too hot.

"No," I said.

"Ada—"

"You heard me. No."

The priestess sighed like I was a difficult patient. "Then you'll watch her burn instead. The seal fails either way. The only question is what it fails *into*."

* * *

**Mira**

Inside the Spire, the air tastes like the inside of a bell.

They climb. Stairs of fused glass, spiraling up through the dark, and at every turn the heat rises and the light rises and Mira's skin rises to meet both, gold bleeding through her arms now past her elbows, past hiding.

At the heart of the Spire there is a hollow, and in the hollow there is the dragon.

He is smaller than her dreams made him. Ancient beyond reckoning, yes, vast, but *spent* — coiled around the covenant-stone like a dog around a grave, his scales gone gray as old iron, only the last ember of him burning low in his chest. When he sees her, the ember flares, and his voice fills her skull in the First Tongue, warm and ruined:

*Little seal. Little last light. You came.*

*You're dying,* she answers.

*I have been dying for nine hundred years. It is slow work.* His eye turns toward Ada, huge and yellow and knowing. *And this one. The god-touched. The branded. She carries his mark still.*

Ada's hand goes to her wrapped forearm. "What does he say?"

Mira translates, because some things should be shared. The dragon rumbles something that might, in an older tongue, have been laughter.

*Ask her if she wants her god's voice back. Ask her what she thinks he would say, if he could say anything at all.*

But Mira never gets to ask. Because the stone beneath them shivers, and the ember gutters, and the light under Mira's skin stops being light-under and becomes light-through, and the burning starts.

It doesn't hurt. That's the worst part. It feels like being *opened* — like every sealed door in her body swinging wide at once, and through the doors pours everything her father locked away to keep her safe: the whole weight of the broken covenant, looking for somewhere to live.

She hears Ada screaming her name from very far away.

She thinks: *four hundred heartbeats. Maybe fewer.*

She thinks: *I'm sorry. I wanted more days.*

* * *

**Ada**

There is no plan after that. There is only motion.

The priestess's people come up the stairs with white steel drawn, chanting the restoration liturgy, and I put myself between them and the child because that is the one oath left to me that hasn't lied. I kill two of them before they reach the hollow. I don't feel anything about it. Six years of numbness finally useful for something.

The dragon's voice cracks through the Spire like a collapsing roof, and everyone falls down but me — me, because I carry the god's mark, and whatever the dragon says next, he says it straight into the brand on my arm, and it burns like truth burns.

*"His silence was not a wound taken. It was a sentence served. He broke the covenant to hide the last seal — your god unmade his eight siblings' voices to keep one egg alive. Ask yourself, mercenary, why the church wants the seal dead and the voice restored. Whose voice do you think they are restoring?"*

And I understand. All of it, all at once, the way you understand a trap only from inside it. The silence isn't my god's sacrifice. It's his prison. And the church doesn't want to free *him* — they want to wear what's left of him, speak with his mouth, rule with his name. Kill the seal, take the throne of a silenced god.

Mira is on her knees at the covenant-stone, coming apart into gold light, and the priestess is shouting the final words of the liturgy, and the dragon's ember is guttering, guttering—

Every oath that kept me alive stands in a row and tells me no. Loyalty. Survival. The numb, safe exile. The bargain. All of it saying: kneel, Ada. Kneel and let it happen. Someone always pays. Better her than you.

My knife is in my hand. My sleeve is torn back. The brand — his mark, the closed eye, the open hand — stares up at me from my own arm like it's waiting.

"Well," I say, to the god who cannot answer, "here's the last prayer you'll get from me."

I drive the blade into the brand.

The hook for what comes next writes itself in fire.
