## 16. The King Who Used to Be

*The candle is lit.*

He knows this because he can see it, and seeing is the first thing that comes back each time — a small yellow flame reflected in the polished wood of his own desk, doubled, trebled in the inkwell's black water. The candle means evening. Evening means the day has happened without him.

Aldric of the House of Veyra, third of his name, King of the Nine Valleys and Warden of the Thornfell Passes, counts what he has: one candle, one desk, one hour — perhaps less, perhaps more; time inside the binding moves like water through cupped hands — and one thought, held so tightly it has worn smooth as river stone:

*I am still here.*

He does not know how long he has been here. He knows there was a twelfth moon of last year, and that on that night he did not sleep, and that when he woke — if waking is the word for it — something else was wearing his hands. He knows the thing feeds on being loved. He knows it speaks in his voice from balconies, and the crowds weep, and every tear makes it stronger, and every tear makes *him* smaller, because there is only so much of Aldric left to spend.

So he spends it carefully.

Tonight the surface-thoughts are quiet. The Devoted have finished their singing below; he can feel their devotion like warmth through a wall, like standing near an oven, and the thing that wears him is sated and slow, sunk deep in the gold of its own worship. That is when Aldric surfaces. That is when he can move the hands.

His hand moves. It takes up the pen.

*Write,* he tells himself. *Write while you can. Write like a man leaving a house on fire.*

* * *

The decree is already drafted on the desk — the thing's work, dictated to a trembling chancellor that morning. Aldric reads it with the part of him that once read everything twice, that made his tutors despair and his father proud in equal measure. *By order of the Crown, the granaries of the eastern valleys shall be opened without tariff, that no child of the realm go hungry through the winter.* Good words. Kind words. Words that will make ten thousand people love him by morning, and every ounce of that love will pour down through the cathedral floor into the dark where the stone sits, and the stone will drink, and the thing will grow, and Aldric will grow thinner.

But the decree is not finished. There is a margin. There is always a margin.

His hand drifts to the edge of the page, and there, in letters so small they might be flourishes, might be the idle habit of a king who doodles, he writes:

*Wren. If you ever see this. The maps you burned were true. I am sorry about the maps.*

The pen shakes. Not with weakness — with fury, the old clean fury that is the last thing the binding cannot digest, because rage is not devotion and the thing has no teeth for it.

*I am sorry about most things,* he adds. *Start with the maps.*

Below the chancellery seal, in the space where the royal signet will stamp its golden sun, he writes four more words, disguised as a pen trial, the kind of absent scratch any clerk would overlook:

*Do not save me. Run.*

Then, because he is still a king even hollowed, even halved, even spent down to his last clear hour — he signs the decree properly. The people must eat. Even damned decrees can feed children. He has learned to take his victories where they do not also feed the wolf.

* * *

There was a garden once.

He finds it sometimes, in the shallow water between surfacings — not a memory exactly, more like a room he can stand in while the tide is out. A walled garden behind the map-room, where a young cartographer used to spread her charts on the grass because the light was better there than in her cramped office, and a prince who should have been studying treaties used to come and crouch at the edge of her work and ask questions just to hear her explain things she loved.

Her name was Wren. Is Wren. He holds onto the *is* the way a drowning man holds a spar.

They exiled her for falsifying border maps, and he signed the order, because kings sign orders, and he did not fight for her, because princes do not yet understand that there are fights worth losing a crown over. He has had years inside the binding to understand it now. Years and years. The binding gives a man nothing but time to understand his mistakes.

In the garden-memory, she is pointing at a mountain range drawn in her own careful hand. *"This pass doesn't exist,"* she says, tapping it. *"See? I invented it. A smuggler's fiction. But look how well it lies — anyone reading this map would believe in that valley. Maps teach people what to believe about the world. That's why they're dangerous."*

He said something foolish then. He remembers saying something foolish. He cannot remember what, and it grieves him more than the lost years, more than the crown, more than almost anything — that he cannot remember the foolish thing he said that made her laugh.

*Hold onto the laugh,* he instructs himself. *You don't need the words. You only need to know there were words, and they worked.*

The tide comes back in. The garden floods. He goes under.

* * *

Surfacing again — how long? The candle has burned a thumb's width lower. His body has been walking. He can feel it in his legs, the ache of stairs climbed, marble under boots. The thing has been holding court in his absence, or rather in his presence, which amounts to the same cruelty. Somewhere below, a line of petitioners knelt and wept and were healed of small ailments, and each healing cost the stone a sip of them and fed the tenant of his skull.

But there is paper in front of him now. New paper. His hand is moving.

This is the trick he has learned, the one small door the binding leaves ajar: the thing does not watch his hands when they write. Writing is beneath it. Writing is servant's work. It wears the crown and the voice and the smile, but it disdains the pen, and so the pen belongs to Aldric, and every night — some nights — he commits his treasons in the margins.

Tonight the margin gets the important things, because he can feel himself thinning, feel thoughts going out of him like heat from an open window, and he has learned to triage. What must survive him?

*The binding stone is beneath the cathedral. Third crypt level, past the reliquary gate, where the oldest foundations meet the mountain rock. They built the throne above it on purpose. Every king since Veyra the First has sat on that stone. Ask why no king before me lived past fifty years of good health and then went strange. Ask why the histories call it "the Anointing."*

His handwriting is deteriorating. He presses harder.

*It is not new. Gods help me, it is not new. Whatever wears me wore my grandfather, and his grandmother's father, and back and back until the first of us chained it into service and called the chain a sacrament. We did not crown a god. We enslaved one, and it has been eating its keep ever since, and calling the eating holiness.*

He stops. Breathes — the body breathes whether he attends to it or not.

*If someone reads this: it can be unbound. Maghda of the Thornfells would know; her line kept the counter-rites. But hear me, whoever you are, hear me the way you'd hear a dying man because that is what I am: do not pay for me with yourselves. I have watched this thing trade in devotion for two years and I know its arithmetic. It will offer you bargains. Every word it says will be true. That is the worst thing about it — it never lies, it only costs. Refuse the bargain. Let me go if letting me go is cheaper than keeping me. A kingdom can survive a dead king. I no longer believe it survives a willing saint.*

He looks at what he has written. In the morning the thing will glance at these pages with his own eyes and see only a king's restless marginalia, scholarly scratches, nothing. It never reads closely. Contempt is the crack in it, and Aldric has built his whole resistance inside that crack, a smuggler's road of his own, hidden in plain sight.

*A smuggler's road,* he thinks, and almost laughs. *Wren would appreciate that.*

* * *

Some nights the thing notices him.

Not often. But there are evenings — he dreads them, marks them like red days on a calendar — when the feeding has been rich and the thing is playful, and it turns its attention inward the way a rich man strolls through his own cellars, checking the stores. Those nights Aldric hides in the deepest room he has, the garden-memory, the laugh he cannot reconstruct, the smell of rain on the map-room roof tiles, and he makes himself small and true, because truth is the only camouflage that works. The thing cannot digest what is genuinely his. It can wear his face and spend his voice, but the actual Aldric — the man who loved maps and the woman who drew them, who hated hunting and loved his sister's terrible singing, who once cried at a shepherd's funeral in a village whose name he has written in the margins eleven times so he won't lose it — that man tastes wrong to it. Too bitter. Too much like a person.

Tonight is such a night. He feels the attention turn, vast and patient, like weather.

*"You're awake,"* it says, in his voice, inside his skull, warm as a bath. *"Good. I want you to see tomorrow. There's a girl coming to court — a pilgrim, they say, with her sworn shield. The Devoted found them interesting. You'll want to watch. You always did enjoy the interesting ones."*

He says nothing. Saying nothing is a skill now; he has practiced it the way other men practice swordwork.

*"Still fighting,"* it observes, and there is something almost like admiration in the borrowed timbre. *"Two years, and you're down to a candle-end of yourself, and still fighting. Do you know what I was, before your ancestors put me in the stone? Neither do I. That's the joke, little king. They bound me so long ago that even I've forgotten. Sometimes I think: let the rite come. Let someone unbind me. And then I remember I don't know what's underneath, and neither do you, and men fear what they'd find under their own floors."*

It withdraws, satisfied, sated, careless. Careless is good. Careless means margins.

Aldric stays very still in the flooded garden until the tide recedes, and then he climbs back to the surface, to the candle, to the desk, and he finds his hand already moving, writing the last message of the hour, the one he writes on the bad nights when he can feel how few hours remain:

*To whoever comes: I felt them tonight. Two of them, coming toward me through the winter. One of them hears the land the way I used to hear Wren laugh — I don't know how I know this, but the stone knows, and the stone is afraid of her, and mark this well: THE STONE IS AFRAID OF HER.*

Underneath, in letters growing faint as the candle gutters:

*My name is Aldric Veyra. My name is Aldric Veyra. My name is—*

The tide rises. The candle drowns. Somewhere far above, in a body that walks and blesses and is loved, the thing smiles with his mouth at nothing, and does not notice that the king has hidden one more letter in the world — small, stubborn, alive —

— and that somewhere in the capital, in a pilgrim's disguise, a woman who once drew false passes through mountains is already learning to read the margins.
