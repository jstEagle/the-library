## 31. Aldric's Whisper

Edmund had never heard silence before.

He'd lived inside it for forty years, of course — the whole valley was silence, magic sliding off it like water off oiled cloth — but he had never *listened* to it until now, crouched in the under-city dark with Sera's head heavy against his shoulder and her breath gone shallow and wrong. The under-city hummed. Old spells bled from the palace above like water through a roof: wards murmuring in their sleep, enchantments pooling in the cracks of ancient masonry, the Ember's vast heartbeat thrumming down through fifty feet of stone so that the floor itself seemed to breathe.

All of it slid off him. All of it, and everyone pressed close against him for the same reason. In a city that screamed with Veyra's listening magic, Edmund Yarrow was a hole in the song, and his people huddled inside that hole like travelers sheltering under an arch in the rain.

"Her fever's holding," Odo said quietly, across the little ring of their meager fire. Rell sat beside him, their shoulders touching now without either of them pretending it was an accident of cramped space. "The willowbark's doing something."

"It's doing what willowbark does," Sera muttered without opening her eyes. "Which is make waiting bearable. Which is all any of us are doing right now."

"We could be doing it louder," Pip offered from the dark. He hadn't spoken much since Marlow's ashes. When he did speak, it came out flat and careful, like a man walking on river ice. "There's a whole rebellion upstairs apparently. Marlow would've loved it. He always said if we ever started a war, I should get to do the speeches."

"You'd be terrible at the speeches," Edmund said gently.

"I know." And there — the ghost of a smile, quick and painful as a caught breath. "Marlow would have written them for me."

The fire popped. Sera's breathing evened into sleep, her fingers still curled loosely around Edmund's wrist, and no one spoke for a while after that, because speaking meant deciding, and every decision they'd made in the vault had cost them something they couldn't afford. Rell dead on the stairs. The ritual collapsed. The Ember still beating its stolen light beneath the palace, and Veyra knowing now exactly where they were and exactly how close they had come.

Edmund stared into the flames and turned Veyra's words over for the thousandth time.

*Your gate was never a door, warden. It was a cage. I have simply built a larger one.*

He had faltered on that stair. He knew it; they all knew it; nobody had said it, which somehow made it worse. Fourteen words from a sorcerer-queen's mouth and thirty-nine years of certainty had come apart in his hands like wet paper. He had guarded the gate his whole life because the gate kept something *in*. That was the whole architecture of his soul. Take that away and what was left? A man who smelled of lamp oil and sheep, good for carrying things and being invisible.

"The gate holds nothing but the valley's own peace."

He said it out loud before he knew he was going to. The others looked up.

"What?" Odo asked.

"That's what Aldric told me. In the vault." Edmund's mouth felt strange saying it, as if the words were borrowed. "When I touched the Ember, I heard him. Screaming, mostly. But at the end — when everything went wrong — there was this... quiet. His voice in the quiet. Like a man whispering from the bottom of a well." He rubbed his face. "I thought it was the Ember lying to me. Another trap, like the desires it showed you all. So I didn't say anything."

"And now?" Rell asked. His voice was careful. Everything about Rell was careful these days, a man relearning how to stand after the ground proved false.

"Now I've been sitting here for three hours watching Sera sleep and thinking about how Veyra had no reason to lie to me on that stair. She thought she'd won. People who think they've won tell you true things, because true things hurt more." He laughed, short and bitter. "My father told me the gate held back the Wyrm under the mountain. His father told him. We were wardens fourteen generations. Do you know what none of us ever did? None of us ever *looked behind the door.* There was a rule against it. There was a rule against everything."

Pip leaned forward. "What does that mean, though — nothing but the peace? The valley's peaceful. Everyone says so. Nothing blights, nothing burns, the sheep are famously smug—"

"The sheep *are* famously smug," Edmund agreed, and something in his chest loosened slightly at Pip's huff of almost-laughter. "But think about what that means. A valley where magic can't follow. Where crops never fail and storms go around and the worst thing that happens in a year is somebody's fence. I used to think we were guarded. What if we weren't guarded? What if we were *kept?*" He heard his own voice pick up speed, gathering the thought the way you gather spilled grain. "Veyra told me my family were living locks. Sera found the lineage records — bred magicless, generation over generation, for one purpose. But what purpose? Locks need doors, and doors open both ways. Unless—" He stopped. Started again, quieter. "Unless the lock was never for our benefit. Unless the whole valley is a story the wardens were told so we'd stand at that gate forever and never ask why a prison needs such beautiful weather."

The fire cracked. Somewhere far above, muffled by fifty feet of stone, a bell rang the second hour past midnight.

"She bought your loyalty with a lie about the weather," Odo said slowly. "That's..." He shook his head. "That's exactly the sort of thing she'd do. I stood guard outside her council chambers for six years. She never once raised her voice. Why would she? She just arranged the world until raising your voice seemed insane." He looked at Edmund with something new in his face — not pity, Edmund realized. Recognition. "She did to you what she did to the king. Gave you a duty, wrapped it in a story, and let the story do the guarding."

Edmund nodded, and found that his eyes were hot, and was quietly furious about it. Thirty-nine years. His father's rough hands showing him the ward-signs. The cold mornings at the threshold, watching frost form patterns the wind couldn't explain. Every prayer he'd whispered at that door — *keep it sleeping, keep us safe* — offered up to a Wyrm that had never existed, guarding a peace that had been purchased like a pig at market.

"I failed on those stairs," he said. "Because she took the last thing I had."

"You didn't fail," Rell said. "You grieved. There's a difference, and I've been on both sides of it."

"Well." Edmund scrubbed his sleeve across his eyes and managed something that wanted to be a grin. "Then I'm done grieving, because I've just worked out that I spent my whole life as the unpaid groundskeeper of a lie, and I find I'm insulted."

That got a real laugh out of Pip — rusty, startled out of him, the first in eleven days — and Edmund held onto the sound like a coal carried in cupped hands.

* * *

He didn't sleep. He sat by the fire while the others finally sank into exhausted rest, Odo taking first watch with Rell pointedly refusing to leave his side, and he let himself remember the vault. Properly, this time. Not the terror of it — the *quiet.*

The Ember had screamed when he touched it. That part he'd told them. What he hadn't told them, what he'd buried under shame and doubt, was the shape of the scream: not mindless, not monstrous. *Articulate.* A scream with words inside it, a king's mind compressed into a flame and crying out in language, and when the ritual collapsed and Veyra's power had come roaring down the stair, that voice had pressed close against his null silence like a drowning man finding driftwood.

*Warden. Warden, listen — she lies — the gate —*

And then Rell died, and the ceiling came apart, and Edmund had run.

Now, in the dark, he closed his eyes and put his hand flat against the stone floor. Fifty feet up and a quarter mile east, the Ember beat in its vault like a captive heart. Its magic filled the palace, the city, the air — everywhere except the small quiet hole where Edmund sat.

If the king's echo wanted to reach him, Edmund thought, then Edmund's silence wasn't a wall.

It was a window.

He breathed out, slow, the way he'd learned to calm spooked sheep — the one pastoral skill he'd owned that had turned out useful on this mad road — and instead of pushing the hum of the city away, he listened *into* it. Past Odo's low-voiced watch-report to Rell. Past Sera's fevered dreams. Down into the stone, along the grain of the old spells, toward the enormous slow pulse that was two heartbeats braided together: a flame, and a man.

*I'm here,* he thought, not knowing if thinking was enough, willing it to be. *I'm listening. Say it again.*

For a long moment, nothing. The pulse continued, indifferent as tide.

Then —

Not sound. Nothing so kind as sound. It arrived the way a memory arrives, fully formed, from nowhere: pressure against the inside of his skull, faint and frayed and *enormously* patient, like a voice that had been rehearsing for decades and would wait another decade if it must.

***Warden.***

Edmund's eyes flew open. The fire hadn't moved. Nobody stirred. The pressure wrapped around him, and he made himself breathe, made himself stay, the way he'd once stood his ground in a flood-year when the river took the lower pasture and all he had was a fence post and his own stubbornness.

***I am here. I have perhaps a hundredth of myself left to speak with — the rest the Flame keeps. Do not waste me on questions I cannot answer. Listen.***

"I'm listening," Edmund whispered, and his breath frosted in air that had not been cold.

***Veyra told you the gate is a cage. She believes it. That is the cruelty of her — she arranges the world until her beliefs become everyone's facts. But I was king when the valley was sealed, warden. My grandfather sealed it. I read the founding writs before she burned them.*** The voice strained, thinned, gathered itself again. ***There is nothing behind your gate. No Wyrm. No horror. No nameless thing that ends the world. There is orchard country and a hot spring and a village called Merrow's Ford where they make terrible cider, and there has never been anything else.***

"Then why—" Edmund's whisper cracked. "Why seal it at all?"

***Because a valley without magic, walled and hidden and ruled by a family sworn to never leave, is worth more to a ruler than an army.*** The bitterness in that phantom voice could have curdled milk. ***Think, warden. Where does a queen send the people whose loyalty she doubts? Where do inconvenient lords' sons go to disappear respectably? Your valley takes them. Your wardens keep the records, tend the fences, ask no questions — because asking questions means leaving, and leaving means the peace ends, and the peace is the only holy thing you have ever been permitted to believe in. It is not a prison, warden. It is a *retirement.* Yours was the softest cell ever built, and the lock was on the inside, and it was shaped like duty.***

Edmund sat very still. The words went into him slowly, the way cold water goes into a boot.

His mother, humming over the garden. The festival of the Sealing, every autumn, the whole valley turning out to renew their thanks to the wardens. Travelers arriving, occasionally, dazed and grateful, "fleeing trouble," settling into empty cottages that always seemed to be waiting. He'd thought it charity. He'd thought it *luck.*

It had been policy. They had all been somebody's tidy solution.

"Why tell me?" he asked the dark. "Why not Sera? Why not anyone?"

***Because they would hear me and hope. Hope is loud. Hope can be tracked.*** A pause, and when the voice resumed there was — impossibly — warmth in it, tired and rueful. ***But you, warden. You are the quietest thing in my kingdom. You are the one place in all this burning city where I can whisper. And because — ***

The voice frayed. Edmund gripped the floor.

***— because you touched me, and I saw your heart, and it was the first honest thing I have held in nine years.*** The pressure steadied, gathered. ***Listen carefully, for I grow thin. The unbinding failed not because your friend's ritual was flawed. It failed because the Flame defended itself, and the Flame defends itself with truth bent sideways — the desire-visions, Veyra's revelation, your despair. All one weapon. But a weapon needs a hand, and the hand is hers, and hers is *mortal.* She channels the binding through her own will. Cut the channel — not the Flame, not the king, the *channel* — and the binding unravels like a cut hem.***

"How?" Edmund demanded of the dark. "How do we cut it?"

***Sera's ritual was right. It only needed to finish. It needed — *** The voice wavered like a candle in draft. ***It needed the one thing Veyra cannot offer and cannot counterfeit. It needed someone with nothing at stake in magic, standing inside the circle, holding the thread while the storm pulls. Your friend nearly had it. She stopped believing at the crucial moment because I could not tell her soon enough that the visions were lies. But you — you believed nothing, warden. You hoped nothing. You stood in my screaming and stayed yourself. That is the whole art. That is everything.***

Edmund's hands had gone numb on the stone. He thought of Beatrix, dying on his clean floor, pressing heat-marked leather into his palms. *Carry it, don't wield it.* He'd thought it riddles. It had been instructions.

"She'll be guarded," he said aloud. "After the vault, she'll—"

***She will be *certain,* *** the king's echo said, ***and certainty makes tyrants slow. Three days. The Binding Day ceremony. She will walk me before the crowds as her trophy, and the Flame's focus will move aboveground with her, into open air, away from the vault's old defenses. One door instead of seven. One crowd instead of labyrinth stone.*** The voice was almost gone now, worn to a thread. ***Warden. One more thing, and it is the smallest and the largest. Your valley — the peace is real, whatever its price. Whatever you choose afterward... that part was never a lie. Only the reason was.***

"Aldric—" Edmund scrambled upright, heart hammering. "The free cities are marching. Odo's proof, Pip's refugees, the whole coast — when they see you—"

***See me,*** the echo repeated, and there was laughter in it, terrible and bright, the laugh of a caged thing that has just heard the key turn. ***Yes. Let them see me. Let her parade her trophy on Binding Day. Kings make poor trophies, warden. We are heavy in ways queens forget to weigh.***

The pressure withdrew like a tide going out, and Edmund was alone in the dark with the ordinary hum of old spells and the small snoring of exiles, shaking from crown to heel.

* * *

"Wake up," Edmund said. "All of you. Wake up, I know where the king is going to be."

Odo came up off his bedroll with a knife already in his hand, which explained a great deal about how he'd survived six years in Veyra's palace. Rell was on his feet slower but no less awake. Pip sat bolt upright. Sera surfaced from her fever with a groan, and Edmund crossed to her first, kneeling, taking her burning hands in his.

"Sera. I have to ask you something, and I need you to trust me even though it sounds mad."

"Everything about you sounds mad," she croaked. "It's one of your best qualities. Ask."

"The king spoke to me. Just now. Through the null — my silence. He's been trying since the vault." Her eyes widened and she started to speak and he pressed on, fast, before doubt could get a foothold. "I know. I know what it sounds like. I stood on that stair and fell apart because of Veyra's lie, and I've hated myself for four days, and I'm not asking you to take my word. I'm telling you what he said and you're going to check it against your ritual notes, because he described exactly why the unbinding failed and exactly what it needed, and it matches your work point for point. Things I couldn't know. Things nobody could know unless they were *inside* it."

Sera stared at him. Then, muttering, she dragged her satchel into her lap and pulled out the ritual pages with shaking, feverish hands, and Edmund read them aloud beside Aldric's words, and watched her face change — watched disbelief give way to scrutiny, scrutiny to wonder, wonder to something fierce and wet-eyed that she hid by shoving her spectacles up into her hair.

"He said the visions were lies," she whispered. "At the crucial moment. I *felt* it was a trick, Edmund, I felt it in my bones, and I stopped anyway because I couldn't prove it, and if I'd known—" Her voice broke. She pressed the pages flat against her knees. "If I'd known, I'd have finished it. We'd have finished it *then.*"

"Then we finish it now." Edmund stood and turned to the room, to all of them — Odo with his knife, Rell with his broken ribs and steady eyes, Pip hollow-cheeked and burning, Sera grey with fever and already, visibly, beginning to calculate dosages and diagrams. Ordinary people. Broken-road people. His people. "Binding Day. Three days from now. Veyra's going to march Aldric aboveground as her trophy, and she thinks she's untouchable, and she's wrong, because the king has been awake this whole time, waiting for one quiet man to listen." He heard his own voice steadying, hardening, becoming something he recognized at last. "She stole my valley's story to keep me at that gate. Fine. Then she can have the truth back, with interest. On Binding Day I'm going to stand in front of her whole kingdom, and I'm going to carry."

Pip rose slowly to his feet. He picked up Marlow's folded banner from where it lay, and slung it over his shoulder, and when he spoke his voice didn't shake at all.

"Then we'd better go write some speeches," he said. "Marlow always said the good ones start with *wake up.*"

Above them, far away, the palace bells began to ring the third hour — and beneath their ringing, faint as a heartbeat, the Ember pulsed once, twice, like a man clearing his throat before he speaks to a kingdom.
