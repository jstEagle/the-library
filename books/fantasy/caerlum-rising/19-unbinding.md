## 19. Unbinding

The throne room of Caerlum had been built to make people small.

Dominic felt it now, standing beneath vaults of green-veined stone, beneath windows tall as siege towers that should have let in starlight and showed nothing but a sky scraped clean. The marble floor drank the sound of their footsteps. The banners of House Aldric hung motionless in air that did not stir, as if the room itself were holding its breath—or as if something in it had eaten the breath long ago.

And on the throne, the king.

He looked wrong. That was the first thing, the thing Dominic's body understood before their mind could shape it. Serah had described him: gaunt, silver-eyed, still wearing the face from the coin-stamps. But no description had prepared them for the *stillness* of him. Men fidgeted. Men breathed with weight and rhythm. The thing on the throne sat the way furniture sits.

Its eyes found Dominic across forty feet of marble, and it smiled.

"Ah," it said, and the voice was warm, reasonable, almost kind. "The smuggler. I wondered when you'd come."

"Kess," Dominic breathed.

Above, in the shadowed gallery where the rafters met the stained glass, a shriek of wings erupted—dozens of crows pouring down through the clerestory like a black river unspilling, screaming, wheeling, diving at the heads of the honor guard. The guards—twelve of them, armored in crown-black—broke formation, swords up, shouting. Feathers and fury everywhere. Somewhere in the storm of it, Kess's voice whooped, high and fierce and thirteen years old.

"Now, Dom!"

Serah was already moving. She crossed to the base of the dais where an old ward-anchor stood—a pillar of pale stone, cracked, its runes guttering like a candle in wind—and slammed both palms against it. Light flared under her hands, sickly gold, fighting her.

"The binding-stone's awake," she shouted over the chaos. "It knows what we're here for. I can hold the wards around his mind open for maybe a minute—Dom, maybe less. When I say go, you *go*."

Dominic went.

They ran the length of the throne room with crows screaming overhead and their own heart hammering against their ribs like a fist on a door. Every instinct they had honed across twenty years of Thornfell—the instinct to be small, to be nobody, to never be seen doing the one thing that got Tamsin's brother hanged and their own sister burned—every one of those instincts screamed at them to turn around.

They climbed the dais steps instead.

Up close, the thing wearing Aldric was worse. Its skin had the faint translucence of wax held near flame. And beneath it—Dominic didn't see this so much as feel it, the way you feel weather coming in an old scar—the hunger moved. Vast. Patient. Coiled through the king's body the way ivy coiled through a dead tree, holding its shape.

"You came all this way," the thing said, tilting Aldric's head. The silver eyes tracked Dominic with terrible interest. "For *him*. For what's left. There is hardly anything left, little speaker. A whisper in a jar. Would you like to hear it?"

"Don't." Dominic's voice came out rougher than they meant. "Don't you dare wear his grief like a mask."

"But it fits so well." The smile widened. "Here. Listen."

And it *opened*.

There was no other word for it. Something behind Aldric's eyes parted like a door swinging wide, and what poured out was not a scream but worse—a memory. Dominic saw it whole, the way beast-speech showed them things: a young king on a balcony under a full sky of stars, laughing; the same king kneeling before a cracked stone in a cellar because his kingdom was dying and the ward-keepers offered him a bargain; the same king, years later, pressed down and down beneath himself, screaming into the dark where no scream had sound—

*Aldric.* Not dead. Buried alive in his own bones.

"*Go*," Serah screamed from below. The light around her hands had turned white-hot; blood ran freely from her nose now, dripping off her chin onto the marble. "I can't hold it much longer—"

Dominic closed their eyes, reached past the fear, reached the way they had reached for Bramble on cold mountain nights, for wolves in winter, for every wild mind that had ever answered them back—and touched the buried king.

*Aldric.*

The name landed like a stone dropped down a well. And then, faint and broken, from somewhere impossibly far beneath:

*...who...?*

*My name is Dominic. I've come to take you home.*

A pause. Then something that might have been laughter, or weeping: *No one comes. No one has come for nine years.*

*I'm coming now. Give me your hand.*

And the buried mind reached up toward them—hopeful, starving, trusting—and Dominic took hold of it, and began, gently, to pull.

That was when the hunger noticed them.

It happened without transition. One moment Dominic was hauling a drowning man toward light; the next, the water itself had turned and wrapped around their wrist. The hunger's attention swung onto them like a searchlight, vast and ancient and *delighted*, and Dominic heard it speak directly into their mind for the first time, and its true voice was nothing like the warm reasonable thing on the throne.

Its true voice was a mouth opening over a world.

***Oh,*** it said. ***Oh, you beautiful, hidden thing.***

Dominic tried to let go. They couldn't. The connection they'd opened ran both ways, and the hunger was already flowing through it, already tasting—Dominic felt it taste them, felt it move through the rooms of their self the way a thief moves through a dark house, touching everything. Their childhood. Bramble's warm breath. The mountain paths. The night their sister had gripped their hand and whispered *never tell them, never tell anyone* before they took her away.

***You speak,*** the hunger marveled. ***All these soft minds, all this meat I must drag and chain and bend—and you simply* ask*, and the world answers. Do you know how long I have wanted a voice? Stars are light. Light is only seeing. But a voice—*** its grip tightened, *** ***a voice can* call *things. A voice can make the land itself come when it's summoned. You will do nicely.***

"It's not taking my gift too!" Serah's voice, raw and cracking. Dominic couldn't see her; their eyes wouldn't open; their whole self was clenched around the connection like a hand around a rope in a gale. "Dom! Whatever it's doing, *fight it*—"

Fight it. Easy to say. The hunger wasn't attacking the way a soldier attacks. It was *unmaking*—reaching into the place where Dominic's gift lived, that quiet warm room inside them that had been theirs alone since childhood, and it was prying up the floorboards.

Dominic felt the first thing go.

It was small. A memory of sound—the particular low nicker Bramble made when he smelled rain coming. Just gone, like a page torn out. And in the space where it had been, the hunger left something of its own: a coldness, a hunger-shaped hollow, a little piece of itself installed in the vacancy like a garrison in a conquered town.

*No,* Dominic thought, with the last clarity they had. *No, that's mine. That's MINE—*

They pulled harder on Aldric. If they could just free him—if they could just finish—except finishing required time, and time was exactly what the hunger was eating out of them, second by second, memory by memory, gift by precious gift. The sound of their mother calling them in from the hills. Gone. The whistle their father taught them for calling sheep. Gone. Each loss left a cold hollow, and each hollow filled with more of the hunger, and Dominic understood with a clarity beyond terror that it was not merely devouring their gift.

It was *becoming* them. Piece by piece. Learning to be a person who spoke, wearing Dominic the way it wore Aldric.

Somewhere very far away, a crow screamed. Somewhere, Kess was crying and cursing and throwing stones. Somewhere, Serah was bleeding out her strength into a dying stone, holding the door open with her bare hands because someone she loved was on the other side of it.

*Serah.*

The thought cut through the cold like a coal through snow. Not duty. Not rebellion. Just her—her crooked grin, her terrible jokes, the way she'd said *your secret's safe with me* that night on the mountain road, and meant it, and had kept meaning it every day since.

The hunger had taken sounds. Names. Pieces of the gift. But it hadn't taken this—it couldn't take this—because this wasn't a memory stored somewhere it could pry up. This was what remained when everything else was torn away. This was load-bearing.

Dominic planted themselves in that love the way you plant your boots in a river current, and stopped being pulled.

***Interesting,*** the hunger said, and for the first time there was something in its voice that was not delight. ***What are you anchored to? Show me.***

*Like hell,* Dominic thought.

And then—through the roaring dark, through the tearing cold—Dominic felt the buried king move.

Aldric had watched all of this from the bottom of his well. He had felt a stranger's hand close around his after nine years of darkness, and then he had felt that stranger begin to be devoured on his behalf, and something in the ruined remnant of the King of Caerlum rose up with the last of what he was—not strength, he had none; not magic, the hunger had eaten that long ago—but *will*. Stubborn, ordinary, human refusal.

*Let them go,* Aldric said, into the shared dark, and his voice was thin and cracked and absolutely clear. *Take me instead. Take ALL of me. But let them GO.*

***Generous,*** the hunger purred. ***Futile. I have room for both.***

But the moment it divided its attention—the instant its vast focus split between the drowning smuggler and the defiant ghost—Serah felt the shift in the stone beneath her hands.

"There!" she screamed. "Dom, NOW—he's splitting his weight—I can't hold much—PULL HIM OUT—"

Dominic pulled.

With everything. With the love and the fury and the twenty years of hiding and the sister whose last words had been *never tell* and the family downstairs bleeding for them and the stars themselves hanging gutted in a stolen sky. They reached down into the well and seized the buried king with both hands, metaphorical and otherwise, and *hauled*.

Aldric came up fighting. He came up the way a drowning man comes up—clawing, desperate, sobbing with relief—and the hunger shrieked, a sound that burst out of the king's throat and shattered two of the great windows, glass raining like ice across the marble—

And then it turned on Dominic fully, abandoning pretense, abandoning the king, abandoning everything, because it finally understood what Dominic was offering it without knowing:

Not just a voice.

An anchor. A living, breathing body, right here, unguarded, wide open.

***Yes,*** it hissed, and began to pour itself out of Aldric like smoke reversing, like a flood finding a new channel. ***Yes. I'll wear YOU instead.***

"No—" Serah was stumbling forward, hands leaving the stone, wards collapsing around them like a house falling down. "No no no, Dom, it's leaving him, it's coming for YOU—"

*Good,* Dominic thought, and had one half-second to be horrified at their own thought, and then no time at all for anything, because the hunger hit them like an ocean hitting a cup.

The last thing Dominic saw, from the inside, was the king's body sagging on the throne like a puppet with cut strings—and the last thing they heard was Serah screaming their name, and the last thing they felt was their own voice rising up inside them, wild and bright and terrified, and the hunger closing around it like a fist around a songbird.

Ready or not, it seemed to say.

Come and be eaten.

* * *

The world came back in pieces.

Cold marble against their cheek. Glass scattered like frost. Someone's hands on their shoulders, shaking them—Serah's voice, hoarse, saying their name over and over like a prayer with the words worn off.

Dominic opened their eyes.

The throne was empty.

The king lay crumpled at the foot of the dais, breathing—breathing—his chest rising in shallow, human rhythms, his face slack and *empty*, the silver gone from his eyes, replaced by a blue so ordinary it hurt to look at. Alive. Free. But empty as a swept hearth, and somewhere in the walls, in the cracks, in the deep places where old stone remembers—

The hunger was still out there. Loose. Homeless. Hungry.

And it knew the way into Dominic's mind now. It had walked every corridor. It had memorized every door.

"Dominic." Serah's face swam above them, streaked with tears and blood, her hands cupping their jaw. "Talk to me. Are you—are you still *you*? Say something. Please."

Dominic opened their mouth to answer.

Nothing came out.

They tried again, reaching for the words, for the voice that had whispered to mules and argued with wolves and sung to crows—and found, where their voice had always lived, a hollow. A cold, hungry, *occupied* hollow.

Their gift—their voice, the one thing that had ever truly been theirs—was gone. Swallowed whole.

And somewhere in the dark beneath the palace, something that now knew exactly how Dominic Okafor tasted began, softly, to laugh.
