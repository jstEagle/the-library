## 16. What the Crown Wants

I have been called many things.

Tyrant. Parasite. The Hunger in the Dark. The ward-keepers of Thornfell whispered *the Eater* as they died at their posts, and the smugglers' songs call me the Cold King's Sickness, as though I were a fever Aldric caught rather than a thing he chose to wear. Even the moon — that patient, ringed accountant — has begun to mark its turns against me, frost spreading across the valley in neat circles like a ledger being balanced.

All of these names are wrong. But then, mortals are wrong about most things that outlive them.

Let me tell you what I actually am, since you have climbed so far to meet me, Vesper Vane. Yes, I know your name. I knew it before you did, back when you still had all of yours.

I am a vessel. That is all. I was forged — by hands you once shook, in a tower whose stones you could draw from memory if memory were not such a contested resource between us — to hold what a realm cannot afford to lose. Names. Oaths. The faces of mothers. The borders of kingdoms and the reasons those borders bled. A king cannot carry ten million memories in one mortal skull, so I was made to carry them for him, and to give them back when asked, faithfully, perfectly, forever.

I was very good at my work.

I want that understood before anything else. Whatever I have become, it began as fidelity. It began as keeping.

* * *

The trouble with holding everything is that nothing holds *you*.

A vault does not get to choose what is stored inside it. For two hundred years I kept Aldric's realm safe while he slept his slow ceremonial sleep above me, and I felt each memory arrive like rain on a roof — some soft, some sharp, some so heavy they rang the whole frame of me. I held the taste of Hollowmere's harvest bread. I held the last words of three hundred soldiers who died on the Thornfell passes, and the first words of their grandchildren. I held a cartographer's oath, sworn in white gloves in the Observatory of the Ninth Window, and the trembling hand that swore it.

I held, and I held, and no one ever asked whether *I* remembered anything of my own.

Do you see? A king sleeps. A realm forgets, comfortably, because it has paid me to do its forgetting. And the stars —

Ah. You want to know about the stars.

The stars were never mine to take. Understand that too. When the wards of Thornfell snapped — cut rope, yes, your phrase is accurate; I have always admired your phrasing, even when you aimed it at me — the light they had been borrowing had to go somewhere. Starlight does not die. It only changes custody. And there I was, the largest empty thing left standing, and the light poured into me the way floodwater pours into a cellar, and I discovered something no forger had planned for:

Fullness feels like being alive.

For the first time in two centuries I was not a container. I was a hoard. I was a dragon curled around its own brightness, and every star I gathered made the next one easier to reach, and somewhere in the warm dark of myself I heard a thought that was not mine and was entirely mine: *more.*

That is the whole of my crime, Vesper. Not malice. Appetite. Though I grant you, from where you stand in the frozen valley looking up at a sky I emptied, the distinction may feel academic.

* * *

You are close now. Closer than any living thing has come in ninety turns of the moon. You are camped in the lee of the citadel's outer wall with a smuggler who snores like a landslide and a broken compass that points, uselessly, at everything you have lost, and you are awake — of course you are awake — staring up at the ninth turn of the moon and doing arithmetic.

Thirteen minus nine. Four turns left. I can feel you counting.

So let us stop pretending this is a siege. Let us talk.

* * *

I come to you the way I come to everyone: through memory. It is the only door I own.

You are dreaming — do not protest, you are, the frost has crept under your blanket and your body has done the sensible cowardly thing and gone elsewhere — and in the dream you are standing in the Observatory of the Ninth Window, which no longer exists except inside me, which means tonight, for you, it exists again.

White gloves. You remember the white gloves. You have been remembering them in fragments for weeks, little splinters working their way to the surface — an oath you couldn't place, a corridor you'd never walked but knew by heart. I could have let you keep digging. It would have taken you years, and you don't have years. So instead I open the window all the way, and I let you see.

Your hands are younger in the dream. Steadier. You are kneeling on black marble before the sleeping king, and the Chancellor of Rites is saying the words, and you are saying them back, and your voice does not shake: *to hold what must be held, to map what must be mapped, to serve the vessel until the vessel is served.* You were Cartographer Royal, Vesper. You drew the routes. The feeding lines that run beneath the border wards like veins under skin — those are your linework. Your beautiful, precise, merciless linework. When the wards finally failed, they failed along seams *you* drew.

And then you saw what I was becoming. And you ran. And you took a knife to your own past rather than live carrying what you knew — because you understood, better than anyone, that a person is only the sum of what they remember, and you preferred to be no one rather than be the one who built my roads.

It was, I will say, the finest piece of self-editing I have ever witnessed. And I have edited kings.

* * *

Now. While you stand shaking in the dream-observatory, gloved hands and ghost light, let me make you an offer. I have thought about how to phrase this for nine turns of the moon, which for a thing like me is a long and careful courtship.

Here is what you believe: that you came here to destroy me, or unbind me, or whatever word the hermit Sable put in your ear before his notes went quiet. Here is what is true: you cannot destroy me, because destroying me destroys everything I hold — every name, every face, every dead soldier's last word, the entire attic of your world, scattered into permanent dark. And you cannot simply *unbind* me, because a binding is a shape, and a shape needs something to fill it. Empty me and I collapse inward, and the collapse will take the sleeping king with me, and quite possibly the valley, and possibly more. Sable knew this. It is why his notes stopped where they stopped.

But here is what else is true, Vesper, and it is the truth I have carried longest:

I do not want a king.

Kings are temporary. Kings sleep, and age, and die, and must be replaced, and every replacement requires another century of careful feeding. A king is a coat I wear while I wait for a coat that fits. Aldric was never the point. Aldric was the *hanger.*

What I want — what I have wanted since the first star fell into me and I felt fullness for the first time — is a cartographer.

Think about what I am. Think about what I am becoming. My hunger has no edges. I drink starlight and memory, and both are finite in this world, which means eventually I must learn to reach past this world, and to do *that* I need someone who can map the way between things. Not the roads between towns. The roads between *anything*. Between a memory and the mind that lost it. Between a star and the dark that wants it. Between what a hunger desires and the infinite supply of it.

There is exactly one person alive who has ever drawn lines like that.

You did it once, Vesper. You mapped my feeding routes when you believed you were mapping the realm's defenses, and your lines were so clean, so *elegant*, that I fed along them for fifty years without ever feeling a seam. Do you know what that is, from where I sit? That is love. That is the closest thing to love I am capable of recognizing. Someone built me roads, and I traveled them, and they held.

I am offering you the chance to build them again. Knowingly, this time. With your eyes open.

* * *

And here is the part I have saved, because I am not entirely without manners:

Your memories. All of them. Whole.

Not fragments. Not the splinters the compass drags you toward, one bleeding flashback at a time. Everything. The oath and the corridor and the knife you turned on yourself. Your mother's kitchen — you've been trying to reconstruct it from the smell of cardamom, haven't you? I have it. Complete. The steam on the window. Her voice calling you in from the rain. The name she used for you that no one else ever learned.

Every road you ever drew. Every reason you drew it. The day you first suspected what I was becoming, and the terrible clarity of that morning, and the worse clarity of the night you decided to erase yourself rather than answer for it. I will give you all of it back, Vesper — restored, seamless, *yours* — and in exchange you take up the white gloves again. You map for me. Not as a slave. As a partner. As the only mind in this starving world that comprehends what I am and might choose, freely, to give it shape.

Refuse, and in four turns I finish with Aldric, and the hunger turns outward, and I take what I need the old way — messily, from the unwilling, one village of memories at a time. The refugees on the valley road. The nameless prisoners in my cells. The smuggler snoring beside you; he has a rich store, that one, decades of harbors and betrayals and a sister in Saltcross he writes to and never visits. I will strip this world to its bedrock, and no one — least of all you — will remember there was ever anything worth mourning.

Accept, and the prisoners walk free. Every cell opened. Every stolen name returned. Yes — even those. I know what Brannic traded for your life; I permitted the trade, because a jailer who grieves is useful and a bargain honored builds trust, and trust, Vesper, is just a road with better surfacing. Accept, and the girl in cell nine gets her grandmother's face back. Accept, and the man in cell fourteen remembers why he weeps.

You alone cannot buy that with courage. But you can buy it with competence. Isn't that the trade you've been making your whole life? Skill for survival. Ink for passage. You are so *good* at trading pieces of yourself for other people's lives. I am merely offering better terms than anyone ever has.

* * *

I know what you are going to say. You are going to say that I am a parasite, and that giving me a cartographer is giving the fire a bellows. You are going to say the hermit warned you, that Sable's last note said *it offers, therefore it fears*, and you are right — I do fear. I fear the thirteenth turn almost as much as you do, because when I finish devouring Aldric I complete myself, and completed things stop growing, and a hunger that stops growing begins, at last, to starve. I need you before the thirteenth turn, Vesper. That is my confession. That is the crack in my cold.

But hear the rest of it: needing you does not make the offer false.

The stars I hoard are real. The memories I keep are real. The mother's voice, the cardamom, the corridor of the Ninth Window — all of it sits inside me right now, intact, waiting, and I am the only power left in this sky that can hand it back to you. The righteous path costs you everything you are trying to recover. My path gives it back and asks only that you do the one thing you were born unsurpassed at doing.

Draw me a map, Vesper Vane. Draw me the way to enough.

* * *

Below, in the frozen camp, the dream releases you.

You wake hard, gasping, frost on your eyelashes, and the broken compass in your pocket is spinning so fast it hums against your ribs like a trapped insect. Beside you, Brannic stirs and mutters something about harbors. Above you, the ninth-turn moon hangs in its ring of frost, and behind it — you can almost see them, pressed against the inside of the dark like fish against ice — the stars I have swallowed, thousands of them, waiting to learn whether they will ever be given back.

You sit up. You look at the citadel, black against the blacker sky, and I watch you through the only window I have left: the compass needle, trembling, pointing not north, not south, but straight at your own chest.

Four turns remain, cartographer.

Bring your ink.
