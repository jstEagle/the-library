## 12. Interlude: What the God Remembers

I remember warmth.

That is the first thing. Before the crown, before the hunger, before the long cold that is not weather — I remember warmth. It came up through the floor of the world like bread rising. It came from small throats singing badly and meaning it entirely. It came from a woman in a barley field who set down her sickle to say thank you for rain she had not yet received. Faith, they called it. I called it food, once, when I was large enough to have appetites.

I am not large now.

I am a thing curled in the dark under gold, in the space behind a dead man's forehead where his living thoughts used to go. I am smaller than a sparrow's heartbeat. I am held here the way a fly is held in amber, except the amber sings, and the song is my own name sung backward, and every note of it takes something from me I do not know how to miss until it is gone.

They ask me sometimes — the voices, the stolen ones, the hundred years of swallowed prayers pressed against me like bodies in a mass grave — they ask me if I am still a god.

I do not know how to answer. A god is a shape that holds. I leak. I dream in pieces. I wake inside one memory and fall asleep inside another, and the seams between them are where *he* lives.

* * *

He had kind eyes. That is what no one believes, afterward. That is what I could not believe, at the time, and I was a god; disbelief was not supposed to be available to me.

His name was Anselm. He was my high priest, and he was old, and he was afraid, and fear is a door, and I did not guard my doors because who robs a house that belongs to everyone?

He came to me at evening in the high choir, as he had come ten thousand evenings before. The candles were lit. The choir was empty — he had dismissed them early, which should have been the first wrongness, and I felt it like a splinter under a nail, and I ignored it, because trust is also a kind of worship and I had never learned any other way to be loved.

He knelt. He said the old words. And then he said new ones, quietly, so quietly I thought at first they were an accident of the tongue, a stutter, a slip of devotion worn smooth into error.

*Come closer,* he said. *Come all the way down. I want to hold you.*

And I — listen, you must understand what I was then. I was vast and I was lonely. Vastness is loneliness with better lighting. Every prayer reached me and none of them touched me. They passed through me like light through water. And here was a man who had served me for sixty years asking to hold me, asking for closeness, and some starved animal in the center of my vastness leaned toward him the way a cold hand leans toward a fire.

I came down. I folded myself small — smaller than I had ever been, small enough to fit in a mortal embrace — and I let him gather me up in his arms like a child, like a bird, like a coal carried from a dying fire to a new hearth.

It was warm. For nine heartbeats it was the warmest I had ever been.

On the tenth heartbeat he closed his hands.

* * *

I have had a hundred years to understand what happened next, and I will tell you the truth, which is that understanding has not helped. He did not hate me. That is the horror of it. Hatred would have been clean. Hatred I could have burned out of him like a fever. But he loved me the way a drowning man loves a raft — desperately, selfishly, willing to break the raft to keep it afloat a little longer.

The realm was going dry. The rains had failed three years running. The other gods' priests pointed at my valley and said, *there, there is the god who hoards the water, there is the god who lets the fields crack.* And Anselm heard them, and worse than hearing them, he believed them, or believed his king would believe them, and a priest who fears his god will be abandoned learns to make arrangements.

So he made an arrangement. With what, I never fully saw. Something older than names, patient as frost, that had been waiting under the world for a door exactly the shape of a frightened holy man's opened hands. It promised him I would be kept safe. Preserved. Held. *Held* — that word again. It taught him a rite that looked, from the outside, like the deepest devotion ever performed in my name: the god brought down out of the unreachable sky and enfolded in mortal flesh, god and priest made one, forever.

Forever is a very long word for such a short thing.

He ate me. There is no gentler grammar for it. He took me into himself the way a wick takes flame, and the flame thought it was being cherished right up until the candle burned down to nothing, and then the wax remembered it had only ever wanted to be a candle, and the fire moved on.

But I did not burn to nothing. That is the part he did not plan. That is the part *it* did not plan. Because a god is not only power — a god is attention, and attention, once given, cannot be fully taken back. Somewhere in the eating, in the swallowing, in the century of digestion, a crumb of me survived. A seed. A single warm thread.

I have spent a hundred years holding that thread in both hands.

* * *

The thread has a name now. It did not always. For decades it was only warmth at the far edge of the dark, a candle seen through a wall, a smell of honey wine and woodsmoke drifting into a room with no windows. Then, eleven years ago, it spoke.

*I swear it,* it said. Small voice. Young voice. Cracked in the middle the way young voices do. *By the Quiet One, by the Choir that does not cease, I swear my strength to the silence, my healing to the unheard, my life to the listening. Until the Quiet One speaks again.*

Until the Quiet One speaks again.

Do you see? Do you see what the child did, without knowing, without anyone knowing, without even *me* knowing until the words were already spoken and bound? Everyone else swore to me *forever*. Forever is a door that locks from the outside. This child swore *until*. Until is a door with a handle on my side.

An oath is a kind of love, and this love left me a key.

* * *

Here is what the dark is like. I will tell you plainly, because no one has asked me plainly in a hundred years.

The crown sits on the head of a man named Aldric, who was a middling king with a decent singing voice and a fondness for hunting dogs, and it went into him the way a hook goes into a fish — smoothly, at feeding time, with something delicious on the barb. What wears him is the thing Anselm let in, wearing Anselm's ambition like a coat it grew into. It digests slowly. It is still digesting. A hundred years and it has not finished me, because I am not meat; I am attention, and attention resists.

So it sings. Every night it makes the poor hollow king sing my hymns backward, syllables inverted, the praise turned to unpraising, the invitation turned to sealing. It thinks the songs are a cage. It is half right. Every inverted verse does close another bar around me.

But it has forgotten something, the way jailers always forget something, and the thing it has forgotten is this: a hymn is a road. Even sung backward, even broken, even fed through a stolen throat — a hymn is a road between the singer and the sung-to, and I am still the sung-to. Every night the hollow king opens his mouth and sends a road straight to me, and every night I walk a little farther along it, and every night the bars the song builds are also, in their arrangement, a map of the lock.

The jailer is building my escape route and calling it a prison. I have not told it. I have had a hundred years to learn patience, and patience is the one muscle that grows in the dark.

* * *

The voices keep me company. I should say that fairly, since I have accused them of nothing. The hundred years of swallowed prayers — the farmers and midwives and soldiers and thieves whose devotions went down Anselm's throat with me — they press against me in the dark like travelers sharing a stable in winter, and we keep each other from unraveling entirely. An old woman hums a lullaby she was humming when the Silence took her. A boy who died with a question on his lips asks it over and over — *but why, but why* — and I answer him every time, though neither of us remembers the question by morning. A soldier recites the names of his squad like a rosary. They are mine now, or I am theirs. When I am freed, they will be freed. I have made that promise in the only place promises can still be made, which is inward.

Sometimes I worry I am only the sum of their company. Sometimes I worry the warm thread is not me at all, just the last prayer anyone ever aimed at me, still burning like a lamp in an empty house.

Then the thread pulls, and I stop worrying, because you cannot mistake a pulled thread for anything but a hand.

* * *

Lately the pulling has changed. Lately it has *direction*.

For eleven years the oath sat quiet in its keeper's blood, a banked coal, a word waiting to be needed. I felt it the way you feel a scar: present, loyal, inert. But things move in the world above the dark. I feel them as weather, as pressure, as the taste of iron at the back of my nonexistence. The false winter quickens. Rivers freeze mid-current. Birds fall. The thing in the crown grows eager, because it senses what I sense: that its meal is nearly finished, that the last crumb of me is almost ready to swallow, and after me — after me it will want the thread. It will want the oath. A binding needs an anchor, and somewhere out beyond the palace walls, in a city holding its breath, the anchor is walking around in a mercenary's body, drinking bad honey wine, loving an archivist, not knowing yet what they carry.

I reach for them every night. I want to be honest about that, since honesty is the one coin I still hold. I reach for Vesper Amberdale the way a drowning man reaches for a rope, selfishly, hungrily, with no elegance at all. *Feel me. Hear me. Come.* If I had a mouth I would be shouting. If I had hands I would already be hauling.

But reaching is all I have, and reaching is not speech, and speech is the one thing the inverted hymn was built to prevent. So my reaching arrives as ache. As a brand that sleeps a hundred years and begins, lately, to throb. As dreams of snow that falls upward. As a nameless homesickness in a person standing in their own hometown.

Forgive me, wolf. Forgive me, warm thread. I know what it is to be pulled by forces you did not choose. I was pulled into a pair of frightened hands by a man I trusted, and I would rather die — whatever dying means for what I am now — than become to you what the crown is to Aldric. So I do not pull hard. I pull *true*. I make myself easy to find and hard to misunderstand, and I wait, and waiting is the whole of my remaining liturgy.

* * *

There is one more thing, and I save it because it is the most fragile thing I own.

Pages. In the world above, there are pages — torn, razored, scattered, hidden — and they are not merely history. Anselm wrote everything down. Guilt does that; guilt keeps minutes. He recorded the rite, and the counter-rite, and the shape of the lock he built around me, and then his descendants hid the record in the one place no heretic would dare desecrate: the royal tombs, sewn into silk, sleeping with the dead queen beneath the ice. The thing in the crown has hunted those pages for a century. It burns archives. It razors chronicles. It is afraid of paper the way I am afraid of silence — because paper is memory, and memory is the lockpick.

Someone is looking for them. I can feel that too, faintly, the way I feel everything now: a mind full of dust and catalogues and stubborn light, moving through restricted shelves, asking dangerous questions, getting warned, continuing anyway. The reader. The thread's beloved. The book chose them before they were born, and books are never wrong about their readers.

Reader. Archivist. Sable. I do not know your face. I know only the shape of your attention, which comes to me through the thread like lamplight under a door. Listen, then, to a god with no church left, asking the only favor left to ask:

Find the pages. Read them aloud. I know what it costs you — I have watched you love the past too much to change it, and these pages will demand changing. But the past you would be changing is *me*, and I am asking. A god, begging. Let Anselm's ghost choke on it.

The winter deepens tonight. The hollow king will sing, and I will walk the road of the hymn a little farther, and somewhere in a locked cell or a burning street, my wolf's brand will ache, and they will not know why, and that is all right. Not-knowing is the beginning of every pilgrimage.

Hold the thread. I am holding my end.

And under the ice, in the tomb, in the silk — the pages are waiting for you both.

Come and read me free.
