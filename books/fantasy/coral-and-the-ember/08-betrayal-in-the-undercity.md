## 8. Betrayal in the Undercity

The contact's name was Wick, and Casper had known him for less than a day, and that should have been warning enough.

He met them in a cellar beneath a tannery that hadn't tanned anything in years, where the air hung thick with old salt and the memory of hides. Wick was small and quick, with ink-stained fingers and a smile that arrived a half-second before his eyes agreed to it. He took Sera's coin, counted it twice, and told them what they needed to know.

"The ritual chamber is under the Grand Basilica. Old foundations—there's a way in through the flood tunnels, the ones the masons sealed two hundred years ago. Nine days becomes eight tomorrow." He tapped the table with one stained finger. "The eclipse is at dusk on solstice night. The binding renews at moonset."

"And the king?" Casper asked. "Sera spoke to him. He gave her the name."

Wick's eyes flicked up, and for just a moment the smile fell away entirely. "Then you know more than most. Careful who hears you say that in this city. Ash-marked doors get new tenants every week, and the old tenants—" he drew a line across his throat, almost playful, almost not. "I can draw you the tunnel route. After that we're done. I was never here."

"You were never here," Sera repeated flatly.

"I'm a reasonable man in unreasonable times," Wick said. "Reasonable men survive."

* * *

Casper knew, walking out of the tannery into the grey drizzle of the undercity, that something was wrong. Not with Wick—with the streets. The beggars had moved. The same ragged figures they'd passed an hour ago were now positioned along their route back toward the safehouse, spaced like beads on a string. And the ravens on the rooflines weren't scavenging. They were watching.

*They sold you,* said a raven, hopping along a gutter above Casper's head. *Amnesty. Papers and pardon. He traded your names this morning.*

Casper stopped in the middle of the lane. Rain dripped off a sagging awning. Somewhere behind them, a door closed too carefully.

"Sera." They kept their voice low. "We're marked."

Sera didn't ask how she knew. She'd spent fifteen years doing the marking; she recognized the shape of it from the inside. Her hand went to her sword hilt, then—deliberately—away. "Safehouse is burned. If they're watching the route back, they already searched it. We run for the flood tunnels now, tonight, or not at all."

"The tunnels are the trap."

"Every road is a trap now." Sera's jaw was set. "So we pick the trap we chose ourselves."

They ran.

* * *

The mouth of the flood tunnels yawned beneath the old customs house, a stone throat slick with green rot. Casper went first because Casper could see—not with eyes, but with the low animal murmur of the dark: the rats mapping the waterlines, the eels tasting the current, the blind fish that swam the flooded sections and felt their footsteps as thunder. The gift that had kept them alive in silence all these years now lit the way forward like a lantern made of whispers.

*Deep water ahead,* the rats reported. *Waist-high, then chest-high, then a dry gallery beyond.*

Behind them, boots. Many boots. Lantern-light bouncing off wet stone.

"They've entered the tunnel," Sera said. No panic in it. Just arithmetic. "A dozen, maybe more. Crossbows."

"Then we go deep and we go fast."

The water rose as they waded in, cold climbing their bodies inch by inch. The tunnel bent twice, narrowed, opened into a vaulted gallery where the ceiling vanished into blackness and the water stood chest-deep and utterly still. Their lantern made a small trembling island of light. Ahead, the dry gallery waited—a raised stone walkway, and beyond it, according to Wick's map, the sealed mason's door that led to the basilica foundations.

Casper heard the hunters before the lanterns reached them. Not the boots—the breathing. Dozen men holding their breath in the dark, spreading out along both walls.

"Down," Casper breathed—and the crossbows loosed.

Bolts skipped off the water, chipped stone, one tore through Casper's sleeve and grazed the arm beneath. Sera hauled them behind a pillar as a second volley splintered against it. The gallery erupted into echoes, every shot multiplied into a hundred, the darkness full of screaming stone.

"Vaela!" Casper shouted it without deciding to. The wolf-mother had shadowed them from the ridgelines for three hundred miles; she was somewhere in the city's bones by now, following the waterways the way wolves follow rivers. "VAELA!"

The answer came as sound first—a howl rolling down through the city above, down through grates and drains, arriving in the flooded gallery like the voice of something vast waking up. Then came the screaming. Not theirs.

The hunters' rear guard broke. In the lantern-light Casper saw her: gray as storm-cloud, enormous, ancient, moving through the water and the men like winter itself had come down the tunnel. She took the first hunter at the waist. The second she caught by the shoulder and threw into the dark. The formation dissolved into chaos, men firing bolts at shadows and each other, and through it all Vaela came on, silver-eyed, terrible, *herself*.

*"Run,"* she said, and her mind-voice cracked across the water like ice breaking. *"The dry gallery. GO, little speaker. I am behind you."*

They ran. Chest-deep, then thigh-deep, then hauling themselves up onto the raised stone of the dry gallery, soaked and gasping. Behind them the fighting moved away into the side tunnels, hunters fleeing or dying, Casper couldn't tell which, didn't dare look back—

Except Sera did look back. Sera always looked back.

"Crossbowmen on the gallery rim," she said quietly. "Four of them. They have the angle on the door." She turned to Casper, and rainwater and tunnel-water alike streamed off her face, and her expression was the calmest Casper had ever seen it. "You can't fight four crossbows and open a two-hundred-year-old seal at the same time. So I hold here. You work the door."

"No." The word came out of Casper raw. "No, together, we—"

"Casper." Sera smiled, and it was the first real smile Casper had ever seen her wear. "I burned my commission for this. I knew the price going in. Hold the route. That's the job. It was always going to be someone's job."

She stepped out onto the gallery with her sword drawn and her body angled to cover the waterline, and the bolts began to fly, and she began to kill.

Casper turned to the seal.

It was old work, older than the kingdom—mason's marks worn to ghosts, a door of fitted stone with no hinge and no handle, meant to be opened only by those who knew its secret. Casper pressed both palms flat against it and listened. Beneath the stone, in the deep places where even rats don't go, something answered. Not an animal. A pressure. A hunger so old and vast that Casper's knees buckled against it—the ember, feeling them through six feet of mountain rock, recognizing a gifted soul the way a fire recognizes kindling.

*"There you are,"* it seemed to say. *"Come closer."*

*Casper wrenched their mind free and did the only thing they knew how to do: they asked the stone itself. The worms in the mortar, the beetles in the cracks, the slow patient life that eats empires from within.* Open. Please. *And the stone, which had been deaf for two hundred years, listened.*

The seal groaned. Cracked. Began to move—

A bolt took Sera through the shoulder. Casper heard her grunt, heard her keep fighting, heard the ring of steel and the wet heavy sounds that followed. Then a second bolt. Then her voice, tight with pain but still steady: "Door, Casper. Door!"

The stone swung inward on a breath of stale, ember-warm air. Beyond lay a corridor lit by veins of dull red light running through the walls like magma through rock—the basilica's buried heart, the ritual chamber's threshold, close enough now that Casper could feel it humming in their teeth.

"SEra!" They turned.

Sera came backward along the gallery, bleeding from the shoulder, her left arm hanging wrong, still keeping herself between the last crossbowman and the open door. The man reloaded with shaking hands. Sera closed the distance before he finished and put him down with the pommel of her sword, and only then did she sag against the wall, sliding, catching herself wrong—

Her right arm didn't come with her.

It lay on the stone behind her. Severed below the elbow where a bolt had gone through and something worse had followed. The blood was everywhere, black in the lantern-light, pooling in the seams between the stones.

"Sera—" Casper was across the gallery before thought, tearing off their coat, binding the stump high and hard while Sera's face went grey and her breath went shallow and fast.

"Hold it," Sera gritted. "Tourniquet. Tighter. I've done this to other people, I know how it goes." A laugh that was mostly pain. "Never thought I'd be on this end."

"Can you walk?"

"I can walk." She looked at her severed arm on the stone, and something crossed her face—grief for the sword hand that had served a crown worth betraying. "Leave it. It's done its last honest work."

From the side tunnels, silence. The kind of silence that has a body count. Then, slowly, Vaela limped into the lantern-light.

Casper's heart stopped.

The wolf-mother was torn open along her whole left flank—bolt wounds, blade wounds, a crossbow quarrel still buried deep beneath her foreleg. Blood sheeted her gray fur, dripping from her jaws, steaming faintly in the cold. Three hunters' bodies floated in the gallery water behind her. She had killed them all, and she had paid in meat and bone to do it.

*Little speaker.* Her mind-voice was thinner than Casper had ever heard it. Frayed. *Is the door open?*

"It's open. We're through. Rest now—we'll find help, there has to be—"

*There is no help for me in this city, and none needed yet.* She lowered herself onto the gallery stone with immense care, like something settling after a long fall. *But I will not be fast again. And what comes next cannot wait.*

"What comes next?"

*The unbinding.* She looked at Casper with her ancient silver eyes, and in them Casper saw the thing she'd carried three hundred miles and would carry until her heart quit: the message. The reason. *Tomorrow is the eclipse. Tonight, the chamber opens for preparation. You must go in while the binding is loose—you must reach the king's buried self before the ember drinks another soul and grows past any voice but its own.*

"We go together."

*I go as far as I can go.* She struggled upright, swayed, held. *Which is through that door. Carry me if you must, little speaker. I did not come three hundred miles to die in a tunnel.*

Sera pushed herself up the wall with one arm and her teeth, white-faced, blood-soaked, and picked up her sword with her remaining hand. "Nobody dies in a tunnel," she agreed hoarsely. "Bad geography for it."

Casper got Vaela's foreleg over their shoulders—she was heavier than stone, lighter than grief—and the three of them went through the broken seal together, into the red-lit dark, toward the humming heart of the basilica's foundations.

Behind them, far above, the bells began to toll midnight.

Hours now. Only hours.

And somewhere ahead, in the dark, something hungry was waiting to be fed.
