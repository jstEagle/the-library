## 9. Ashvei Rises

* * *

**CORAL**

The cell smelled of wet stone and old lies.

Coral had counted her breaths the way she counted stitches in a binding — one, two, three, until the rhythm became something she could hold. Across the dark, Edmund sat with his back against the wall, and even in the gloom she could see the tremor in his hands. The poisons were waking up. Out of the valley, out of the safe ground where his slow immunity had been built, every toxin in his blood had turned restless, like bees whose hive had been kicked.

"You should have stayed," she said. Not for the first time. She'd stopped expecting an answer hours ago.

"You should stop asking," he said. Also not for the first time. "I've told you. I'm a food-taster. Tasting is what I do."

"That's not funny."

"It wasn't a joke." His voice was rough but steady. "Coral. I crossed a world for you. Do you really think I came all this way to sit in a cell?"

She laughed despite herself — a cracked, exhausted sound. Then the floor shuddered.

Dust sifted from the ceiling. Somewhere far above, iron groaned against iron, and a sound rolled down through the council hall's foundations that Coral felt in her teeth before she heard it with her ears: a low, gathering roar, like a furnace remembering it was fire.

"What was that?" Edmund whispered.

The second roar was louder. The walls lit orange at the seams.

"Brack," Coral breathed.

* * *

**EDMUND**

Edmund had never seen a dragon before tonight, and he would spend the rest of his life failing to describe it.

Ashveil did not break free so much as *arrive*. One moment the ceiling above their corridor was stone; the next it was open sky and a shape moving through it like a storm given intention, wings wide enough to blot out the warden constellations, scales the color of banked coals going bright. The council hall — all its white marble and self-importance — caught light from below as dragon-fire poured across it, and Edmund watched a century of parasitic law begin to burn.

He was running before he decided to run. Brack had come down through the smoke with a stolen keyring and no apology, hauling the cell door open with hands that shook.

"Go," the old deserter rasped. "Heartfire's beneath the hall. Ashveil will hold them off, but not forever — the council feeds on the veil itself, they'll—"

A blast threw Brack against the wall. Wardens in grey robes poured into the corridor, and one of them raised a hand and the air *folded*, and Edmund learned that magic, when it wanted you dead, did not announce itself.

It didn't matter. He grabbed Coral's wrist and pulled, because running toward danger beside her made more sense than any safety he'd ever known.

"The rite needs an anchor!" Coral shouted over the roar. "A willing mortal — I tried alone, Edmund, it *rejected me*—"

"I know."

"How do you know?"

"Because Brack told me what the price is." He turned to face her in the burning corridor, and for once in his careful, calculated life, he didn't soften it. "And I'm offering anyway."

* * *

**CORAL**

"No."

The word came out flat and absolute, and Coral meant every syllable of it. They were sprinting down stairs that shook with each beat of Ashveil's wings, past braziers guttering blue, past a warden who took one look at them and fled.

"No," she said again. "You don't get to decide to die quietly. That's not how this works."

"Coral—"

"I read the rite. All of it. It says *a life freely given* — it doesn't say given *up*." She seized his hand, both of hers around his one, poison-trembling fingers laced through hers. "So we give it together or not at all. You want to be my anchor? Fine. But anchors come in pairs. That's just good bookbinding — you never leave a spine unsupported on one side."

For a heartbeat, in the middle of a collapsing world, Edmund Amberdale smiled.

"You're going to argue craft terminology at me right now?"

"I'm a professional."

They burst through the last door into the heartfire chamber — a vast hollow beneath the council hall where the covenant's remnant still burned: a ring of pale flame barely taller than a candle, trembling, starving, surrounded by a century of carved names of wardens who had fed on its decay. Above, muffled by stone, the battle roared. Below, there was only the small fire and the two of them.

Coral knelt at the ring's edge. "The words are simple. The anchor speaks the offering; the Reforger binds. So speak, Edmund. And whatever happens—" her voice broke, and she let it, "—don't you dare let go of my hand."

* * *

**EDMUND**

Edmund looked at his own hand in hers. A taster's hand. A hundred poisons deep, arsenic and nightshade and things without common names, a body that had spent ten years learning to survive what should kill it — alive when nothing should live.

Exactly what the rite needed.

"My name is Edmund Amberdale," he said, and the little flame leaned toward him like a listener. "I was born in a valley where magic cannot follow, and I built my freedom one poisoned sip at a time. I offer this body — saturated, half-immortal, already halfway between what harms and what heals. I give it freely. Let it hold what must be held."

The flame rose. Six feet. Ten. Heat washed over them without burning, and Edmund felt something vast turn its attention upon him — patient, ancient, weighing.

Then pain. Every poison in his blood woke at once and *sang*, and he understood with terrible clarity that the covenant was reading him like a book, testing every page. His knees hit stone. His vision went white at the edges.

*Coral,* he thought. *Don't let go.*

She didn't.

* * *

**CORAL**

She felt him start to slip — felt the rite pulling at him like a tide pulling at a swimmer, taking everything, giving nothing back. Taking his body as fuel. Taking his *life* as price.

No.

Coral had been chosen by a prophecy she never asked for, named in old ink by people long dead, pushed and pulled through worlds by everyone who claimed to know better. This — *this* — was the first thing in all of it she chose with her whole self.

She spoke the binding words over his, her voice cracking the chamber stones:

"Then take us *both*."

The flame screamed — actually screamed, a sound like tearing silk — and recoiled, and Coral seized Edmund's hand tighter and pressed her other palm flat against the burning ring.

"I am Coral Vane, bookbinder, and I bind myself beside him. Two names, one covenant. Neither anchors alone. *Take us both or take neither.*"

The fire went white.

* * *

**EDMUND**

White became gold became a color Edmund had no name for, and through it he felt the poisons in his blood stop fighting and start *holding* — each toxin becoming a thread, each thread a strand in something being woven around him and Coral both. He felt her beside him, fierce and terrified and absolutely unyielding, and he understood that the covenant was not consuming them.

It was *marrying itself to them*.

Above, the council hall burned. He could feel it now — could feel Ashveil's triumph and fury, could feel wardens scattering, could feel Brack standing witness in the smoke with his head bowed. The veil between worlds knit itself closed along new lines, lines that ran through two mortal hearts.

The last thing Edmund saw before the light took everything was Coral's eyes, inches from his, blazing.

"Together," she said.

"Together," he said.

* * *

**CORAL**

When the light faded, the heartfire burned tall and steady and warm, and two people lay on the stone floor holding hands, breathing.

Changed.

Coral could feel both realms now — the valley's quiet safety and the forest's wild grief, layered over each other like pages pressed together. She could feel the covenant humming in her blood alongside Edmund's, twin threads in one binding.

Above them, Ashveil's roar shook the ruins — not rage anymore. Something closer to relief.

And then, through the shattered doorway, footsteps. Grey robes. The surviving wardens, led by the High Warden herself, her face carved from fury and fear — and behind her, dozens more, weapons drawn, surrounding the chamber.

The High Warden looked at the steady flame, at the two figures on the floor, and her voice came out like ice cracking.

"You think a reforged covenant saves you? We have ruled these realms for a hundred years, girl. Do you truly believe we'll simply *allow*—"

Ashveil landed.

The entire mountain shook. The dragon's head filled the broken doorway, eyes like cooling embers gone suddenly, terribly bright, and her voice rolled through the chamber like a landslide learning language:

"*The covenant has spoken. The anchor and the Reforger stand within it. Touch them, and touch me.*"

Silence. Weapons wavered.

And Coral, still on the floor, still holding Edmund's hand, began — helplessly, disbelievingly — to laugh.
