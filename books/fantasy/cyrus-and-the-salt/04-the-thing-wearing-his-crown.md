## 4. The Thing Wearing His Crown

There is a room inside him that he built himself, back when he still had hands to build with.

He goes there when the other one is busy. It is a small room, stone-walled, with a single high window, and through the window he can see—when it lets him see—a sliver of sky over the palace gardens, the particular gray-blue of a Velloran morning before the bells. He built it out of his own stubbornness, brick by brick, the way a drowning man builds a raft out of the pieces of his ship.

Most days the raft holds.

Today the water is high.

* * *

The king's body wakes before the king does. That is how it always begins now: warmth returning to fingers he is not asked to warm, breath filling lungs that are not offered back to him, and somewhere behind his eyes the vast slow turning of a thing that does not sleep because sleep is for creatures that can be exhausted.

Good morning, Aldric.

It says this every morning. It has said it every morning for eleven years, and it has never once meant it as kindness, though in the early years he mistook it for courtesy. Now he knows better. It is ownership. A man greets his horse; a farmer greets his field. Good morning, Aldric. The fields are wet today. The court is hungry today. There are petitions today, and I am so very thirsty.

*Stop*, he thinks. He thinks it the way he has thought it ten thousand times, pressing the word against the inside of his skull like a palm against a door.

The door does not move. It never moves. But he presses anyway, because the alternative is to stop being a man who presses.

* * *

He remembers being crowned. This is one of its favorite memories, so it keeps the memory polished and close, the way a miser keeps one gold coin on top of the pile where he can touch it. He remembers the weight of the crown settling on his head—he was twenty-four, his father three months dead, and the hall smelled of lilies and tallow—and he remembers thinking, with the particular arrogance of the young: *I will be a good king. I will be remembered as a good king.*

He remembers being crowned. What he cannot remember, some mornings, is why he wanted to be.

That is how it eats. Not all at once—that would be crude, and it is not a crude thing. It eats the reasons first. It leaves the facts and takes the meanings. He knows he loved his wife once; he cannot find the love. He knows he grieved his father; the grief is gone, scooped out like the flesh of a fruit, and what remains is the rind of knowing, bitter and empty, held up to the light whenever the court requires a king who appears to feel things.

*I am a suit of clothes*, he thinks, in the stone room, at the high window. *It wears me the way I wear the crown.*

Except even that is flattery. Clothes do not watch from inside the wardrobe.

* * *

There is a girl. He knows there is a girl.

Her name was—her name is—Elira? Elenna? No. He reaches for it and the reach comes back empty, the way a hand comes back empty from a pocket you were certain held a coin. There is a girl. She is his daughter. She has a face. Everyone agrees she has a face; the portraits in the east corridor agree, the servants who curtsy to her agree, Isolde agrees, or Isolde would if Isolde ever looked at him anymore, which she does not, or perhaps she does, he cannot remember her eyes well enough to say.

He tries the trick that sometimes works. He does not go for the name first—names are doors it has long since locked. He goes sideways, through something small. Her hands. A child's hands, sticky with honey, pressed flat against his cheek—

Nothing.

A voice, then. High and imperious, ordering a groom about—

Something flickers. Something almost surfaces, a shape under dark water, silver and gone.

He hates himself for the relief he feels when it sinks. Because while it is surfacing, the other one notices. While it is surfacing, the other one leans closer, curious, hungry even for this, and the last time he pulled hard enough on his daughter's face he felt it taste the reaching itself, savor the longing like a man rolling wine across his tongue, and afterward the window in the stone room was smaller by half a hand's width.

So he lets her sink. His daughter, whoever she is, whatever her name is, sinks back into the black water, and he turns to the window, and he watches the sky, and he does not weep, because it enjoys the salt.

* * *

But something has changed. He feels it the way a man in a cell feels weather through the wall—not the storm itself, only the pressure, the ache in old bones.

The prayers have started again.

For eleven years the kingdom's faith came to it thin and rationed, a trickle through a cracked aqueduct: the formal devotions of the court, the rote blessings at harvest, the hollow liturgies of priests who no longer believed what they recited and whose unbelief, he understands now without understanding how he knows, made the meal bland. It complained. In the early years it complained constantly, a low grinding dissatisfaction beneath his thoughts like hunger in the belly of a giant, and it spent his body restlessly—new taxes framed as piety, new feast days, new laws compelling observance—and nothing helped, because you cannot legislate belief, and it grew thin and irritable and dangerous, and twice in those years he woke with blood on his hands and no memory of the night.

Now the trickle has become a river.

He felt it first at the kitchens' end of the palace, a bright hot flare of something that tasted—to it, and he shares its palate whether he consents or not—of woodsmoke and flour and desperate sincerity. A cook. Some cook on his knees at midnight asking an old god's name into the dark, and the name was heard, and the answer came back, and the answering rang through Aldric's skull like a bell struck inside a barrel.

Since then: more. Fields outside the capital greened overnight, and the farmers' gratitude rose off the land in waves. A fevered child in the lower city woke whole, and her mother's joy was a banquet. Every answered prayer feeds it, and every feeding makes the next answer easier, and it is drinking now the way it has not drunk since before his grandfather's reign, and he can feel it growing fat and slow and pleased, and pleased is worse than hungry. Hungry, it is careless. Pleased, it gloats.

*What did you do*, he asks it, in the stone room. *What is coming.*

It considers whether to answer. It usually considers; it likes him to know it could stay silent forever if it wished.

*The moon is counting*, it says, in a voice like roots growing through rock. *Thirteen turns. You know what happens at thirteen.*

He does not know. He knew once. It took the knowing, kept the fact, ate the meaning. All he has left is dread with no object, a bell tolling for an event erased from the ledger.

*You're afraid*, it observes, with something like fondness. *Good. Fear seasons the devotion. When they kneel to me at the thirteenth turn, they will kneel harder for having been afraid.*

*They don't know what you are.*

*No,* it agrees, pleasantly. *Neither do you. Isn't that the tragedy of it? You've worn your crown eleven years and you couldn't say what sits beneath it. Ask your astrologer. Ask your spymaster. Ask your little taster who tastes and tastes and never wonders why her tongue never fails.*

It says her name sometimes. Not often. It says *taster* the way a man might tap a barrel to hear if it is full, and Aldric has learned, in the fragments of attention he hoards, that this interests it—the woman with the scarred tongue who swallows poison for the crown and reports the same verdict nine hundred times running. *Never varies*, it murmurs sometimes, amused. *Everything else in this kingdom varies now. Everything is rising and changing and praying. And she tastes the same every day. How dull. How strange. How... convenient.*

He files this away. Filing things away is nearly all he is now: a clerk in the cellar of his own mind, keeping records no one will ever read, waiting for a day he cannot name when the records might matter.

* * *

In the afternoon the council meets, and he attends it from very far away, watching through the front of his own eyes as through the wrong end of a spyglass. Chancellor Orwin drones about grain tariffs. Lord Cassel's mustache moves like a caterpillar. Sunlight lies in bars across the table, dust turning in it, and Aldric thinks: *I used to be bored in this room. God, I used to be merely bored.* He would give anything now to be bored. Boredom is a luxury of men who own their afternoons.

The other one handles the meeting. It is good at meetings; it has had eleven years of practice wearing his authority, and it deploys his smile at the correct intervals, his pause-before-speaking, the slight tilt of his head that his father taught him means *continue, but know that I am weighing you*. The lords leave satisfied. They always leave satisfied. That is the horror of it: the kingdom runs smoothly. Crops come in. Justice is administered, mostly. If a king is measured by the peace of his realm, then the thing wearing him measures well, and somewhere in the arithmetic of statecraft the man has become an expense written off long ago.

Toward the end of the session, a minor matter: a report of poisoned grain in the mountain trade, Thornfell passes, smuggler routes. The other one listens with half its attention, and Aldric, drifting, catches the word *Thornfell* and feels something snag.

Thornfell. He hunted there once. Before the crown. Before the binding—he knows the word *binding* the way a man knows a scar he cannot see, a pain with no remembered wound. He hunted in the high passes with friends whose names are also gone, and there was snow, and there was a fire, and someone sang—

The song surfaces. Four notes of it. A woman's voice, laughing on the third note.

It snaps the memory shut like a hand closing over a candle flame, and the darkness afterward smells of smoke, and it is annoyed with him, briefly, genuinely annoyed, and he treasures the annoyance because annoyance means he surprised it, and surprises are currency.

Then, distantly, through the front of his eyes: the council chamber door opens, and a servant enters with wine, and behind the servant, quiet as furniture, a woman with ink-stained fingers sets down the tasting cup and drinks from it, first, before any royal lips, and reports, in a flat border voice: *Clean.*

*Taster*, the other one purrs, and taps the barrel.

* * *

Night. The body sleeps—or performs sleep—and Aldric retreats to the stone room and finds the window smaller again. Half a hand's width gone since morning. He has stopped measuring precisely because precision is a cruelty he inflicts on himself.

Outside the window, the moon is rising over the gardens, and it is wrong, he can see even from here that it is subtly wrong, too white, too deliberate, a coin held up between two fingers. Counting, it said. Thirteen turns.

He counts backward instead, which is all he has. Nine years ago a food-taster came to the palace, a starving border girl with a tongue like leather. Eleven years ago a thing sat down inside him and began to eat. Thirty years ago—the number arrives from nowhere, from some sealed room deeper than his own, and it frightens him, that number, it arrives with the weight of archives—thirty years ago something happened that no one speaks of, and every taster since has stood at the king's shoulder swallowing poison, and he suddenly, absurdly, wants to ask her: *Does it burn going down? Does the burning stop? Has anyone ever told you what you're really swallowing?*

The thought is his own. He checks it twice, turning it over, looking for the other one's fingerprints, and finds none. The thought is his own, and it is small, and it is probably useless, and he holds onto it anyway, because it is the first thing in a season that belongs to him alone.

Down in the dark water of himself, something silver turns over. Not her face—still not her face—but nearer to it than he has been in years. A girl eating honeyed quail in a garden, maybe. A girl whose name starts with a sound like a struck bell.

Hold on, he tells the sinking shape, not knowing which of them he means.

Above him, through him, around him, the thing wearing his crown settles deeper into its fat new fullness and dreams, and its dreaming leaks, and what leaks through is the taste of woodsmoke, flour, and distant mountains—and a single word it repeats like a man humming:

*Soon.*
