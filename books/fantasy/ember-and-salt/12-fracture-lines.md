## 12. Fracture Lines

Brannic had learned long ago that a room could break before anyone raised a voice.

He felt it now, in the tanner's loft they'd rented under a false name in the river quarter — felt it the way he'd once felt a shield wall begin to buckle, that low tremor in the boards beneath his boots, the particular silence of men deciding whether to hold or run. The candle guttered between them. Outside, the fog came off the Saltwater in grey ropes, and somewhere down the lane a dog was barking at nothing, which in this city lately meant everything.

Odile stood by the window with her arms wrapped around herself, still wearing the palace livery they hadn't yet burned. She'd told them everything twice. The hollow king. The vast and hungry thing wrapped around him like smoke around a wick. The way it had *noticed her noticing* — she said those words carefully, the way you'd set down something with an edge.

"So it saw her," Sefa said. The child sat cross-legged on the floor with a bowl of barley soup going cold in her lap. "It looked at her. And we're all just sitting here."

"We're not sitting," Brannic said. "We're deciding."

"Deciding what? It already decided. It looked at her." Sefa's voice had that flat quality it took on when the drowned things were talking through her anger, when she wasn't entirely the girl in the room anymore. "Things that look at you are things that follow you."

Mother Quill set down her needlework with the deliberate calm of a woman who had spent forty years forging documents and knew exactly how much weight a single sentence could carry. "Then let us decide quickly, and decide well. Odile. In your judgment — did it let you go because it didn't care, or because it wanted something?"

Odile didn't turn from the window. "Because it wanted something. It wants me. Or my gift. Or—" Her laugh came out cracked. "Or it wants all of us, and I'm just the door it thinks is open."

"Then we don't use that door," Brannic said.

* * *

The argument, when it finally came, came the way arguments do in small rooms full of tired people — all at once, and about everything at once.

Sefa wanted to run. Not south, not north, but *down* — to the drowned quarters beneath the harbor where the spirits knew her name and the hunters' iron couldn't follow. "The water keeps its own accounts," she said, and there was salt frost on her breath though the loft was warm. "The water remembers everyone the crown erased. We hide where memory is too deep for them to dig."

Brannic understood the impulse. He'd been a soldier; running was a strategy, not a shame. But he also knew what happened to people who ran without a destination — they became a rumor, then a story, then a name on a hunter's list.

Quill argued for negotiation. Not surrender — never that — but contact. "I can forge us into anything," she said, tapping the table where her tools lay laid out like surgical instruments: pens of three different birds, inks mixed with ash and salt and one vial of something that shimmered faintly wrong. "I can make us ambassadors, pilgrims, tax collectors. I can put words in the crown's mouth. If this thing wearing the king wants to talk, then we choose the ground, the hour, and the terms. That's how you survive a stronger enemy. You make him negotiate with your paper instead of fighting with his iron."

"And if he doesn't honor paper?" Brannic asked quietly.

Quill's hands went still. For a moment, the old forger looked every year of her age. "Then we learn that the way we've learned everything else," she said. "Too late, and together."

That left Odile, who had been silent so long that Brannic had almost stopped expecting her to speak.

"I went into the palace," she said at last, turning from the window. Her face in the candlelight was pale as ledger paper. "I stood ten feet from it. I *saw* it. And I'm telling you — fight, flee, negotiate — none of those verbs fit what's on that throne. You can't flee something that erases the road behind you. You can't negotiate with something that isn't a person anymore. And you can't fight—" She stopped. Swallowed. Started again, smaller. "You can't fight it unless you know what it actually is. Tammas's clue led us to the Emberhold. But we haven't asked the real question yet. *What is it?* What was it before it was the king?"

"The answer's in the Emberhold," Brannic said.

"The answer's in the *water*," Sefa snapped, standing, soup bowl forgotten. "The drowned remember what the land forgets. They told me things in the harbor, things I haven't even—"

"You haven't told us?" Quill's voice sharpened.

"Because you'd look at me the way you're looking at me now!"

The room went very quiet. Brannic felt the floor tilt under the weight of it — that buckling again, worse now, the wall actually giving.

"Sefa," Odile said gently. "Whatever you heard, we need—"

"No!" The word cracked like green wood. "You want to know why I didn't say? Because every time I tell you what the dead say, someone decides it means we should do something *brave*. Brave gets people ironed. Brave got my mother taken. I'm not doing brave. I'm doing *alive*."

She was moving toward the stairs as she said it, and Brannic — soldier's reflexes, borrowed strength coiling in his legs — almost caught her. Almost.

The door banged open. Fog poured in. Then her footsteps on the stairs, fast, fading, gone.

* * *

They gave it a hundred heartbeats, no more. Brannic counted them the way he counted everything now — against the dwindling purse of strength in his chest, each beat spending a coin he couldn't earn back.

"She'll go to the water," Odile said, already pulling her coat on. "The harbor steps. It's where she goes when—"

"It's a trap waiting to happen," Quill said grimly, sweeping her tools into a leather roll. "A gifted child alone at night, after the usurper has already marked our crew. Hunters patrol the waterfront since the proclamation. Every hour, more of them."

"Then we don't waste the hour arguing about whose fault it is," Brannic said, and was out the door.

The fog swallowed the streets whole. Brannic moved through it like a man swimming, calling Sefa's name low and sharp, hearing only his own echo come back wrong. Beside him Odile ran silent and quick, and behind them Quill followed at her own pace, uncomplaining, relentless, a woman built for endurance rather than speed.

They found the first sign at the chandlery corner: a smear of chalk on the doorpost, the hunter's mark for *gifted child, unclaimed*. Fresh. Fifteen minutes old, maybe less.

Brannic's blood went cold in a way the fog couldn't account for.

"They've marked her route," Odile breathed. "They've been watching the harbor steps for days."

* * *

They found her at the third stair down from the harbor road, backed against a barnacled post with two hunters closing in — big men in plain oilskin, iron rods in their gloved hands, the kind that hummed the way a struck bell hums just before the sound.

Sefa wasn't screaming. That was the worst part. She was singing — low, wavering, a song in no language Brannic knew, and the water below the steps was rising to meet her voice, climbing the stone in ropes and fists.

"Hold," one hunter said, bored as a butcher. "Just the little one. The others will come. They always come."

Brannic came.

He didn't think about it. Thinking was for men with strength to spare. He simply stepped off the top stair and let gravity and the borrowed power carry him down into the space between the hunters and the child, and the world went red and simple. The first man's rod came up; Brannic caught it barehanded and the iron screamed against his palm, burning, biting, drinking at him — and he *let* it drink, because every drop it pulled from him was a drop it wasn't pulling from Sefa, and because he had perhaps three good minutes left in his body anyway and he intended to spend them like a dying man spends gold.

The second hunter he hit once. Just once. The man folded around Brannic's fist like wet paper and slid down the barnacles into the shallows.

The first one fought harder. Iron flared; Brannic's vision went white at the edges; somewhere far away Odile was shouting his name and Quill's voice cut through the fog like a blade — reciting something, *forging* something, a name spoken aloud with such authority that the hunter hesitated, confused, suddenly unsure who he was and whom he served.

In that hesitation, Brannic broke the iron rod over his knee and put the man down beside his partner.

Then the strength left him all at once, the way a tide leaves a mudflat, and he went down on his hands and knees on the wet stones, gasping, the fog suddenly enormous and cold around him.

Small hands gripped his shoulders. Sefa. Soaked to the waist, salt-frost melting in her hair, eyes wide and terrified and furious all at once.

"You *idiot*," she said, and her voice broke on the word. "You stupid, brave idiot."

"Yeah," Brannic managed. "Takes one to—"

"Don't." She hugged him then, hard, her chin hooked over his shoulder, and he felt her shaking. "Don't you dare finish that sentence."

* * *

They carried him back to the loft between them, Odile and Sefa taking most of his weight while Quill scouted ahead and behind with a lantern shuttered to a slit. By the time they reached the tanner's stairs, Brannic could no longer pretend he was merely tired. His limbs felt like someone else's limbs, badly sewn on. The borrowed strength was nearly spent — not days left in it now, but hours, maybe a handful of mornings.

They all knew it. Nobody said it. That was its own kind of saying.

Inside, Odile lit the candle and stood holding the match a moment too long, watching the flame as if it were a map. When she turned, her face had gone hard and clear, the way it did when she balanced a book that wouldn't balance.

"I've been adding it up," she said. "All night, all of it — the mark on the chandlery, the hunters knowing the harbor steps, the timing. Someone told them where we'd be. Not the usurper. A person. A local person. Someone who knows the water quarter."

Quill went very still. "The sanctuary," she said. "The Guild of Quiet Hands."

"They sold us," Odile said simply. "Or they're about to. Either way, our safe house isn't safe, our route isn't secret, and every hour we stay here, we're sitting in a drawer waiting to be opened." She looked around at them — at Sefa wringing salt water from her braid, at Quill already laying out fresh ink, at Brannic slumped against the wall feeling his own strength ebb like a tide going out. "So we stop arguing about fight or flight. We go down. Into the Emberhold. Tonight, tomorrow, however soon we can move. Because it's the only place in this city where the answers are, and because nowhere else is safe enough to *wait* in."

Silence. The candle flame leaned and straightened.

Sefa spoke first, quiet, not looking at anyone. "The drowned told me something. About the thing in the crown." Her jaw worked. "They said it used to be a man. The king's twin brother. Erased from every record, every memory, every name. That's why it does what it does. It's not hungry for power. It's hungry for *existing*." She finally lifted her eyes. "I should have said sooner. I was scared. I'm sorry."

Quill crossed the room and put her hand on the child's head, brief and wordless, a benediction.

Brannic pushed himself upright against the wall, joints creaking like ship's rigging, and found that what was left in him — past the spent strength, past the fear — was something stubborn and warm. This ragged, cracked, impossible family of his. Fractured tonight, maybe broken. But broken things held together with salt and stubbornness had carried him further than any shield wall ever had.

"Then it's settled," he said. "We go down."

Outside, the fog pressed close against the shutters, and somewhere across the rooftops a bell began to ring — slow, heavy, the alarm toll of the Gift-Hunt patrols changing their pattern, closing like a hand.

Beneath them, deep under the city, something vast stirred in the dark of the Emberhold, and turned its attention toward the sound of their names.
