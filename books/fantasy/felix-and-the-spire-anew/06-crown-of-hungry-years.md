## 6. Crown of Hungry Years

The king wakes in a body that is not entirely his own.

He knows this the way a drowning man knows water — not as thought but as condition, as the whole of his existence now, the medium he moves through and cannot leave. He wakes inside his own skull the way one wakes inside a locked room, and somewhere beyond the walls of that room, someone wearing his face is holding court.

*Good morning, your majesty,* says the Regent, in a voice like coins poured into a velvet purse.

Aldous does not answer. He has learned that answering costs him.

* * *

There was a time — he can almost find it — when mornings were his. When waking meant the smell of beeswax and cold stone, the weight of an actual crown rather than this other thing fused to him beneath the skin of his skull, this thing that hums when it feeds. He remembers a woman's hands braiding his hair before a coronation. He does not remember her name. The Regent took the name first; names are small coins, easily spent, and the Regent spends everything.

It began, he thinks, with a bargain. Everything begins with a bargain. A kingdom fracturing, a war going badly, a young king desperate enough to listen to whatever wore silk and spoke in promises. *Let me carry what you cannot,* it had said. *Grief. Doubt. The memory of every man you sent to die at Redford Ford.* And he, foolish, grateful, had said yes.

Yes. That was the first coin he paid. All the others followed.

Now the Regent carries nothing for him. Now the Regent only takes.

* * *

He sits — is made to sit — on the throne in the great hall of Vael Corrin, and the petitioners come to him one by one, and he watches them through his own eyes as though peering out the windows of a house that has been sold. His mouth opens. His voice issues forth, warm and measured and wrong. *The crown hears you. The crown will provide.*

The Regent loves that phrase. It loves all phrases about hearing and providing and keeping. It speaks always as a custodian, never as a thief, though theft is all it knows. It eats the past out of people the way rot eats timber from within: the shape remains, the strength is gone. A woman came last month to petition for her father's lands, and she could not say why she was weeping, only that something was missing where her childhood used to be. The Regent had smiled with Aldous's mouth and granted her suit, generous as a man giving away stolen goods.

And then it had turned inward, toward the room where Aldous lives, and it had said, conversationally, *You remember her father, don't you? The miller's boy who pulled you from the frozen pond? No. Of course you don't. I have him now.*

That was three days ago. Or three years. Time moves strangely in the locked room. There are no windows in the locked room. There is only the hum of the crown, and the slow drip of himself being spent.

* * *

He hoards what he can keep.

This is the shameful arithmetic of his existence: the Regent devours memories, but it cannot eat them all at once, cannot digest a whole life in a sitting. Some things pass through it unchanged, too bitter or too small or too deeply buried to be worth the taking, and these fall back to him like crumbs from a rich man's table. He gathers them. He sorts them in the dark. He is a beggar king counting crusts.

He has the smell of rain on the training yard, the year he was nine. He has the exact blue of a certain evening sky over the western towers, though he no longer knows why that sky mattered or who stood beside him under it. He has a fragment of a song — four notes, rising — that may have been his mother's lullaby or may be nothing at all, a coin minted from wishfulness. He keeps the four notes anyway. He turns them over nightly, the way a prisoner turns a button, a nail, anything that proves the world outside still contains objects.

Some nights the Regent notices him counting. *What do you have there?* it asks, amused, patient, and Aldous closes his hands around his crumbs and says nothing, and the Regent laughs — his laugh, ruined now, repurposed — and lets him keep them, because hope is more entertaining than despair. The Regent has told him this. *I could take everything, little king. I keep you because watching you hope is the only meal left with any flavor.*

* * *

But lately — and this is new, this is terrifying, this is the first thing in twenty years that has made the Regent's hum change pitch — lately the Regent is afraid.

Aldous feels it the way a horse feels a storm coming: through the floor of himself, through the bones of the shared skull. The Regent paces. It stands at the high window of the map room for hours, looking north and east toward the mountains, and its attention — that vast, cold, grazing attention — narrows to a point.

Something is moving in the Thornfell valley. Something small. Something carrying fire.

*A scale,* the Regent murmurs, not to him, never quite to him, but aloud in the empty map room because it has forgotten how to be silent. *A dragon-scale, warm, awake. After all these years. After I starved them down to embers.* Its fingers — his fingers — drum on the table, once, twice. *Who taught it to walk? Who let it out of the dark?*

Aldous, in his locked room, goes very still.

He knows the word it is circling. He can feel the shape of it through the wall between them, the way one feels a fire through a door. *Spire.* The word comes to him without context, without story, a naked syllable glowing in the dark of what remains to him — and the fact that he still has it, that the Regent has not eaten it, tells him everything. The Regent is afraid of the word. One does not hoard what one fears; one fears what one has failed to destroy.

There was a spire once. There were keepers. There was a fire that did not belong to the crown, a fire older than the crown, a fire that —

The memory shudders and tears like wet paper. The Regent felt him reach for it. Of course it did; they share a skull; they share everything except themselves.

*Clever little king,* it says softly. *Reaching. You know what reaching costs.*

And it takes something. It reaches into the room where he lives and takes something at random, the way a jailer kicks a cell door just to hear the rattle. He does not know what until he searches for it later, turning his pockets of crumb-memory inside out, and finds the hole: the color of the evening sky over the western towers is gone. Just the color. The rest of the evening remains, hollowed, a ring left behind after the stone is pushed out.

He sits with the loss for a long time. Then, methodically, in the dark, he begins to sing his four notes.

* * *

The riders go out at dusk. He watches them muster from the balcony — his balcony, his city, his people cheering his silhouette against the red sky, cheering the thing that wears him — two hundred men in the king's black and silver, bound east for the salt road, bound for a child.

*Silver for silence,* the Regent instructs the captain, speaking through Aldous's mouth with the smoothness of long practice. *Gold for the child unharmed. The token it carries must come back to me intact. Do you understand me, Captain Vess? Intact. If so much as a scale-crack mars it, I will know whose fault it was, and I will visit that man's family, and I will take from them every day they have ever been glad.*

The captain kneels. He does not flinch. They never flinch anymore; the court has been fed on so gradually, for so long, that hunger feels like loyalty to them. This is the Regent's true kingdom, Aldous thinks, not the throne room but the appetite itself, distributed through ten thousand subjects who no longer remember what they've lost and therefore cannot miss it, cannot grieve it, cannot rise against it. You cannot revolt against a theft you cannot name.

Only the old ones flinch. Only the ones who remember the before-times look up at the balcony with something flickering behind their eyes — recognition, maybe, or its ghost. An ancient gatekeeper woman spat on the cobblestones once, years ago, passing through the capital, and looked straight up at him as she did it, and her eyes said *I know what you are* — or perhaps *I know what you were.* The Regent had her memory trimmed for it. Aldous kept the spit. It seemed honest. He counts it among his treasures.

* * *

Night. The palace sleeps, or performs sleeping. The Regent withdraws to its feeding — there are always accounts to settle, always some minister or archivist who has read too far into the wrong shelf, always pasts to be quietly amputated — and for a few hours the locked room grows quiet, and Aldous is alone with the hum of the crown and the vast dark of himself.

He does his inventory. Four notes of a song. Rain on a training yard. The weight of a real crown, which he is beginning to confuse with the weight of this one, and which frightens him more than anything else he owns, because it means even his metaphors are being colonized. The spit on the cobblestones. A hand braiding his hair — he has fought to keep the hand, surrendered the face above it, negotiated in his sleep, he thinks, bargaining with a thief in dreams. The hand stays. The hand is his.

And now, new tonight, fresh-minted, trembling: the word. *Spire.*

The Regent thinks it took his reaching along with the color of the sky. It did not. It took the color and left the reaching, because the Regent, for all its centuries, has never understood that wanting is not stored in things. Wanting is stored in the want-er. It can strip a man down to four notes and a handful of crumbs and the man will still, given one clean hour, one seam of weakness, one flame hot enough — the man will still choose.

He presses the word flat between his hands like a coal.

*Go north,* he thinks, at the child he has never seen, the small warm thing moving through the winter mountains with a dragon-scale against its chest. *Go north, and be faster than my shadow. I cannot help you. I cannot warn anyone. I am a bell with no clapper, a mouth full of another's voice. But I am still in here. Whatever else it has eaten, I am still in here, and I am rooting for the fire.*

Far away, in the map room, the Regent lifts its head — his head — like a hound catching wind.

It has felt him think it. It always feels him. It smiles with his mouth in the dark, and the smile is patient, and the hum of the crown deepens by one note, and somewhere in the high passes snow begins to fall on riders moving fast toward a valley where an old gatekeeper is lying to soldiers with silver tongues.

The Regent whispers to the mountains:

*I am coming for my ember.*
