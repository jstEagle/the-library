## 7. The Road That Remembers

The road is dark and the woman is warm. The woman's hand is big around mine. Her name is Ulyana. She says *walk* and I walk.

I know this road.

I do not say that. Saying things makes them true, and some true things are heavy.

* * *

The tunnel has a smell like wet stone and old bread. Bramble walks in front with a lamp. He is round and loud and his boots talk when he walks — *scuff scuff scuff* — like a small animal following him. I like his boots. I do not tell him that either.

Ulyana asks him questions. *How far to the crossing. Is the bridge still standing. Did the flood take the lower fork.* He answers in words that are mostly shrugs. I count his shrugs. Seven so far.

Then the tunnel opens its mouth wider, and there is a place where three roads meet, and I stop walking because I have been here before.

Not with my feet. With my sleep.

In my sleep there was a wall here with a red hand painted on it, five fingers, the little one bent. And there is a wall here now, and there is a red hand on it, five fingers, the little one bent.

"Vess?" Ulyana squeezes my hand. "What is it?"

I point at the hand.

Bramble lifts the lamp high. His face goes the color of the lamp. "That weren't there," he says. "That were not there last month. I come through here last month. There were no hand."

"Maybe you missed it."

"I don't miss things on my own roads." His voice does a wobble. "This is my road. I've walked it since I was smaller than her. There's never been a hand."

The paint is still wet. I know it is still wet because I can smell it, sweet and thick, like the inside of a plum. I touch it. It comes off on my finger, red as a berry.

Bramble makes a sound like a kettle.

"It's just paint," Ulyana says. But she wipes my finger clean on her coat, slow, like she is wiping something off me that matters more than paint.

I did not paint it. I want to say that. But my mouth keeps all my words locked up, and the only key is a language nobody here speaks, so I hold up my empty hands instead, both of them, palms out, the way you show someone you carry nothing.

Ulyana looks at my hands for a long time.

"I believe you," she says.

Her saying it feels like a blanket. I put it on.

* * *

We walk until my legs are tired ropes. We eat hard bread and cheese that tastes like the inside of a cave. Bramble talks to fill the dark — about tolls, about a man called Fennick who cheats at cards, about how the Thornfell roads are older than the kingdom, older than the kings, older than the *kings' grandmothers*, he says, and every road down here remembers every foot that ever touched it.

"That's smuggler superstition," Ulyana says.

"That's smuggler *fact*," Bramble says. "Roads down here aren't like roads up top. Up top a road lies where you leave it. Down here—" he waves the lamp, and shadows swing like hanged men, "—down here a road is more like an animal. It gets bored. It moves. You don't find the way through Thornfell. The way through Thornfell finds *you*. Or it doesn't, and you die in the dark, and the road remembers that too."

I know what he means. The roads remember me too. They have been remembering me for a long time, since before I had feet to walk them.

I dreamed this next part of the road. In my dream there was a low arch with teeth carved on it, and past the arch the tunnel went down in steps cut by no tool, steps like the inside of a throat, and there was water somewhere below, black water, going *plip. Plip.*

I walk around the next bend and there is the arch. Teeth carved on it. Steps beyond, going down, cut by no tool.

And far below, faint as a heartbeat: *plip. Plip.*

Bramble stops so fast Ulyana bumps into him. "No," he says. "No, no, no. This isn't the way to the Hollow Inn. This isn't any way. This is a *root step*, these are root roads, nobody uses the root roads, they're not roads at all, they're—" he swallows, "—they're grown."

"Grown from what?" Ulyana asks.

"From *down*," Bramble says, like that is an answer, and maybe it is.

I touch the arch. The teeth are smooth. Warm, like the roof of a mouth. Under my fingers the stone does a thing stone should not do: it leans. Just a little. Just toward me. Like a flower leaning at the sun.

I take my hand back.

"Vess." Ulyana crouches to my height. Her eyes are gray like the sky before snow. "Have you seen this place before? In your dreams?"

I nod.

"How many places, on this journey, will you have already seen?"

I think about it honestly, because she asked honest. I hold up my hands and start counting and do not stop counting, and after a while Ulyana closes my fingers down gently, one by one, like she is closing little doors.

"All right," she says. Her voice is steady but underneath the steadiness there is a small crack, like the crack in her wardstone that she thinks I do not know about. "All right. Then we follow your dreams. If the road knows you better than Bramble does, we'd be fools to argue with it."

"I hate this," Bramble says. "For the record. I hate it the most."

"Noted for the record," Ulyana says.

* * *

We sleep in a pocket of the tunnel where the air is soft. Bramble takes first watch even though his eyes keep trying to close like shop shutters. Ulyana wraps me in her coat. It smells of ink and cold iron and a little bit of woodsmoke. It smells like safety smells, if safety had a smell, which I am learning it does.

I dream.

In my dream I am very tall. Taller than Ulyana, taller than the arch with teeth. My hands are long and gray and each finger ends in a careful point, and I am holding a ledger, and the ledger is heavier than a mountain, and everyone in a great dark hall is looking at me and waiting for me to speak, and I open my mouth, and —

I wake up.

The tunnel is glowing.

Little lights, green-white, like drowned stars, are drifting up through the cracks in the floor. Dozens of them. Hundreds. They rise slow as bubbles through honey and hang in the air around us, pulsing soft, in and out, in and out, like breathing.

Bramble is standing with his back against the wall, lamp forgotten, making no sound at all, which for Bramble is the loudest sound there is.

"They answer her," he whispers. "Ulyana. They're answering her sleep-talk. She talks in her sleep and the mountain *answers*."

"What was she saying?" Ulyana is already awake, already between me and the lights, a knife in her hand I did not see her take out.

"I don't know. Old words. Deep words. Words like—" he stops. Wets his lips. "Words like the ones the old stones say. The boundary stones. The ones from before."

Ulyana looks down at me. I look up at her. The lights pulse between us, in and out, patient as tides.

"She's a child," Ulyana says.

"Aye," Bramble says. "And the mountain's a rock. And I'm a respectable merchant." He laughs, one dry bark with no fun in it. "She's not lost down here, Ulyana. That's the thing I can't — we keep saying we're taking her home, keeping her safe, guiding her through. But look at it. Look at the lights. Look at the road bending itself into her dreams before she even walks it. She's not lost. She's *expected*. Everything down here knows her name and I don't even know her name."

"Vess," I say.

They both go still.

It is the first word I have given them in their own tongue. It tastes strange in my mouth, like wearing someone else's shoes. *Vess.* My name, said in the flat bright language of the surface people.

Ulyana kneels. "Vess," she repeats, testing it. Then, careful, slow, the way you approach a deer: "Do you know what's expecting you?"

I could lie. Lying is easy; my mouth is good at staying shut. But she wiped the paint off my finger. She closed my counting fingers like doors. She gave me her coat.

So I do the truest thing my body knows. I point *down*.

The lights all drift a little lower at once, like a bow.

Bramble sits down on the ground without meaning to.

* * *

In the morning — morning being when Bramble says it is morning, because down here morning is a rumor — we walk the root steps. They are warm under my boots. All of them are warm. The whole road is warm, like something enormous lying on its side, and we are walking along its spine, and it does not mind us, and that is either a kindness or a patience.

Bramble walks differently now. He used to walk like the road belonged to him. Now he walks like a guest who has realized the house is bigger than the house. Every time the tunnel forks, he looks at me first. He waits for me to choose. He hates waiting for me to choose. He chooses to wait anyway. I am learning that this is what Bramble is made of: fear with loyalty poured over it, like syrup over something that would rather run.

Once, the road tries a trick.

We come to a stretch where the walls go narrow and the light dies, and ahead there are two ways, left and right, identical as twins. Bramble holds up the lamp and swears softly. "They weren't twins yesterday. This was straight through. I'd stake my license on it."

I stand between the twins and listen.

One passage breathes cold. One passage breathes warm. The cold one is pretending. The cold one is a mouth dressed as a road, and behind its prettiness there is a hunger with no face, and it has been waiting a long time for something small to walk in.

I point at the warm one.

"You're sure?"

I show Bramble my palms again. I carry nothing. I only tell truths.

He looks at the cold passage a moment longer. "Right," he says. "Warm one. Because the child pointed at it. This is my life now." But he goes, and Ulyana goes, and I go last, and as I pass the cold twin I feel it sigh against my cheek, disappointed, and I hear — or dream I hear, the difference down here is thin as paper — a voice like dry leaves:

*Not yet, then. But the debt comes due.*

My own phrase. My prayer. Said back to me in a stranger's mouth.

I grab Ulyana's hand and hold on hard enough to hurt and she does not ask why, which is another kind of blanket, and I add it to the pile I am carrying.

* * *

Near the end of the day the road gives us a gift, and the gift is worse than the tricks.

There is a chamber where the ceiling opens into a shaft, and far above, a coin of real daylight falls through, dusty gold, the first sun any of us has seen in a day and a half. Bramble whoops at it like a friend. Ulyana tips her head back and lets it land on her face and for a moment she looks young, younger than me almost, which is a strange thing to think about a big person.

On the wall of the chamber, where the daylight lands, there are carvings.

Old ones. Older than the red hand, older than the arch. A procession of figures cut deep into the stone, worn smooth by time, marching in a line toward a great door. And above the door, a crown. And under the crown, a small figure — smaller than all the others, set apart, alone.

Bramble raises the lamp. The shadows move. The figures seem to move with them, marching, always marching, never arriving.

"Deep folk work," he murmurs. "Pre-kingdom. You see this in museums behind glass. Not just sitting out on a wall like—"

He stops talking.

Because Ulyana has gone very quiet, and I have gone very quiet, and the reason we have both gone quiet is the same reason: the small figure under the crown is a child. Barefoot. Alone. And carved beside the small figure, worn but readable, is a line of writing in the old tongue, the tongue from my dreams, the tongue from the primer Ulyana carries wrapped in oilcloth like a wound.

Ulyana reads it aloud, halting, translating as she goes, her voice getting smaller with each word:

"*The heir hidden above. Kept safe until the mountain calls her home.*"

Nobody says anything. The dust turns in the coin of daylight. Somewhere far below, patient as ever: *plip. Plip.*

Bramble breaks first. "That's coincidence," he says. "That's — those carvings are thousands of years old, they can't be about — she's seven. Eight. She's a *foundling*, she turned up on your doorstep barefoot in the snow, that's not an heir, that's a—"

"Bramble." Ulyana's voice is gentle and terrible. "Shut up."

He shuts up.

Ulyana crouches in front of me. The daylight makes a halo of her flyaway hair, which would be funny if my chest were not full of rocks. She takes both my hands in both of hers.

"Vess. Listen to me. Whatever you are — foundling, heir, whatever the mountain thinks you are — up top, you were a child at my door, and I took you in, and that part is *mine*. That part doesn't get carved on walls or decided by courts. Do you understand me?"

I understand her. I nod.

But I also understand the other thing, the thing I have understood since the lights rose through the floor: that the road remembers me because the road made me a promise a long time ago, before kingdoms, before debts, and promises made by mountains do not care about doors or coats or the word *mine*.

Behind us, back the way we came, the twin passages have become one passage again. The cold one has folded itself away, satisfied, saving its appetite.

Ahead, the warm road goes down.

And far, far below — closer than yesterday, farther than tomorrow — something that has been setting a table for a very long time hears our footsteps on its spine, and begins, softly, to hum.
