## 5. Salt on the Crown

The boy's fever broke at midnight, and that was when the dreaming started.

Astrid had seen fevers before. She had sat with dying smugglers and birthing mothers and one memorable pox year when half of Saltrow had sweated through their sheets, and she knew the difference between a body fighting itself and a body being *spoken to*. This was the second thing. Juniper lay in her spare bed with his small hands open at his sides, palms up, the way people lie when they have surrendered something, and beneath his closed lids his eyes moved fast and desperate, like a man reading a letter he cannot put down.

She should not have touched him. She knew that even as she did it. But she had spent three days listening to him speak a language that lived behind her ribs like a swallowed coal, and the not-knowing had become worse than any danger. Astrid Okafor made her living by taking what was inside other people's heads. Restraint was for people who had something to lose.

She pressed two fingers to the boy's brow, just above the bridge of his nose, where the skin was hot and thin as paper.

And she fell.

* * *

There is a throne room with no doors.

Astrid knows this immediately, the way you know things in dreams — not by seeing but by *being told*, by the room itself pressing the knowledge into her like a seal into wax. The walls are white salt, packed and glittering, and they rise higher than any cathedral she has ever imagined, and there are no doors because no one has left this room in a very long time.

On the throne sits a king.

He is not what she expected. She had braced herself for a monster — the fever-dreams of a cursed child had led her to expect fangs, or a crown of black iron, or eyes like drained pools. Instead the man on the throne is handsome in a worn, tired way, forty years old perhaps, with grey coming in early at his temples and deep lines around a mouth that looks like it used to smile often and has recently forgotten how. His crown is gold, simple, honest workmanship. He wears it the way a man wears a yoke.

*Hello,* says the room.

Not the king. The room. The walls. The white light that comes from everywhere and nowhere, warm as candlelight and utterly without source. It has a voice like sand pouring slowly through an hourglass, patient, unhurried, and Astrid understands with a lurching horror that the voice is addressing *her*, that she is not watching a memory from outside but standing inside it, present, visible, a trespasser in someone else's skull.

*You are not supposed to be here.*

"I know," Astrid says. Her voice sounds strange, flattened, like speech heard underwater. "I'm sorry. I didn't mean to—"

*But you are hungry too.* The voice tastes the words. *I can feel it. You take things into yourself. You understand keeping.*

"I'm nothing like you."

*No?* Something shifts in the white light, a pressure, a leaning-in. *Then why are your hands full?*

Astrid looks down. Her hands are full of memories — she can feel them, dozens of them, the widow's grief she lifted last week, the smuggler's coastline, the midwife's steady calm, years and years of other people's lives carried in her cupped palms like water she refuses to spill. She has never once thought of them as hunger. She has thought of them as coin, as craft, as the only way a woman with no past can afford a roof.

The light withdraws, almost gently. Almost pityingly.

*We will return to that,* it says. *First, let me show you my housekeeping.*

* * *

The king tries to warn her.

It happens between one blink and the next — the throne room is suddenly closer, or she is suddenly closer to the throne, and the man's eyes fix on hers and for one terrible instant Astrid sees that he is *in there*. Behind his own face. Pressed against the inside of his own eyes like a drowning man against river ice.

His lips do not move. His hands do not move. Nothing about him moves except his eyes, which are screaming.

*Don't listen to it,* he does not say, but she hears anyway, the way you hear thoughts in dreams, thought-shape without sound. *Whatever it shows you, whatever it offers — it is starving. That is all it is. That is all it has ever been. Don't mistake its hunger for a plan.*

"Who are you?" Astrid breathes.

*Aldric.* The name arrives with weight attached, with banners and coronation bells and the smell of incense, a whole life folded around two syllables. *King Aldric Vane. Third of my name, though I would give the third back if I could stop wearing the first.*

The Salt's voice cuts across him like a hand smoothing a wrinkled sheet.

*My king is tired,* it says fondly. *He has reigned for eleven years and he finds the work heavy. So I help him. Watch.*

And the throne room fills with courtiers.

They come through walls that have no doors — that is the first wrongness, and Astrid's skin crawls with it, because she knows doors, she has spent her whole life in a valley whose entire virtue is that things stay out and people stay in, and a room without exits is a room where nothing leaves alive. The courtiers file in dressed in silk and ermine, bowing, smiling, and every single one of them smiles exactly the same smile. Not similar. *Identical.* The same curve of lip, the same crinkle at the eye corners, copied from face to face like a stamp.

*They come to swear,* the Salt says, and there is something in its voice now that Astrid recognizes from her own trade: the satisfaction of a craftsman at good materials. *Oaths are the finest food there is. Bread made of words. A man swears loyalty and the oath passes through him into me, and I eat the binding, and what remains in the man is — compliance. Peaceful. So peaceful. You would not believe how much trouble a kingdom has when everyone still has their own will rattling around inside them. Wars. Tax riots. Wives leaving husbands. All of that noise, gone.*

A young lord approaches the throne. He cannot be more than twenty, with a scholar's ink stains still on his fingers, and as he kneels Astrid watches his face and sees the exact moment the fear leaves it. He recites the oath in a clear voice — *my life, my service, my blood unto the last generation* — and the words rise off his tongue visibly, threads of pale gold light, and the Salt drinks them. Astrid feels it drink. It is like watching a leech swell. The young lord stands, turns, and smiles the Smile, and walks back through the wall, and somewhere behind his eyes a room has gone dark that will never be lit again.

*Eleven years of this,* Aldric screams silently, and his scream has rust in it, exhaustion, the hoarseness of a man who has been shouting against ice for a decade. *Eleven years, and it grows bolder. It used to need the oaths freely given. Now it takes. Last month it emptied a whole regiment in an afternoon. It is learning to bite through the skin instead of waiting to be fed.*

"Why tell me?" Astrid asks the drowning king. "What can I do?"

And Aldric's eyes — those imprisoned, frantic, human eyes — slide sideways. Downward. Toward a place below the throne, below the floor, below the salt.

*Toward the boy,* Astrid realizes, and her stomach drops through the dream-floor. *Toward Juniper.*

*He carries a piece of me,* Aldric says, and now the thought comes broken, flickering, because the Salt is noticing, the white light is turning toward them like a head turning. *When it took me — my wife hid our son. She could not hide the child whole, so she hid the part of me that was still mine. My true mind. A fragment. Sealed in — sealed in —*

The light flares.

*Enough,* says the Salt, and for the first time its patience cracks, and Astrid hears underneath the sand-voice something vast and hollow and howling, a hunger so old and so bottomless that her knees nearly buckle. *Enough. Keeper's blood. I smelled keeper's blood the moment you touched him.*

The throne room begins to close. The walls lean in, glittering, and the identical courtiers turn their identical smiles toward her, and Aldric's eyes hold hers for one final instant, and in that instant he gives her everything he has left, which is not much — a location, half-formed; a word in the dead language that means *vault* or *womb* or *the place beneath*; and a feeling, pressed into her like a coin into a beggar's palm:

*Please. He is six years old. Whatever it costs — do not let it eat the boy to get to me.*

* * *

Astrid came back to herself on the floor beside the bed, fingers still resting on Juniper's brow, her whole body shaking as though she had run a great distance.

Outside, the salt storm had stopped. That was wrong. Storms in the valley ran for days; this one had lasted four hours, cut off clean, like a held breath released. Through the window the night sky over Saltrow was unnaturally clear, and along the northern horizon — the direction of the capital, the direction of everything — the stars were going out one by one, in a slow line, like something enormous walking toward her and blotting them as it came.

Juniper opened his eyes. They were clear, feverless, ancient. He looked at her and said one word in the dead language, and Astrid understood it without wanting to:

*Keeper.*

Then his eyes filled with tears, and he was only a child again, reaching for her with both hands, and Astrid Okafor — who had never once in her life left the valley, who had built her entire existence around the certainty that magic could not follow her here — stood up, went to the corner, and began packing her bag.

Because the stars were going out, and something knew her name, and the only way out of the valley was the one road she had sworn never to take.

She was going to have to leave.
