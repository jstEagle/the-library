## 3. The Ember Court's Bargain

The world behind us was coming apart in the shape of a word.

I didn't understand that yet. I understood running, though. Running I knew the way other children knew their mothers' lullabies — by heart, by footfall, by the burn of it in my chest. Edmund Nystrom ran ahead of me through the birch wood with his ledger clutched against his ribs like a shield, and every few strides he looked back to make sure I was still there, as if I were the precious thing being carried out of a fire.

Which, I suppose, was exactly what was happening.

"Keep talking," he gasped.

"Talking?"

"The words. The First Tongue. Whatever you were saying back at the guildhall." He ducked under a low branch and held it for me, which no grown-up had ever done. "The veil is thinning where you speak. It's how we're getting through."

So I talked. I gave the trees the only words I had left of her — my mother's words, the ones she'd sung into my hair before the fever took her, the ones the matrons at the foundling house had slapped me for repeating because they sounded like *nothing*, like *rubbish*, like *a child making noises*. The syllables came out of me now and the air went soft and silver around them, and the birches leaned aside like courtiers making way.

I had spent six years believing my mother's language was broken. It turned out the world was what was broken, and I was one of the last people who knew how it used to fit together.

* * *

We came through at moonrise, into a country that smelled of struck flint and warm bread.

That is not a smell that goes together. That is the first thing you should know about the Ember Court: nothing there goes together, and all of it is deliberate. Fields of black glass grew wheat the color of coals. Rivers ran uphill, unhurried, as if gravity were a suggestion they'd politely declined. And everywhere, under everything, a low orange light, as though the whole land were a hearth seen from inside the wall.

Edmund stopped walking so suddenly I walked into his back.

"Mara," he said quietly. "When I tell you to run, run. When I tell you to agree to anything, don't. And whatever they offer me—" He hesitated. His hand found my shoulder, light as a bird landing. "Whatever they offer either of us, we decide together. All right? You're not cargo. I've carried enough cargo in my life to know the difference."

"You mean the ledgers," I said. "You mean the names."

He flinched. Good, some small spiteful part of me said. *Feel it.* But then he said, "Yes. The names," without looking away from me, and the spiteful part went quiet, because it is very hard to stay angry at a man who admits what he is.

They came for us before he could say more — figures made of ember-light and long shadows, wearing crowns that were clearly metaphors for something, though I couldn't have told you what. They did not touch us. They simply *arrived around us*, the way cold arrives around an open door.

And a voice that was not a voice but a warmth against the ear said:

**THE SCRIBE OF THE GUILD OF IMPOSSIBLE THINGS. AND THE ANCHOR.**

"I'm not an anchor," I said, because I was tired, and frightened, and six years old in the ways that matter even when you're older than that. "Anchors hold things down. I unmake things. Apparently."

A sound moved through the assembled Court — not laughter, exactly. More like the crackle of a fire finding dry wood.

**COME AND BE JUDGED, LITTLE UNMAKER.**

* * *

The Court kept its hall in the heart of a mountain that had been hollowed out and filled with slow fire. We stood on a floor of cooled glass, and beneath our feet, far down, something vast turned over in its sleep and glowed.

Edmund bowed. He had a scribe's bow, quick and economical, the bow of a man who has learned that lowering yourself quickly leaves less time for others to do it for you. I did not bow. Nobody had taught me how, and I found, standing in that burning hall, that I did not want to learn.

**THE COVENANT FAILS,** said the warmth-voice, which I understood now belonged to all of them at once, the way a chorus belongs to no single throat. **IT WAS WRITTEN IN THE FIRST TONGUE, SEALED IN EMBER-BLOOD, AND ANCHORED IN A LINE OF SPEAKERS. YOUR GUILD HAS BEEN BURNING THE PAGES FOR A HUNDRED YEARS AND CALLING IT ARCHIVE. THE LAST SPEAKER STANDS BEFORE US. WHEN HER WORDS ARE SPENT OR SILENCED, THE VEIL TEARS ENTIRELY. YOUR REALM FALLS INTO OURS. OURS FALLS INTO YOURS. NEITHER SURVIVES THE MEETING.**

"So reforging it is urgent," Edmund said. His voice was steady. His hands, at his sides, were not. I had learned to read hands; a scribe's hands confess everything.

**URGENT IS A MILD WORD. IT MUST BE REWRITTEN BY DAWN. IN THE FIRST TONGUE. IN BLOOD THAT FREELY CHOOSES THE BINDING.**

"And if it isn't?"

**THEN BOTH REALMS BURN, SCRIBE, AND YOU WILL NOT BE ALIVE TO FILE THE REPORT.**

Silence. The fire below turned over again.

Then the Court did the thing I would think about for the rest of my life, in both of them. It turned its attention — all that vast, warm, terrible attention — onto Edmund alone, and offered him a bargain.

**YOU ARE INDENTURED. WE HAVE READ THE SHAPE OF THE DEBT ON YOU; IT WRAPS YOU LIKE WIRE. THE GUILD OWNS YOUR HANDS UNTIL YOUR FIFTIETH YEAR. WE CAN UNMAKE THAT. ONE WORD FROM US AND THE LEDGER THAT BINDS YOU BURNS TO ASH. YOU WALK FREE, SCRIBE. FREE AT THIRTY-SIX INSTEAD OF FIFTY. FOURTEEN YEARS RETURNED TO YOU.**

Fourteen years. I watched those words land on him. I watched him do the arithmetic every indentured person does without meaning to — fourteen years of mornings he chose himself, of ink he spilled for no one, of doors he could open just to feel the hinge move. I watched him want it the way a drowning man wants air: totally, helplessly, with his whole body.

All he had to do was hand them me.

"All you want in exchange," Edmund said slowly, "is Mara."

**THE ANCHOR MUST BE HELD SOMEWHERE WHILE THE COVENANT IS REMADE. SHE WILL LIVE HERE, IN COMFORT, IN HONOR, AMONG THE EMBER-FOLK WHO WILL CHERISH WHAT YOUR GUILD SOUGHT TO ERASE—**

"She'd be a page," Edmund said. "Kept flat. Kept safe. Kept *still*." His voice cracked on the last word, and I realized he wasn't arguing with them. He was arguing with himself, loudly, in front of witnesses, and winning. "You're offering me freedom to put a child in a case with glass over her. I've catalogued enough relics to know what 'cherished' looks like. It looks like a lock."

**SCRIBE—**

"No." He stepped forward, in front of me, this thin man with ink-stained fingers and a debt wrapped around him like wire, facing down a court of living fire. "No. You can keep your fourteen years. You can keep fifty. Here is what I know about debts: I've written them my whole life, and every one of them was a story someone told about what a person owed. Well. Here's mine." He drew a breath. "I owe Mara nothing. She owes me nothing. What's between us isn't in any ledger, and I will not trade it, and I will not trade *her*, and if both realms burn tomorrow then let them find us standing on the same side of the ash."

The hall went very quiet. Even the fire below seemed to hold its breath.

Then the warmth-voice spoke again, and it had changed. Softer. Almost amused.

**IN THREE HUNDRED YEARS, EVERY AGENT OF THE GUILD OFFERED THIS BARGAIN HAS SAID YES.**

"They would," Edmund said. "It's the first thing the guild teaches you: everything is owed, and everyone can be priced."

**AND YOU REFUSE.**

"I refuse."

**KNOW THEN, SCRIBE, WHAT YOUR REFUSAL COSTS. THE GUILD'S HUNTERS HAVE CROSSED ALREADY. THEY COME FOR THE ANCHOR WITH IRON AND BLANK PAGES, AND THEY WILL BE HERE BEFORE DAWN. WE CANNOT INTERVENE — THE OLD TERMS FORBID IT UNTIL THE NEW ONES ARE WRITTEN. YOU HAVE CHOSEN THE HARDER DEBT.**

"I know," Edmund said. And then, because he was who he was, he added: "I'd like it noted in the record that I chose it anyway."

Something in the hall — I swear this — *laughed*.

**NOTED,** said the Court. **GO, THEN. BOTH OF YOU. AND LISTEN WELL: THE COVENANT MUST BE REFORGED BY DAWN, IN THE FIRST TONGUE, IN FREELY GIVEN BLOOD. THERE IS ONLY ONE PLACE WHERE SUCH A WRITING CAN TAKE HOLD — THE THRESHOLD WHERE YOUR GUILDHALL STANDS, WHERE THE FIRST WORD WAS EVER SPOKEN. RUN, LITTLE UNMAKER. RUN, KEEPER-THAT-IS-NOT-YET. AND SCRIBE—**

The warmth gathered itself, one last time, close against my ear and his.

**WE HAVE WATCHED A GREAT MANY CREATURES CROSS THIS HALL. FEW HAVE EVER LEFT IT POORER AND RICHER AT ONCE. GUARD WHAT YOU HAVE. IT IS RARER THAN EITHER REALM.**

* * *

We ran.

Behind us the ember-light dimmed, and ahead the veil hung like a curtain of grey silk, already shivering — and through it, faint but unmistakable, came the sound I had been dreading since the guildhall: boots. Many boots. And voices reciting, flat and toneless, the guild's nulling-cant, the words that ate language.

Edmund's hand closed around mine. Small, ink-stained, trembling, and absolutely certain.

"Mara," he said. "Do you remember what I told you at the border? About deciding things together?"

"Yes."

"Decide fast. They've brought the blank pages. And I know exactly whose blood we're going to need."
