## 14. Debt of the Wolf Queen

The wolves did not come to Xanthe. That was the first thing Xanthe learned about calling in a debt: you did not summon them, you *asked*, and then you waited in the cold until they deigned to arrive.

Xanthe had been waiting three days at the edge of the Thornwood, in the ruins of a shepherd's cot that smelled of old smoke and older sheep, when the crows finally brought word.

"They're coming," rasped the eldest crow, perched on the broken lintel with the air of one delivering news it found personally tedious. "All of them. The Queen insisted."

"The Queen?"

"You'll see." The crow preened a wing feather back into place. "I'd stand up straight if I were you. She hates slouching."

"I'm sitting on a floor."

"Then I'd get off the floor."

Xanthe got off the floor.

They came out of the treeline at dusk — not the ragged handful of scouts who had covered their escape from Vellamare, but dozens of wolves, grey and black and storm-silvered, moving through the bracken with the terrible quiet of things that had never once needed to apologize for existing. And at their head, larger than any wolf had a right to be, with fur the color of frost over iron and one ear notched like a torn banner, came a she-wolf whose gaze made Xanthe's spine attempt to exit through the top of their skull.

*The Speaker,* she said, and her voice was like a landslide deciding to be polite about it. *You are smaller than I expected.*

"People say that," Xanthe managed. "Usually right before they ask me for something."

*Amusing.* She circled once, slow, nostrils flaring as though Xanthe were something she might yet decide to eat. *I am Vaela. I led the packs that bought your coward's retreat from Vellamare. Three of mine took witch-finder steel so that two soft-skinned creatures could run.*

"I know." Xanthe's throat tightened. "I've carried their names since. All three."

Vaela stopped circling. Her head tilted, one good ear forward. *You carry names.*

"Brannic. Sorrow-of-the-Long-Night. And the young one, Fern, who was fastest and died slowest." Xanthe said each name carefully, the way you set down something breakable. "I say them every morning. I'll say them every morning until this is finished or I'm finished."

The clearing went very still. Somewhere overhead, the crows had stopped even pretending not to listen.

*Hm,* said Vaela at last, which Xanthe would later learn was, by wolf standards, an ovation. *You owe us a favor, Speaker. Unnamed. That was our price, and you paid it without haggling, which was either wisdom or stupidity, and I have spent a season deciding which.* She sat, folding her great paws before her like a queen taking a throne that happened to be mud. *Now I name it.*

Xanthe's heart performed a small, grim maneuver. "Name it."

*Ashfall's citadel stands on the bones of the old covenant ground. You knew this?*

"I suspected. Rosalind found references — the hall beneath the citadel is built over the place where the tablet was first sworn."

*Then know the rest.* Vaela's voice dropped, and every wolf in the clearing leaned in to hear, so that the whole forest seemed to lean with her. *The third relic is not hidden in some drowned temple or buried vault. It never left the covenant ground. Ashfall keeps it beneath his own feet. The original tablet — the First Word, the stone on which the covenant between your kind and ours was written in both bloods — sits in his deepest cellar, behind his strongest wards, because he knows what we know: that whoever holds the tablet holds the covenant itself.*

Xanthe felt the truth of it land like a stone dropped into still water. Of course. Of course he kept it close. A usurper does not steal a kingdom and leave the deed lying about.

"He stole it," Xanthe said slowly. "That's how he did it. He didn't just outlaw magic — he took the tablet, broke the public covenant, and rewrote the law so no one could prove there'd ever been anything else."

*He silenced us first,* Vaela said, and for the first time something moved under the ice of her voice — something old and furious and grieving. *A hundred years ago, the beasts spoke in the councils of men. There were Speakers in every city, and the tablet bound both realms to hear one another. Ashfall could not rule a world where the small voices had votes. So he took the stone, forged his edicts, and taught your kind that our voices were superstition and yours were crime.* Her lip curled from her teeth. *He did not fear magic, Speaker. He feared being answered.*

The crows muttered agreement in the branches. One of them said something unrepeatable about Lord-Regents generally.

"So the favor," Xanthe said. "You want me to steal it back."

*I want more than theft.* Vaela rose, and the whole pack rose with her, a ripple of muscle and intent. *A stolen tablet smuggled out in a sack changes nothing. Ashfall would hunt it, and we would bleed again, and in a hundred years some other clever man would steal it again. No. The tablet must be reclaimed *openly*. Sworn again, in the old ground, before witnesses of both realms. It must be *lawful*. And laws, Speaker, require someone to speak them.*

"And that someone is—"

*You.*

The word landed in the clearing like an axe.

"There are other Speakers," Xanthe said quickly. "There's a whole army of outcasts gathering at Highmoor —"

*Who look to you already. Who crossed the borderlands because you asked. Who will march because you lead.* Vaela stepped closer, and her breath was warm and wild against Xanthe's face. *Do not mistake what I am offering. This is not a request I make lightly, nor a trap. I have watched you bargain with rats and mourn my dead and argue with delegates twice your age until they agreed to things they swore they never would. You do not want to lead. Good. The ones who want to lead should never be allowed near it.*

"That's a terrible system."

*It has kept us alive ten thousand years.* Her eyes held Xanthe's, unblinking. *Here is the debt, named at last: the packs of the Thornwood, the rat-clans of the sewers, the crows, the eels of the deep roads, every beast who remembers the covenant — we will march on Ashfall's citadel. We will bleed for it, as we bled at Vellamare. But we do not follow Sigil-Bearers and we do not follow generals and we do not follow prophets. We follow the Speaker. You walk at the front, Xanthe Thackeray. You give the word, and every voice in this forest answers it. That is the favor. That is the whole of it.*

Xanthe stood very still.

They thought of the night market, of charms sold cheap and gossip bought cheaper, of a life built carefully small so no one would ever look too closely. They thought of Rosalind's face in the dark of the reliquary, saying *you were never meant to hide*. They thought of Brannic and Sorrow-of-the-Long-Night and little Fern, and of all the silenced voices in Ashfall's cages, waiting a hundred years for someone to answer them.

Every part of them wanted to refuse. Every part of them had always wanted to refuse; that was the whole architecture of their life.

And underneath all of it, quieter than a rat's footstep, was the knowledge that refusing had never actually been on the table. Not since the night a stranger bought a luck-charm and lingered.

"If I give the word," Xanthe said, "people die. Wolves die. Rats. Crows. People who trusted me."

*Yes.*

"And if I don't, they die anyway, just slower, in cages, unheard."

*Yes.*

"That's not much of a choice."

*It is the only kind worth having.* Vaela lowered her great head until her forehead nearly touched Xanthe's, and her voice, when it came, was almost gentle. *You think leadership is wanting it. It is not. Leadership is standing where the blow falls first and saying 'follow me' anyway. I have done it forty winters. I would hand it to you gladly for one night, if it bought my children back their voices.*

Xanthe closed their eyes. Breathed. Opened them.

"All right," they said. "I'll lead. But I have terms."

Vaela's ears pricked forward, and somewhere in the dark, a crow snorted. *Terms.*

"One: nobody marches until Rosalind is free. We go together or not at all — she's the Bearer, and if the prophecy wants a death, I intend to be extremely inconvenient about providing one. Two: no beast dies holding a door when a Speaker could hold it instead. My kind breaks things; let us bear the breaking. Three—" Xanthe's voice caught, steadied. "Three: when this is done, when the tablet is sworn and the covenant stands again, the first thing written on the new page is the names of every creature who fell getting there. Both realms. All of them. No one gets forgotten to make the story tidier."

The clearing was silent long enough that Xanthe heard their own heart.

Then Vaela threw back her head and howled — and the forest answered. Dozens of voices, then hundreds, wolves near and far joining until the sound rolled over the hills like weather, and the crows rose shrieking into the purple sky, and somewhere in the earth below, Xanthe would have sworn, the rats were singing too.

*It is sworn,* Vaela said when the last echo died. *The Speaker leads. Send your word when the Bearer is free, and we come.* She turned, then paused, glancing back with something that in a human face would have been a grin. *One more thing, small one. Your mate waits in a cage. March fast.*

"She's not my—" Xanthe began, but the Queen of the Thornwood was already vanishing into the trees, having delivered her parting shot with the precision of a creature who had been saving it up.

The crows spiraled down around Xanthe as the pack faded into the dark.

"Well," said the eldest crow. "That went better than the last Speaker who negotiated with her. He cried."

"I considered it."

"We noticed." The crow hopped closer, head cocking. "So. You're going to lead an army of beasts into the most fortified citadel in the free cities to steal a rock from the most dangerous man alive. What's the plan?"

Xanthe looked east, toward Greyhollow, toward the citadel's distant shadow, toward a cell where Rosalind was reciting false prophecies to infuriate her jailers, utterly unaware that an army was assembling in her name.

"The plan," Xanthe said, "is that Serah Dune is going to have a very bad week."

* * *

They traveled light and fast after that — a crow relay ahead, rat-runners below, Xanthe moving through the borderlands like a rumor with legs. By the fourth night they reached the high pass above Greyhollow, where the outcast Speakers had gathered, and found the camp in an uproar.

Not the bad kind. The other kind.

Word had traveled faster than Xanthe — it usually did; rats were terrible gossips and proud of it — and the camp turned out along the ridge to watch them come. Old Marta with her hedge-craft and her six cats. The twins Ferro and Liss, who spoke to moths and were deeply embarrassed about it. A dozen others, frightened people who had hidden their whole lives, now standing in the open with torches, looking at Xanthe like Xanthe was something other than a crime.

"They're saying you spoke with the Wolf Queen," Marta said, falling into step beside them. "They're saying the packs will march."

"They're saying right."

"Then it's true? All of it?" Her voice cracked on the question, hope and terror fighting openly in her face. "We're really doing this? We're really going to *answer*?"

Xanthe looked out over the torchlit camp — at the frightened, stubborn, extraordinary people who had spent their lives being quiet and were, tonight, choosing noise.

"Yes," Xanthe said, and found, to their enormous surprise, that the word didn't shake. "But first we need a way into the citadel. And I happen to know exactly which witch-finder is going to give us one."

Above the pass, far to the east, a single signal fire bloomed on a mountain peak — and Xanthe, watching it, smiled for the first time in days.

Someone had opened the door.
