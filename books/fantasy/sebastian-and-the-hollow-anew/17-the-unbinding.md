## 17. The Unbinding

### Rosalind

The throne room of Vellamere had been built to make you feel small, and it succeeded even now, flooded ankle-deep in salt water, its gilded ceiling furred with white mineral bloom like the inside of a very expensive seashell. I stood in the shallows with a bone knife in one hand and my own future in the other, and I thought: someone paid a fortune for this room, and the sea is redecorating it for free.

"Rosalind." Sebastian's voice came out layered, two speakers in one throat — his own, young and frightened, and beneath it the older tongue, the one that made the water shiver. "It's starting."

"I know." The dead had been screaming at me since we entered the undercrofts, all of them at once, a choir with no conductor. But underneath the noise there was a thread, and I followed it the way I'd follow any voice: patiently, stubbornly, the way my mother taught me before she became one of them. "Say your part. I'll say mine."

My part was the wrist-bone resting in my left palm, still warm from my body, still technically mine. Odric's notes had been clear about the mechanics, in the way scholars are clear about things that should be unsayable. A prison of willingly given bone. A binding spoken in the old tongue by a willing mouth. And a price written into the material itself: whatever the bone held, the giver lost. My wrist-bone had heard forty years of the dead. Every whisper I'd ever caught, every mourning charm I'd ever secretly charged with more than decoration — it was all in there, marrow-deep.

I was about to carve away the only sense that had ever made me feel less alone.

Well. I'd survived worse company than silence. Probably.

The Hollow sat on the throne wearing King Aldous like a coat five years out of fashion. That was the horror of it, up close — not monstrousness but *fit*. It had worn him so long it had learned his posture, his breathing, the way his hands rested on the armrests carved with lions whose faces had dissolved to salt. Only the eyes were wrong. Hollow-eyed, Corvane had said, and she'd been precise: not empty, but *hollow*, as if someone had scooped out the man and left the shape of him standing.

"You came," it said, in Aldous's voice, warm as a hearth. "I hoped you would. So few visitors brave the plumbing."

"Don't talk to it," Sebastian whispered.

"I wasn't talking to it," I said. "I was talking to the king. If he's in there."

The thing on the throne smiled with Aldous's mouth, and for one terrible half-second the smile was entirely human, and entirely pleading.

* * *

### Sebastian

The words were already moving in me before I chose them.

That was how it always felt, in the worst moments — like the language wasn't something I spoke but something that spoke through me, a river using my mouth as a culvert. Rosalind had spent weeks teaching me otherwise. *You are not a door,* she'd said, tapping my sternum with her carving knife, gently, which for Rosalind was practically an embrace. *You're a throat. Throats choose what they swallow.*

So I chose. I planted my bare feet in the salt water — I hadn't worn shoes since the night I ran from the palace, some superstition I couldn't explain, and tonight of all nights I wasn't going to start — and I began the unbinding.

The old tongue doesn't have a word for *unbinding*, exactly. What it has is a word that means both *opening* and *grieving*, depending on where you put the stress. Rosalind had drilled me on the stress until I dreamed in it. I put it where the sorceress would have wanted it. Where the king would have wanted it.

*Grieving-open. Open-grieving. Let what was swallowed remember its own name.*

The water reacted first. It went still — not calm, but *attentive*, every drop listening. Then the salt bloom on the walls began to fall, great soft flakes of it drifting down like indoor snow, and each flake hummed faintly as it fell, and I understood that the palace itself had been holding its breath for five years.

The Hollow stood up.

"No," it said, and Aldous's warmth drained out of the voice, leaving something older underneath, something that sounded like a well echoing. "No, little archive. You don't know what you're opening."

"I know exactly," I said, and my voice didn't shake, which surprised me. "You ate it. I'm giving it back."

"It isn't *his*," the Hollow said, and here was the terrible thing, the thing I'd have to fight later, in the dark, when this was over if I survived it: it said it kindly. "Nothing is anyone's. Memory is a current, boy. I am only the riverbed. Give me what you carry and I will give you back your childhood — every scraped knee, every lullaby, your mother's—"

"*Don't.*" The word cracked out of me in two languages at once. "You showed me her face once. You don't get to hold it again."

Behind me, I heard the small wet sound of Rosalind's knife entering bone. I didn't look. Looking would have undone me. Some things you can only do if you refuse to watch the person you love pay for them.

* * *

### Rosalind

Carving your own wrist-bone while it's still attached is exactly as pleasant as it sounds. I'll spare you the details except one: bone sings. Everything I'd ever carved sang under the knife, a thin harmonic only I could hear, and my own wrist sang loudest of all, a note like a struck glass, like my mother humming over her workbench, and I had to bite my cheek bloody to keep my hands steady.

The design was Odric's — a spiral prison, closed at both ends, with the Hollow's true name (which Sebastian had pried out of a drowned chambermaid's memory three days prior) coiled down the center like a worm in an apple. Every groove had to be cut in order. No second chances. Bone doesn't forgive revision.

The dead crowded around me so thickly I could barely see the throne room. They knew what I was doing. Some of them — my mother among them, front row as always, arms crossed in that judgmental way of hers — were trying to tell me something. I couldn't stop to listen. For the first time in my life, I shut them out on purpose, and it felt like going deaf in a burning house to save my hands.

"Rosalind." Sebastian's voice, fraying. "It's fighting the words. It's — oh. Oh no. It's showing me—"

"Sebastian. Stress on the third syllable. *Grieving*-open. Don't listen to anything it offers. Nothing it has is free."

"But it's *her*, it's my mother, she's asking for me—"

"She's been dead eleven years and whatever's asking is wearing her like a glove. Cut the third syllable deep and keep walking."

Gods forgive me. I said it like it was easy. I said it while my own mother screamed my name six inches from my ear, trying to warn me — or bless me, or just say goodbye; I'd never know, because in four minutes I'd never have heard her at all.

The spiral took shape. Third ring. Fourth. My blood dripped into the salt water and bloomed there like ink, like roses, like everything red I'd ever pretended not to feel.

And then the Hollow turned from Sebastian and looked at me, and King Aldous surfaced.

* * *

### Sebastian

I felt it happen before I saw it — a shift in the pressure of the room, like a knot loosening. The Hollow staggered, caught itself on the throne's armrest, and the salt-crusted lion heads seemed to lean in.

And the eyes changed.

They were still hollow-rimmed, still wrong, but something looked *out* of them suddenly, something exhausted and drowning and furiously alive, and I knew it the way you know your own reflection: I had carried this man's coronation, his marriage, his grief for his stillborn daughter, his terror on the last night, all of it pressed into me since I was seven years old. I knew King Aldous the way I'd never known my own father.

"*Boy,*" he said. His real voice. Rougher than the Hollow's version. Human. "*Listen to me. Both of you. Stop.*"

"The unbinding doesn't stop," I said, and hated how young I sounded.

"It must." He gripped the armrest hard enough that salt flaked away beneath his fingers. "You don't understand what it costs. I've watched it work from the inside for five years. I know its arithmetic better than it does. The binding wants a vessel and the vessel wants a life — it will take the boy's memories whole, sorceress, do you hear me? Not the Hollow's leavings. *His.* His childhood, his name, his—"

"That's not the binding Odric mapped," Rosalind said flatly. She didn't look up from the bone. Her hands never stopped moving. I'd learn later that her hands never stopped moving precisely because looking up would have ended everything.

"Your scholar mapped the theory," Aldous said. "I've lived the practice. Five years, madam, of being the practice." He turned those ruined eyes on me, and his voice dropped, gentled, became the voice I'd heard in a hundred inherited dreams, reading me to sleep across a distance of decades and death. "Sebastian. I have watched you grow up inside my memories. Do you know what that's like? To be a grandfather to a child who runs from you? I have loved you since you were seven years old and I will not — I *refuse* — to be saved at the price of you. Put down the knife. Both of you. Let me drown. I've had five years to make peace with the water."

The words hit me harder than any blow the Hollow had landed. Because I knew they were true. Because I could feel, in the memories he'd given me, exactly how much of himself he'd spent keeping the Hollow from reaching past the palace walls — five years of slow erosion, a king used as a dam.

"If we stop," I said, "it finishes eating the city. Corvane's daughter. Everyone's grandmothers. Everyone's names."

"Then let it finish with me last," he said, "and find another way—"

"There is no other way," Rosalind said quietly. "There's only this one, and it's terrible, and we're taking it."

* * *

### Rosalind

He looked at me then, the king did, and I gave him the truth because he'd earned it and because lying to a drowning man seemed gauche even by my standards.

"Your Majesty. I've spent my whole life hearing the dead and pretending I didn't. Shrinking myself down to survive. Selling whispers of my gift because the whole gift would get me burned." The knife moved. Fifth ring. My hand was steady and the rest of me was not. "Tonight I stop shrinking. This bone holds every voice I've ever heard. When the binding closes, I lose all of it — permanently, irrevocably, gone. I know the price. I've read the ledger. I'm paying anyway."

"And the boy?"

"The boy keeps what's his. That's the whole point of him." I finally looked up, and met Aldous's eyes, man to near-corpse. "You sealed your memories in a child to save them. We're doing the same thing, only we're asking first. That's the difference between you and us, sir, and I mean it respectfully."

Something moved through his face — pain, and pride, and underneath both, terribly, relief. The relief of a man who has been holding a door shut for five years finally hearing someone say *we'll take it from here.*

"Then hurry," he said, and his voice was already fraying, the Hollow pulling him back under like a tide. "It's — it's found the flaw in the boy's words, it's — *Sebastian, the sixth syllable, it's turning your grieving into its*—"

The light in his eyes guttered. The Hollow rose behind them like a shadow rising off a wall.

* * *

### Sebastian

The sixth syllable. He was right — I could feel it, the word curdling in my mouth, the *grieving* souring into something hungrier, the language bending toward the Hollow the way water bends toward a drain. It had been listening to me speak for minutes now, learning the shape of my voice, fitting itself into the spaces between my syllables.

*Rosalind—*

"One ring left," she said. Her face was gray. Blood ran freely from her wrist now, and the bone in her hands was half-carved, spiraled, beautiful in the way prisons are beautiful, which is to say terribly. "Talk faster."

So I talked faster. I threw the rest of the unbinding at the Hollow like a man throwing stones at a flood — the opening-words, the naming-words, the words the court sorceress had died whispering, the ones she'd pressed into a seven-year-old boy along with a king's entire memory and one instruction: *run.* I spoke them with the stress in the right places and the love in the wrong ones and both of them counted, both of them mattered, the old tongue weighing everything.

The water rose. Not gradually — *all at once*, a wall of brine lifting off the floor and hanging in the air around the throne, and in it I saw faces. Thousands of them. Everyone the Hollow had ever eaten, suspended in salt water, mouths open, and they weren't screaming.

They were *helping*. Echoing me. Every stolen grandmother and forgotten uncle repeating my words a half-beat behind, a chorus of the digested, lending their voices back for one moment against the thing that had taken them.

The Hollow shrieked — a sound with no throat in it, no king, no humanity, just appetite meeting resistance — and it turned from Aldous, from the throne, from everything, and it *lunged* at me.

Not at my body. At what I carried. I felt it pour toward the place inside me where King Aldous lived, five years of a man folded small, and I braced to hold the door—

"NOW," Rosalind roared, and the last ring closed under her knife, and she raised the finished bone above her head dripping with her own blood, and the whole room — the water, the dead, the drowning king, the salt-bloom snow — stopped.

Everything waited on the next word.

Mine to speak. Hers to bind.

I opened my mouth —

—and the Hollow got there first. It poured into me like cold, like night, like every memory it had ever stolen arriving all at once with nowhere else to go, generations upon generations upon generations, a thousand years of other people's lives crashing into the small room of my skull—

*Rosalind's knife flashed down.*

* * *

### Rosalind

Later — if there was a later — people would ask me what the moment of binding felt like, and I'd tell them the truth: it felt like a dam breaking in reverse.

The Hollow came out of Sebastian in a torrent and into the bone in a stream and the stream was endless, years of it, centuries, whole dynasties funneling through my carved spiral, and the bone grew hot and heavier than bone should be, heavy as guilt, heavy as a city's worth of stolen inheritance, and I held on. My mother's voice was the last thing I heard clearly — not warning, not blessing, just my name, *Rosalind*, the way she said it when I came home — and then the final groove sealed, and the world went quiet.

Not quiet. *Silence.* There's a difference, and I live in it now.

The bone went cold in my hand. Ordinary. Just a carved thing, a trinket, the kind of thing I'd sold a thousand of to people who never knew what they were buying.

On the throne, King Aldous slumped forward, gasping, whole, hollowed of everything except himself — and alive, gods help us, alive, blinking at his own hands like a man waking from a long illness.

And Sebastian lay in the shallow water, eyes open, staring at nothing, his lips moving silently through words in a language only he could now remember.

He didn't answer when I called his name.

He didn't answer the second time, either.

And somewhere beneath the silence where my dead used to live, I felt the bone in my hand shift — just slightly, just once — like something settling in for a very long stay.
