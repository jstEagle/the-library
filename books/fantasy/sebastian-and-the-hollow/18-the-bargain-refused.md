## 18. The Bargain Refused

The keystone was warm in Sebastian's hands.

That was the first thing he noticed — that and how absurd it was to notice anything at all. Gideon lay in his arms with a wound through their chest that should have ended everything, blood pooling black in the starless dark of the throne room, and Sebastian's mind had chosen this moment to observe that the stone felt like a loaf of bread fresh from the oven. His mother — whoever she had been, whatever she had made him for — would have despaired of him.

*Or perhaps not,* he thought. *Perhaps she built me exactly like this. A clockmaker's apprentice to the end. Noticing the small things while the large ones kill him.*

The three keystones sat before him on the shattered dais: water, stone, and now blood. The third pulsed like a heart that had been beating for two hundred years without permission from anyone. Around it, the covenant's voice filled the ruined court — not sound, exactly, but pressure, a presence older than the Ashen Court, older than Hollowmere, patient as erosion.

***THE STONES ARE ASSEMBLED,*** it said. ***ONE LIFE BOUND TO THE MAGIC. THE TERMS ARE OLD AND THEY ARE CLEAR.***

"I know the terms," Sebastian said.

***THEN CHOOSE.***

He looked down at Gideon. Their eyes were open — of course they were open; Gideon Delacroix had never once in Sebastian's acquaintance done the sensible thing and simply faint — and they were trying to speak. Blood at the corner of their mouth. A hand rising, trembling, to press against his wrist.

"Don't," Gideon rasped. "Sebastian. Whatever it's offering—"

"It's not offering," Sebastian said quietly. "That's rather the whole problem."

***THE VESSEL UNDERSTANDS,*** the covenant said, and there was something almost like satisfaction in it, vast and cold as deep water. ***THE VESSEL WAS MADE FOR THIS.***

"Yes," Sebastian said. "I was. That's what everyone keeps telling me. Cassian told me. The murals told me. You're telling me." He shifted Gideon gently against a fallen pillar, ignoring the wet warmth spreading over his hands, and stood. "Everyone has been telling me what I was made for since the moment I learned I was made at all. And do you know what none of you have done? Not one of you — not the covenant, not Cassian, not the woman who sealed me in a warded cradle, not even—" his voice caught, "—not even the person bleeding out behind me — not one of you asked me what I *wanted*."

***WANT IS IRRELEVANT TO COVENANT.***

"Then we're going to have a disagreement about vocabulary," Sebastian said, "because where I come from, an agreement nobody agreed to is called something else entirely."

The magic pressed down on him. He felt it then, truly felt it, for the first time since the stars had gone out — the full weight of the old bargain, the machinery of it, two realms lashed together by a contract written in blood because blood had been the only currency its authors trusted. It was not evil. That was the terrible thing. It was simply old, and tired, and built by people who could imagine only one way to hold something together: bind it, take from it, make the price so high no one would ever dare refuse.

Gideon made a small sound behind him. Sebastian did not turn around. If he turned around, he would look at them, and if he looked at them, he would do the arithmetic the covenant wanted him to do — *one life for two realms, one death for everything* — and the arithmetic was seductive. It was designed to be seductive. It had been polished smooth by centuries of desperate people standing exactly where he stood.

"Here is my counteroffer," Sebastian said.

***THE VESSEL DOES NOT COUNTEROFFER WITH THE COVENANT.***

"First time for everything." He knelt beside the keystone of blood and set his palm flat against it. It beat against his skin like a second pulse, eager, hungry, already reaching for the life inside him the way a leech reaches for warmth. "You want a life bound to the magic. Freely given — that part matters to you, doesn't it? Even your ugly old terms require consent. Cassian never understood that. He thought he could *take* the vessel, force the binding, but the stones wouldn't sing for him. They've been waiting for someone to say yes."

***YES,*** said the covenant, with something that in a smaller creature might have been relief.

"But you said a *life*," Sebastian continued. "You said blood. You said the whole of it, all at once, forever, irrevocable. And I'm telling you those terms are bad drafting." He heard himself, heard the clockmaker's apprentice talking contract law to an entity older than language, and a wild laugh nearly escaped him. Somewhere in the back of his mind, Wren's voice said, *you absolute idiot, negotiate later, bleed less,* and he loved her for it even though she wasn't there. "A life is not the only thing a person has. It isn't even the most valuable thing. Ask anyone who's sold theirs."

Behind him, Gideon went very still.

"A memory," Sebastian said. "I offer memories. Mine. Freely given. Not taken, not harvested, not extracted from some hollow-eyed servant in your courts — offered, the way you'd hand someone bread, the way you'd tell a story by a fire because you *wanted* to share it. That's the amendment. That's the new term. Take my past instead of my future. Take what I remember instead of whether I wake up tomorrow."

***THE COVENANT IS WRITTEN IN BLOOD.***

"The covenant was written by people who hadn't thought of anything better," Sebastian said. "I have. Amend it."

The silence that followed lasted three heartbeats. In it, Sebastian felt every memory he owned rise up inside him like water finding its level — the clocktower, the smell of oil and brass, Master Aldous's cough and his terrible singing, the festival lanterns, the night the stars went out, Gideon's crooked grin in the archive-dark, Wren's fierce thin arms around his neck on the mountain, the taste of stolen apples, the first time Gideon laughed at something he said and looked almost surprised to be doing it—

All of it. He could feel the covenant weighing all of it.

And underneath the fear — and there was fear, cold and enormous, because he knew what he was about to lose — underneath it was a strange, stubborn calm. Because this, at least, was *his*. No one had made him for this. No mural had painted him here. This was the one choice in his entire manufactured existence that belonged to him alone.

"You should know," he said, and now he did turn, because he wanted Gideon's face to be the last thing the old Sebastian saw, "that I've thought about this since the Spire. Since I understood what 'the vessel' meant. I spent weeks being furious about it — at you, at the covenant, at the entire universe's habit of deciding things about me without asking. And somewhere in all that fury I realized something." He smiled, and meant it. "They were all wrong. Not about the sacrifice. About the shape of it. Everyone assumed the price had to be paid *to* the magic. But magic isn't a merchant, Gideon. It's a mirror. It becomes what we bring to it. Two hundred years of blood made it cruel. So we'll bring it something else."

"Sebastian." Gideon's voice cracked. They were trying to sit up, failing, hating it. "There are other ways. I told you — I've been looking — there has to be—"

"There isn't. We both know there isn't. You've been looking for a way around this since before I even knew what I was, and I love you for it, and it doesn't exist." He said it plainly, without drama, because Gideon deserved plainness after all their careful lies. "But here's the thing you keep missing, thief. You taught it to me without meaning to. Every memory you ever sold — you survived it. It cost you, God knows it cost you, pieces of yourself I'll spend the rest of my life helping you mourn. But you *survived*. Memory isn't life. Losing it hurts, but it doesn't stop the heart." He knelt and pressed his forehead briefly to Gideon's. "So let me pay in the coin I can afford. Let me keep the heart."

"If you do this," Gideon whispered, "you won't know me. You won't know any of it. You'll wake up and I'll be a stranger—"

"Then I'll get to meet you again," Sebastian said. "Honestly? There are worse fates than falling in love with you twice."

And before Gideon could answer — before either of them could ruin the moment with more argument, which they absolutely would have, they were both far too stubborn for their own good — Sebastian turned back to the keystone, opened every door inside himself at once, and gave.

* * *

It did not hurt.

That surprised him most. He had braced for tearing, for burning, for the sensation of being emptied out. Instead it felt like exhaling after holding his breath for years. The memories left him not ripped away but *released*, each one slipping free like a bird from an open hand, streaming into the keystone in ribbons of light — the tower first, then the valley, then the cradle-woman whose face he barely remembered anyway, then the road, the Spire, the drowned city, Wren laughing with an apple in her cheek, Gideon saying *don't ask questions you can't afford the answers to* and then answering every question he ever asked anyway—

He gave the fear too. The night the stars went out. The riders. The wards shattering like sugar-glass. All of it, freely, because a gift half-meant was no gift at all, and if the magic was going to learn a new language it needed to hear the whole sentence.

The keystone of blood drank it in.

For one endless moment nothing happened. The throne room hung silent, dust drifting through light that came from nowhere, Gideon's ragged breathing the only sound in the world. Sebastian knelt with empty hands and an emptier head, aware distantly that he could no longer remember why he was kneeling, only that it mattered, only that the person behind him mattered, only that—

Then the covenant spoke again, and everything had changed.

***AMENDED,*** it said.

Not thunderously. Almost softly. Like a very old clerk discovering, after two centuries of the same ledger, a new column worth keeping.

***THE OFFERING WAS GIVEN, NOT TAKEN. THE TERMS ARE... SUFFICIENT.***

"Sufficient," Sebastian repeated. His own voice sounded strange to him — familiar and foreign at once, like hearing a recording of himself. "Two realms realign, and I get *sufficient*."

***THE COVENANT WILL STUDY THE AMENDMENT,*** said the ancient voice, and if Sebastian hadn't known better he'd have sworn it sounded embarrassed. ***MAGIC DIMS WHERE IT WAS HOARDED. MAGIC FREES WHERE IT WAS GIVEN. THIS IS... NEW.***

"Yeah," Sebastian said. "Welcome to the rest of your existence."

The light began.

It rose out of the three keystones together — blue-white from the water-stone, deep gold from the stone-stone, and from the keystone of blood a color Sebastian had no name for, warm and shifting, the color of a story told aloud, of a memory shared on purpose. It poured upward through the broken ceiling of Emberhold, through the ash-choked sky, and Sebastian felt it go out across the world like a tide reversing: the wards of Hollowmere reweaving themselves — differently now, openly, gates instead of walls; the drowned city of Mirrormere settling into true rest, its ghosts bowing as they faded; the Shattered Spire finally finishing its collapse, gently, the way a tired soldier lays down arms. Far away, he somehow knew, stars were kindling back into the sky. Dimmer than before. Present. Honest.

And Gideon breathed.

It was not dramatic. That was the miracle of it — no blaze of healing light, no gasping resurrection. Gideon simply took a breath, and then another, and the wound in their chest closed the way a river closes over a thrown stone, and the gray went out of their face, and they blinked up at the ceiling like someone waking from a nap they'd fought the entire way.

"Gideon," Sebastian said.

Gideon's eyes found him. Focused. Widened.

"Oh no," Gideon said hoarsely. "Oh no, no, no. Your face. You don't—" They struggled upright, wincing, grabbing his shoulders, searching his eyes with mounting horror. "Say something. Say my name. Say *anything*—"

"Gideon Delacroix," Sebastian said slowly. The name fit in his mouth like a key in a lock he couldn't remember installing. "I know your name. I don't know..." He frowned, groping. "I don't know why I know your name. I don't know why I'm kneeling in a ruined throne room. I don't know why you're crying, or why that makes my chest hurt, or why I appear to be holding your hand when I have absolutely no memory of taking it."

"Because you gave them away," Gideon said. The tears were falling freely now, and they weren't fighting them. "Your memories. All of them. To save the realms. To save *me*, you impossible, reckless, magnificent idiot—"

"Hm." Sebastian considered this. Considered the stranger in front of him — sharp-eyed, bloodstained, beautiful in a disreputable sort of way, crying over him as though he were worth crying over. Considered the fact that his chest ached with something enormous and nameless, grief and recognition tangled together, the ghost-shape of a life he could no longer see. "Well," he said at last. "I seem to have excellent taste in strangers."

Gideon laughed — a wet, broken, astonished sound — and pulled him into a hug that smelled of blood and ash and, beneath it, something Sebastian's mind insisted was *home* even though home was just a word to him now.

"We'll fix it," Gideon whispered into his shoulder, fierce as an oath. "Whatever it takes. I stole memories for a living, Sebastian Vale. Returning them is going to be the easiest work of my life."

"Sebastian Vale," he repeated, testing it. It fit too. Most things, he suspected, would fit, if this person was the one handing them to him. "That's my name?"

"That's your name." Gideon pulled back, and their smile was trembling and radiant and utterly untrustworthy, and Sebastian found he liked it immediately and completely. "And I'm going to spend as long as it takes earning the right to use it."

Above them, through the broken crown of Emberhold, the first returning stars pricked through the dark — dim, stubborn, real.

And somewhere below, in the wreckage of the throne, a small sound: a girl's voice, ragged with smoke and triumph, calling up through the corridors —

*"Oi! Are you two done saving the world? There's a whole city out here that needs telling!"*

Sebastian didn't know her. He was almost certain he was going to love her.

He got up, and helped Gideon stand, and went to find out.
