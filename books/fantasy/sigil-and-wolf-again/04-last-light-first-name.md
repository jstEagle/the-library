## 4. Last Light, First Name

The throne room burned the way a held breath burns—slowly, from the inside out.

Silas stood at the foot of the dais with ash drifting around her like grey snow that had forgotten which way was down. The fires did not consume the pillars; they lived in them, coiling through the stone as though the mountain itself had caught the child's fever. Above, on a throne of black iron and older bone, King Aldric sat unmoving, and upon his head the crown drank.

She could see it drinking now. She had not been able to see it before—the way light bent wrong around its seven points, the way each spire ended in a mouth too small to see and too deep to measure. It fed in silence, and the king's face above it was smooth as an unsent letter, all the writing scraped away.

*Like mine*, she thought. *Like what I made of myself.*

The child stood beside her, small hand buried in the fold of Silas's sleeve, gripping the wool the way she had gripped it the first night at the temple door—mud-soaked, wordless, speaking a language older than any kingdom that had ever taxed a valley. The fire under her skin flickered visibly now, veins of gold showing through dirt-streaked skin like cracks in a kiln.

"They're coming," Brannoc said.

He had planted himself between them and the great doors, sword drawn, and even disgraced—even stripped of his wolf-mantle and his order's name—he filled the doorway's frame of shadow the way a mountain fills a pass. Beyond him, down the corridor, boots were striking marble in unison. The thralls. Courtiers and guardsmen whose eyes had gone the color of candle-flame, whose wills the crown had sipped at for years, little by little, keeping them just alive enough to serve.

"How many?" Silas asked.

"Enough." He rolled his shoulders, and there was something old and glad in the movement, something that remembered running under a different moon. "Keep the girl behind you. Whatever you hear—whatever you hear me become—do not stop the work."

"Brannoc—"

"I remember the old king," he said quietly. "I remember what he was before. If any part of Aldric can be woken, it's because someone once loved this kingdom enough to bind rather than kill." His yellow eyes found hers. "You wrote mercy into that ritual, scribe. Finish reading it."

The doors burst open.

Silas turned to the dais and did not look back.

***

The ritual was not written in ink. She understood that now, standing close enough to feel the crown's hunger like heat off a forge. It was written in names—and she had been its final author, which meant she was its final page.

The binding required two voices: one to hold the parasite still, and one to speak the vessel free. The first voice had been hers, decades ago, when she was not Silas Vasquez, debt-bound scribe, but someone with a name that had cost too much to keep. She had erased that name from every record, every ledger, every memory she could reach with temple craft—and in doing so, she had torn the ritual's second half out of the world.

But erasure was never destruction. She had learned that in the archives, copying records for men who believed that burning a page destroyed what was written on it. Ink soaked through. Words left impressions in the vellum beneath. And a name spoken aloud by its own mouth—

Her hands shook. Not from fear. From recognition.

The thralls poured through the doors, and the sound of Brannoc meeting them was like weather—a low growl that became a roar that became something with too many teeth to be a man's voice. Steel rang. Someone screamed. Silas kept her eyes on the crown.

"I need your name," she said to the child, kneeling so they were eye to eye. In the dragon-tongue, the words came easier now, though they tasted of smoke: *Give me what is yours to give.*

The child studied her with those ember-bright eyes, and for a long moment said nothing. Then she raised one small hand—not to offer, Silas realized with a lurch, but to *ask*. Her fingers traced a sigil against the air, the same sigil Silas had found scratched into a sealed archive folio twenty years ago and copied without understanding: *willingly given, freely received, or the fire turns.*

"You choose me," Silas whispered. "Not the crown. Not the ritual. Me."

The child nodded once, solemn as a coronation, and pressed her burning palm flat over Silas's heart.

Fire entered her—but gently, the way dawn enters a window. It did not burn. It *illuminated*. And in that light, Silas saw everything she had buried: a young woman in a scriptorium that no longer existed, ink to her elbows, laughing with a knight who would later lose everything for remembering. A name being written into a great ritual circle by her own hand. The cost laid bare before her—the binding would take the binder's name, unravel her from every record, every mind, every mirror—and her choosing it anyway, because the alternative was letting the crown finish devouring the last true king.

She had not run from guilt. She had run from grief, and called it peace, and paid forty years of silence for the lie.

The name rose in her like water finding an old channel.

She opened her mouth and spoke it.

***

The world *remembered*.

It came back all at once—every record rewriting itself in some archive beyond archives, every erased line reappearing in its own hand, every person who had ever known her feeling a door open in their memory that they hadn't known was shut. Silas felt herself become legible again, felt her history snap back onto her bones like a spine straightening, and she wept without shame for the woman she had been, because the woman she had been deserved tears, and tears required a name, and she had one now.

*"I AM THE SECOND VOICE,"* she said, and the throne room rang with it, and the crown shrieked.

It was not a sound any throat could make. It was the sound of a parasite discovering its meal had teeth. The seven spires flared, dragging at the king, at the thralls, at the child—but Silas stepped forward, blazing with borrowed fire willingly given, and spoke the binding's second half into the air between them.

Words she had written. Words she had paid for. Words that meant *you are held, you are seen, you are finished.*

Brannoc went down under three thralls and rose as something larger, grey-furred and terrible, scattering them like leaves—but she heard him laugh, a rough human laugh inside the wolf-shape, because he was fighting for something again instead of merely surviving.

The crown tore free of Aldric's head.

It lifted into the air, writhing, feeding on nothing, guttering like a lamp out of oil—and Silas watched it die the way all hungry things die: furious, then frantic, then small. It hit the flagstones with a sound like a bell cracking and lay still, just metal now, just seven cold points of dead iron.

Aldric breathed.

It was a terrible breath, ragged and long, the breath of a man surfacing after drowning for years. His eyes opened—brown, ordinary, exhausted beyond all measuring—and found her.

"You," he rasped. "I knew a voice like yours. Before."

"Yes," Silas said. "You did."

He looked past her at the wolf standing guard over a small fire-lit child, and something in the ruined king's face cracked open—not into madness, but into grief, clean and human. He wept like a man who has just learned how many years he lost, and the whole court, waking by degrees around them, began to weep with him.

***

Later—hours later, when the fires had settled into embers and the embers into warmth—Silas sat on the steps of the dais with the child asleep against her shoulder and Brannoc sprawled beside her, human-shaped again, bruised and grinning at the ceiling.

"The fire," she said quietly. She could feel it in the girl, banked now, patient. "It's not going back to sleep. Dragons don't sleep forever."

"No," Brannoc agreed. "They wake."

"And when she wakes fully—" Silas looked down at the small weight against her, this child she had known four weeks and would burn down kingdoms for, "—someone will have to teach her what her own flame is for."

Brannoc turned his head. His yellow eyes were warm.

"I know a valley," he said, "that takes in strays."

Silas laughed—really laughed, for the first time in forty years, with a name to hang the sound on. Outside, dawn was coming up over a kingdom learning how to breathe. Somewhere far north, she was almost certain, something ancient felt a dragon-fire settle into new hands and turned its attention toward the south.

Let it look, she thought, tightening her arm around the sleeping child. We are not hiding anymore.
