## 9. Coronation of Ash

**FELIX**

The bells began at dawn, and by noon the whole city of Vell had been funneled into Coronation Square like grain into a millstone.

Felix stood in the crowd with the map pressed flat against his chest beneath his shirt, inked onto skin that had stopped feeling like his own three days ago. Ten thousand people packed shoulder to shoulder, all of them craning toward the dais where King Aldric would appear to mark fifteen years on the throne. Fifteen years. The number sat in Felix's stomach like a swallowed stone.

*You did that,* said a voice in his head that sounded like Sera. *You opened the door.*

*I was twelve,* he answered it. *I didn't know.*

*Nobody ever knows. That's what makes it theft.*

He scanned the square for her and found nothing — which was the plan, but plans were cold comfort when every witch-finder's grey cloak looked like an executioner's hood. Somewhere up there, on the balconies ringing the square, Sera Vane was walking into the one place she'd sworn never to return: the Inquisition's own roost, carrying evidence that would get her burned twice over.

And somewhere below, in the undercity, Dane was doing exactly what Mother Quill had ordered.

The trumpets sounded. The crowd hushed. And Aldric came out onto the dais.

From this distance he looked like any king — tall, silver-crowned, robed in white and gold. But Felix had learned to read people the way other thieves read locks, and what he saw made his blood go thin. The king moved like a marionette testing its strings. His smile arrived a half-second after his cheeks lifted. And when he raised his hand to bless the crowd, ten thousand people sighed as one breath —

— and Felix felt it.

A tug. Faint, like a hook set behind his eyes. The sigil-map on his chest flared hot enough to scald, and around him the crowd swayed, faces going soft and dreamy, mouths parting on little gasps of pleasure. He watched a baker's wife beside him smile at nothing, tears sliding down her face, and he knew — with the certainty of a man who'd spent his life bottling other people's years — that she was being *drained*. Her wedding day, her children's first steps, siphoned up through the air like smoke drawn into a chimney.

The crown wasn't feeding on Aldric anymore.

It was feeding on everyone.

"No," Felix breathed. "No, no — not yet —"

The map burned. He pulled it from under his shirt and the sigils were crawling, rearranging themselves, the final unbinding glyph sliding away from where it should be like a word refusing to be spoken. The entity knew he was here. It had always known. This whole ceremony was a net, and he'd walked into it because there was no other door.

He started pushing through the crowd, toward the dais stairs, and the crowd pushed back — gently, terribly, ten thousand dreamy faces turning toward him with the same mild smile, hands rising like wheat in wind. Not attacking. *Holding.* The entity didn't need violence. It had a city.

Then a scream cut through the haze from the eastern balcony, sharp and human and furious, and Felix knew that voice anywhere.

* * *

**SERA**

Sera stood on the Inquisition balcony with her old badge torn from her coat and the archive ledger open in her hands, and she did the thing she had spent fifteen years failing to do.

She told the truth.

"LOOK AT YOUR KING!" Her voice carried on the amplification charms wired into the balcony rail — charms meant for royal proclamations, turned now against the crown itself. "Look at his eyes! Fifteen years ago Aldric the Just ended a famine, pardoned the border rebels, opened the archives to the guilds! And then something changed him — I have the records, I kept the records, I *burned people* to bury these records —"

Below, the witch-finders on the balcony were turning toward her, iron rods already glowing. Captain Voss, grey-bearded and grey-souled, stepped forward with something like pity in his face.

"Sera Vane. You were the best of us once."

"I was the worst of us," she said. "That's why I know." She held up the ledger, pages fluttering. "Read the entry for the winter of the king's fifth year! Read what we erased! There is a thing wearing Aldric like a coat, and right now it is drinking your memories while you stand here smiling — ask yourselves, any of you, what you had for breakfast! Ask yourselves your mother's name!"

The crowd murmured. Somewhere below, a woman began to cry — not the dreamy weeping of the drained, but the sharp grief of someone who had just reached for a memory and found a hole.

Voss's rod came up. "By authority of the Crown —"

"WHICH CROWN?" Sera shouted. "Yours is eating you!"

And it was — she could see it now, the way the entity's hunger rippled outward through the square in visible shivers, the crowd's collective face slackening by degrees. She had perhaps a minute before Voss decided duty outweighed doubt. A minute was enough. A minute was all Felix needed her to buy.

She dropped the ledger, drew the broken inquisitor's blade she'd carried since her disgrace, and put her back to the balcony rail.

"Come on, then," she told Voss. "Burn me properly this time."

* * *

**FELIX**

The crowd's grip broke when Sera's voice cracked it — doubt moving through ten thousand minds like acid through ice, and in the cracks, Felix ran.

Up the dais steps two at a time. Royal guards turned, and he braced himself to fight men who were only half-awake — but they weren't guards anymore, their eyes gone milk-pale, and they moved with the entity's smooth wrongness, four of them converging with blades drawn in perfect unison.

Then the flagstones erupted.

Dane came up out of the undercity access like something exhumed, guild-knives in both fists, and took the first guard's legs out with a sweep that would have been beautiful if Felix hadn't known it was meant for him next.

"Felix." Dane's voice was flat as slate. "Walk away. Last kindness I've got."

"You're feeding them to it." Felix backed up the steps. "Your own people. Mother Quill's been shipping memories upstairs for years and you *knew* —"

"I knew we ate or starved!" Dane's face finally cracked, and underneath the slate was the boy who'd shared stolen bread with Felix in a cellar that smelled of lamp oil and rain. "The city was dying anyway, Felix. At least we chose who got eaten."

"That's not choosing. That's just being the knife instead of the throat."

Dane came at him.

They fought up the dais through the collapsing ceremony — Felix with a stolen fruit knife against his brother's guild steel, taking a gash along the ribs, giving back a cut across Dane's forearm, neither of them fighting to kill and both of them knowing it. Around them the square was coming apart: witch-finders streaming toward Sera's balcony, crowds scattering and then standing frozen as the entity redoubled its pull, the air itself thickening like the inside of a bottle.

"Please," Dane said, knife trembling. "Don't make me be the one."

"You won't be." Felix caught Dane's wrist, twisted, and drove his elbow into the old scar on his brother's jaw — the one Felix himself had given him at fourteen, the one Dane could never quite guard. Dane went down hard, and Felix knelt, pressed his forehead briefly to his brother's.

"Finish the work," Felix said. "Or don't. But stop holding the door."

He left Dane on the steps and climbed toward the king.

* * *

**SERA**

They didn't burn her. That was the surprise of her life.

Voss raised his rod — and hesitated — and in the hesitation, his own memory betrayed him. Sera watched it happen: the captain reaching for the moment he'd sworn his oath, the day he'd joined the Inquisition full of purpose, and finding only fog. His face went through confusion, horror, and something worse — recognition.

"It's true," Voss said hoarsely. "Gods. It's been *eating us.*" He turned his rod, not on Sera but on the dais, on the silver-crowned figure standing rigid above the chaos. "Inquisition! The warrant stands — but the traitor is on the throne!"

What followed was not a battle so much as a dam breaking. Half the witch-finders turned on their own doctrine; the other half, deeper-drained than they knew, kept advancing with empty smiles. Sera leapt from the balcony into the fray with her broken blade and fifteen years of guilt sharpened to a point, cutting a path not toward the throne —

— but toward the boy climbing it.

Because she'd read the rite. She knew what the last sigil required. And she knew, with a sick lurch, that Felix had known too, and had said goodbye to her last night without saying the words, pressing an extra ration of bread into her pack like a man settling accounts.

"No," she snarled, carving through the melee. "No, you don't get to do this alone —"

* * *

**FELIX**

Aldric — the thing in Aldric — was waiting for him at the top of the dais, and it wore Felix's childhood voice like it had never taken it off.

"You came back," it said, with the king's mouth, in the voice of a twelve-year-old boy counting coins in a cellar. "I knew you would. You always come back for what's yours."

"Give him back." Felix stood at the edge of the final sigil, the map blazing on his chest, the crawling glyphs finally locking into place — because the entity couldn't stop the rite, only make him pay for it. "The king. My mentor's memories. All of it."

"I gave you my offer." The entity tilted Aldric's head, and behind the royal eyes something vast turned over, patient as deep water. "Your mentor, alive. Your brother beside you. Your whole self, kept. All you have to do is keep the door shut. Keep *me*. You've fed me fifteen years, little thief. Don't you want to know what you'll be when I'm done?"

"I know what I am." Felix thought of the widow whose grief he'd bottled, of Sera's confession in the dark, of Dane's cracking voice, of a mentor who chose ash over service. "I'm the one who opened the door. So I'm the one closing it."

He pressed his palm to the sigil.

The world tore.

Not the square — *him*. The entity poured up through the connection like floodwater through a broken lock, and it went for everything. His mother's face, gone. The cellar, the lamp oil, the rain. His first theft, his first sale, the taste of stolen bread with Dane, the sound of Sera saying *I burned him* — the thing ripped through his history with claws made of hunger, tearing pages out of the book of him, and Felix held on because there was nothing left to hold with except the choice itself.

*I remember that I choose this,* he thought, as his own name began to blur. *Whatever else goes. I remember that I chose.*

Through the tearing, dimly, he heard screaming — Sera, close now, fighting toward him, shouting his name, shouting it again, and he wanted to answer but he was losing the shape of the sounds. The king beneath him convulsed. The crown blazed white. Across the square, ten thousand minds snapped taut like cut ropes as the feed reversed, memories pouring home in a great silent flood.

The last thing Felix Yarrow knew — the very last, a single ember cupped in his hands as the flood took everything else — was a woman's voice, raw and relentless, telling him:

*Hold on. I'll remember. I'll remember for both of us.*

Then the ember went out, and the man on the dais opened his eyes on a world with no past in it at all.
