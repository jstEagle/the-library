## 8. Two Prisoners, One Throne

The feast was still roaring two floors above when Ines came up the servants' stair with her knife drawn and her heart hammering like a siege drum.

She had killed men in throne rooms before. Twice, actually — once for coin, once because the man on the chair had burned her employer's village and no one else would do the work. Both times her hands had been steady. Both times she'd told herself the same lie: *this is just a job.*

She wasn't telling herself anything now. She couldn't afford to. Odile's map of the crown-ward was inked into her memory in Odile's precise, anxious hand: *the binding anchors at the crown itself, but the seams run through the eyes, the throat, the hands. If you must strike, strike nothing above the collarbone.* And beneath that, smaller, almost pleading: *Ines. Look at his eyes first.*

Bramwell was somewhere below, in chains or worse, having bought them this corridor with his surrender. Tam and Odile were at the ward-anchor in the undercroft, keeping the god's broken sigil warm against Tam's chest, waiting for her signal. The plan was simple. Simple plans were the ones that got people killed.

The doors to the throne room were oak and iron, tall as three men, banded with the king's sigil — the old sigil, the silver drowned-star she'd carried across half a country. Someone had tried to pry it off the doors recently; the gouges were fresh, and around them the wood was scorched black, as if the metal had fought back.

Ines pressed her palm flat against the door.

Warm. The whole door was warm, like a body.

*Come in,* said a voice that was not a voice, threading up through her bones the way it had in the drowned shrine, the way it had every night since, faint as a pulse under snow. Her god. Ten years silent, and now it spoke to her more often than it had ever spoken in her childhood, and always in fragments, always like something shouting through water.

*I'm coming,* she answered, though she didn't know if the dead sigil could hear her. She pushed the doors open.

The throne room was empty of court. That was the first wrongness. A victory feast raged overhead — she could hear the fiddles, the drunken singing, the crash of overturned cups — but here there was only candlelight and cold air and one man standing at the window, looking out over a city he had not yet finished burning.

King Aldric. Or the thing wearing King Aldric.

He was not what she expected. She had built him out of ten years of rumor: a tyrant vast and terrible, crowned in shadow, his armies marching under banners that made grown soldiers weep. What stood at the window was a lean man in black and gold, graying at the temples, one hand braced against the glass as if he needed it to stay upright. His crown sat crooked. Actually crooked — tilted, as though it didn't quite fit the head beneath it anymore.

"You're the mercenary," he said, without turning. "From the valley."

Her knife hand tightened. "You know me?"

"I know *of* you." He turned then, and Ines saw his eyes, and everything in her went cold and hot at once.

Odile had said *look at his eyes first*, and Ines understood why. They were wrong in a way that was hard to name — both of them open, both of them seeing, but *two* of them, two gazes layered in the same sockets like reflections in deep water. Behind the usurper's cold amber stare, something else looked out. Something drowning.

"Ten years," the mouth said, smooth and amused. "Ten years I wore this crown and built an empire, and the valley sends me one mercenary, a mad scholar, a deserter, and a dead boy." He smiled. It was Aldric's face doing it, and it was not. "I confess I expected more."

"I'm enough." Ines moved left, circling, keeping the candles between them. Every instinct screamed at her to close the distance and put the knife in his throat — *strike nothing above the collarbone* — and the war between those two truths made her voice come out rougher than she meant. "Where is he? The real king."

The usurper laughed. "The real king. You say it as if there's meat left on that bone."

And then the laugh broke.

It happened between one breath and the next — the smile splitting down the middle like ice on a thawing river, the amber eyes rolling back, the whole body jerking as if yanked by strings from inside. When the head came up again, the second gaze was on top, and the man behind it was *screaming*. No sound came out. That was the worst part. His mouth opened wide and wide and nothing emerged except a thin whistle of air, and his hands flew to his own face, clawing at his cheeks as if trying to tear the other presence off him like a mask.

"*Please,*" the mouth ground out, in a different voice — lower, cracked, human, a voice that had been screaming without sound for ten years. "Please. Can you hear me. Can anyone — I am here. I have *always been here.*"

Ines did not remember crossing the room. One moment she was circling; the next she was three feet away with her knife low and her free hand extended toward him like a fool, like someone offering help to a fire.

"Aldric," she said. "Aldric. Look at me."

His eyes found hers. Both layers of them. The drowning thing behind the amber stared at her, and through the contact — skin, warmth, the shattered sigil humming in its wrapping against her ribs — the god's voice rose through her spine like floodwater.

*Two prisoners,* it said, grief-stricken, fragmented. *One throne. One crown. We are — we were — we* ***agreed*** *—*

And the vision took her.

* * *

She stood in a hall of white stone on a night that existed only in memory, and she watched a younger Aldric kneel before a circle of robed figures while a crown of silver was lowered onto his head. She knew this. Tam had shown them this, through his drowned memories, on the road — but seeing it from *inside* was different. Seeing it from inside was like being flayed and remade.

*Take it willingly,* the priests had chanted, *and it cannot be taken from you.*

*For my people,* young Aldric had whispered, tears cutting through the dust on his face. *Let them keep their sky. Let them keep their stars. Take me instead.*

And the god had poured into him like light into a lamp — and for nine days, nine *days*, king and god had shared one body in harmony, holding back the dark together —

— until the ninth night, when the third presence had come. The thing that had waited in the crown's making, smuggled in by a priest whose face Ines could not see even in memory, wrapped in shadow. It had slid into the binding like a blade into a gap in armor, and it had locked the doors from the inside, and Aldric had gone screaming into a cell built of his own flesh while the usurper put on his life like a coat.

Ten years. Ten years of watching his own hands sign death warrants. Ten years of feeling his own voice call himself a tyrant. Ten years of the god pressed against him in the dark, both of them screaming where no one could hear.

The vision released her and Ines staggered, caught herself on the arm of the throne, and realized she was crying.

"That's not true," she said hoarsely. "That can't — you let him do it. You let him burn—"

*"Did I?"* The usurper's voice again, silk over rot, and he straightened, shaking off the moment of weakness like a horse shivering off flies. He descended one step toward her. "The boy remembers drowning. The scholar reads her dead wards. But you, mercenary — you believe what you see. So look. Look at your king's hands." He raised them, elegant, ringed. "Whose blood is this, truly? Does it matter? The world will only ever see one set of hands."

"It matters," Ines snarled, and her fury came up like a wall between her and the pity, and she was grateful for it. "It matters because I've spent ten years praying to a god I thought had abandoned us, and it turns out it was chained in *here* the whole time, listening to you use its silence as proof it never loved us. It matters because a boy drowned under your binding ritual and remembers every second of it. It matters because my friend is in your dungeons right now because of you." Her knife came up. "*Everything* you've done matters."

"Then kill me," the usurper said softly.

Behind his eyes, Aldric screamed *no* — she saw it, felt it through the thread of the god, felt the trapped king lunge against his cage like a man throwing himself at a locked door. But the usurper spread his arms, crown tilting, smiling with Aldric's mouth.

"Strike true, mercenary. Above the collarbone, yes? Your scholar's little map. Kill the body and the binding dies with it — and so does your god, and so does your precious king, and so does any hope of unmaking what I have built. Or lower your knife, and walk away, and live long enough to watch my armies take the free cities one by one." He tilted his head. "There is no move on your board that saves anyone. I made sure of it, ten years ago."

"You're lying."

"About which part?"

"The part where there's no way out." Ines backed toward the doors, knife still up, mind racing. *The sigil can be reforged. Odile said. From a memory freely given. That was always the way — that was the whole point of carrying it here.* "Because there is a way out. You just don't know about it yet."

Something flickered across the layered eyes — the first honest uncertainty she'd seen in that face. "What way?"

"The kind that doesn't need your permission." She reached the doors. Below, faintly, she felt rather than heard it: a hum through the stones, the sigil waking, Tam calling her name through the bond they'd all stopped pretending they couldn't feel. "Keep screaming, Aldric. Both of you. Hold on. We're coming to cut you loose."

The usurper lunged for her — fast, far too fast for a man his age — and Ines slammed the doors between them and ran, taking the stairs three at a time, the god's voice rising in her chest like a tide:

*Hurry,* it said. *He is tired. He has been tired for ten years. Hurry, before he decides the dark is quieter than the fighting.*

Behind her, muffled through oak and iron, she heard the throne room doors burst open, and footsteps — unhurried, certain, crowned — begin to follow her down.
