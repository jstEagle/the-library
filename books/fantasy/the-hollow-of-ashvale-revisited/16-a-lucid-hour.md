## 16. A Lucid Hour

*The daughter's laugh. Keep it. Keep it.*

He keeps it the way a starving man keeps a crust in his fist — not to eat, not yet, but to know it is there.

Aldric does not know how long he has been inside himself. That is the first theft, and in some ways the deepest: time went first, taken whole, like a thief lifting an entire purse rather than picking coins from it one by one. He remembers being a king. He remembers rain on the orchard at Vellamere, the year the apples came in crooked and sweet. He remembers his wife's hands, though her face has gone soft as wet paper. He remembers that he had a daughter.

He does not remember her name.

That is the cruelty of it. The thing that wears him lets him keep the shape of his love while scooping out everything that made the love specific. It is a careful eater. It leaves the bones of a man standing so the clothes still fit.

*A laugh,* he tells himself. *High and sudden, like a bird startled off water. She laughed with her whole chest even when she was small. She snorted when she was trying not to.*

He holds that. He turns it over. Somewhere beneath the crown — beneath the thing that is not a crown but wears one, the way a hand wears a glove it has grown into — somewhere down in the flooded cellar of himself, there is a room where that laugh lives, and he has bricked up the door, and he checks the bricks.

It notices when he checks the bricks.

*There you are,* says the attention, warm as a hearth, cold as a well. *What are you counting, little king?*

*Nothing,* Aldric says. *Inventory.*

It likes that word. Inventory. It thinks he means the ledgers, the taxes, the dull arithmetic of rule. Let it think so. A king survives by letting things think what they wish about him. His father taught him that, or someone taught his father; the lesson has come down to him worn smooth, without its handle.

* * *

Here is how it works now, in the country of one man:

Every true memory is a coin. Not all memories — the false ones are worthless, the ones the Hollow mints by the thousand to pad his purse: coronations that blur into other coronations, banquets where every face is the same face, decrees signed in ink that was never wet. Those are paper. Counterfeit. The Hollow makes them and spends them through him and calls them his reign.

But the true ones — the laugh, the rain, the smell of his wife's rosemary oil in her hair after she rode — those are gold. Small, heavy, real. And every day the Hollow comes to collect.

It does not take by force. That is the horror no physician has guessed, sitting at their comfortable distance, murmuring *melancholia, melancholia* over their wine. It takes by consent, or something shaped like consent. It offers him lucidity — one hour, sometimes two, of clear sky inside his own skull — and the price is a coin. Pay the laugh, walk freely for an hour. Pay the rain on the orchard, remember who you are until dusk. Refuse, and the fog closes in, and he becomes a puppet with its strings run up through the crown, smiling at petitioners, signing deaths, calling servants by names that belong to the dead.

He has learned to bargain. That shames him more than anything else. A king should not bargain with the thing that eats him. But a king who will not bargain feeds it everything at once, and then there is nothing left to save anyone with.

So he hoards. He rations. He spends like a miser in reverse — paying out only enough to stay upright, keeping the last coins buried deep, telling himself there will be a use for them, there must be a use for them, a king's final treasury cannot be spent on mere walking.

* * *

Today the collector comes early.

*You have a guest tomorrow,* it says, and the voice is honey poured over iron. It speaks inside him, which means everywhere. *A taster. A curious one. His blood is interesting.*

*Taster,* Aldric repeats. The word arrives from far away, down a long corridor of himself. *I have tasters.*

*Not like this one.* A pause, and he feels it savoring something, the way he once savored the first plum of summer. *His grandmother had clever hands. You would not remember. I do.*

Something in him goes very still. Grandmother. Clever hands. There is a stone in the flooded cellar of him with words cut into it, and he cannot read the words, but he knows the stone is there because it hurts to stand near it.

*Why tell me?* he asks. *You never tell me anything.*

*Because your fear seasons you,* it says pleasantly. *And because it changes nothing. You are a cupboard, little king. Cupboards do not warn the bread.*

Then it withdraws, taking with it the hour he did not agree to pay for, and the fog rolls in across the fields of him, and Aldric — whoever Aldric is this week — smiles at the wall and waits for the next petitioner.

But down in the cellar, behind the bricked door, the coins shift in the dark. Counting themselves. Holding their breath.

* * *

He gets an hour at dusk. He pays for it — he cannot see what he pays, only feels the purse grow lighter, feels some small bright fact go out of him like a candle pinched between wet fingers. He will grieve it later without knowing what he lost. That is the arrangement. Grief without object. Mourning as weather.

One hour of clear sky.

He uses it the way he always does now: fast, ruthless, kneeling in the wrecked treasury of himself, taking inventory before the fog returns.

*Name. My name is Aldric. Second of that name, no — third. Father was Aldric too. No. Father was — * gone, spent, months ago, a whole man traded away for a single afternoon of clarity during the harvest festival, and worth it, God forgive him, worth it, because for one afternoon he had stood in the orchard and felt the sun and known he was king and known why that mattered.

*Wife. Wife's name — * gone. Her face — gone. Her hands remain, and the rosemary, and the way she said his name when he was being a fool, two syllables with a blade hidden in the second one. He guards those fragments like relics. They are all he has of her, and he suspects — he has begun to suspect — that the Hollow saved them deliberately, kept them fresh the way a cook keeps a garnish, because a husband's love for his wife is a rich flavor and it does not keep if the host stops feeling it.

*Daughter.* The name is gone. The laugh remains. The snort. The way she ran — he can see it, he can SEE it, a small figure tearing down the orchard rows with her arms out like wings, and the apples crooked and sweet, and the rain coming on gray from the west, and she was shouting something, shouting his —

*Papa.*

No. That word is counterfeit. He knows it is counterfeit because it is too smooth, too available, the way the Hollow's memories are always available. The true ones are hard-won; they cost effort to reach, like stones at the bottom of a well. This one floats. It is bait. If he swallows it, if he accepts *papa* as hers, the Hollow will have a hook in the deepest part of him, and it will be able to reel up anything it wants.

He spits it out. In whatever place a mind spits.

The real memory surfaces slowly, grudgingly, the way truth does. Not *papa*. She called him something else. Something from a game, from a story he told her once — he told her a story once, about a bear, no, about a fox, a fox who — 

Gone. The hour is draining. He can feel the edges going soft.

*Fox,* he insists, gripping it. *She called me the fox. Because of the story. Because I — *

The fog takes the story. Leaves him the word. A coin too worn to read, but he knows it is gold, he KNOWS it, and he buries it with the others.

* * *

Some nights, when the Hollow sleeps — if it sleeps; he has never decided whether it sleeps or merely digests — he walks the corridors of the palace that exist only in him. Memory-stone, the old builders called it. Every wall of Ashvale's palace was raised with it, stone that remembers what happened against it, and the Hollow drinks from the walls the way a tick drinks from a dog. But the walls are vast, and the thing is not infinite, whatever it believes. There are rooms in him it has not entered in years. Dusty rooms. Rooms with children's chalk marks on the doorframes.

In one such room he found, three lucid hours ago — paid for with the sound of bells, and worth it — a mark on a doorframe. Height-lines, cut with a knife. Dates beside them. And a name.

He could not read the name. The letters swam. But the knife-cuts were real, and the smallest line was barely above his knee, and he stood in that room in himself and wept without tears, because he understood: she had been measured here, year after year, and he had held the knife, and he had joked about feeding her more bread, and all of that was true and none of it could he reach anymore except as the shape of a fact.

Facts are cheap. The Hollow leaves facts. It eats the living part — the warmth, the weight, the specific gravity of a moment — and leaves the skeleton of information standing. He knows he loved his wife. Knowing is not loving. Knowing is the receipt for a thing already sold.

That is what he fights for, in the end. Not survival. The receipts.

* * *

Tonight the collector comes again, and this time it lingers.

*Tomorrow,* it says, tasting the word. *The taster comes at midday. And tomorrow night I feed. You may watch.*

*I always watch,* Aldric says. *You make me.*

*Mm. But tomorrow you will want to look away, and you will not be able to, and I want you to know that I know it.* A ripple of pleasure moves through him that is not his pleasure, that has never been his pleasure, that he has spent eleven years learning to distinguish from his own. *Your body will smile at me, little king. Your hands will pour the wine. Your voice will say the blessing. And underneath, you will count your little coins and hate me, and the hating will make the meal sweeter. It always does. Resistance is a spice.*

*Then starve,* Aldric says.

The laughter goes through him like cold water through a net. *Oh, I have eaten kings who fought me. Charlemagne's line, the grey queen in the south, the boy with the poet's tongue. Do you know what they have in common?*

*They lost.*

*They were delicious.* The attention withdraws, satisfied, and takes the light with it.

He kneels in the dark of himself and counts.

The laugh. The rain on the orchard. The rosemary. The fox. The height-marks on the doorframe. The taste of the harvest apple, the first one, stolen, shared — shared with whom? With her, it must have been her, split with a thumbnail, half and half, juice down both their chins. Six coins. Perhaps seven. Perhaps the grief itself is a seventh, the grief with no object, which is at least honest.

Seven coins, and tomorrow night the thing will feed, and feeding nights are bad — feeding nights it roams him like a landlord inspecting property, kicking open doors, checking the bricks.

He needs the coins to survive the inspection. But he is so tired of surviving.

And then, in the last gray moment before sleep-that-is-not-sleep, a thought comes to him that is not a thought the Hollow planted, because it hurts too much and is too small and too stubborn, the way all his true things are small and stubborn:

*A cupboard does not warn the bread.*

No. But a cupboard can choose what falls behind it. A cupboard has cracks. A cupboard has a back panel, and nails, and if a man had one hour of clear sky and a knife-edge of will, he could carve something into the underside of his own mind where the thing does not look, because it never looks down, it never looks at the floor, it thinks the floor is just floor —

A word. One word. Not a name — names it would find, names it would eat. A word that means nothing to it and everything to whoever comes close enough to hear.

He does not have the strength tonight. Tomorrow, perhaps. Tomorrow, while his body pours the wine and smiles its owned smile, while the thing feeds and gloats and does not watch the floor — tomorrow, in the one place left to him, under the armrest of the throne where his hand rests a thousand hours a day and no eye ever follows, he will begin to cut.

Seven coins. He will spend six staying alive long enough to spend the seventh on one carved word.

It is not much. It is not an army, not a rite, not a rescue.

But a king's last treasury is not spent on mere walking.

Down in the dark, behind the bricked door, the coins settle and wait — and far above them, through stone and crown and stolen years, the palace begins to prepare for a midnight meal, and somewhere in the city below, a taster with interesting blood is being taught how to open a door.
