## 20. Ledger Entries, Closed

*From the Master Ledger of the Guild of Impossible Things, Volume CXII, folio 440–452. Recovered in full. Marginalia preserved where legible.*

**ENTRY 9,441 — ACCOUNT: ASHGROVE, JUDE**

*Date of opening:* One hundred years prior to the winter turn.
*Nature of debt:* One memory, whole and unpartitioned, sold against covenant-breaking. Payment received: survival. Interest accrued: one century, compounding.

*Status:* **PAID IN FULL.**

*Method of payment:* See attached addendum. Do not read the attached addendum standing up. Do not read it alone. This is not a warning. It is a courtesy.

*Filed by:* Under-clerk Maren Doss, Third Desk, Reconciliation.

---

*Marginal note, hand unknown, ink browned with age but dated this season:*

He was never supposed to come back to us as a blank page. He was supposed to come back as a debtor. The ledgers do not know what to do with a man who pays everything and keeps nothing. I have been a clerk for forty years and I am telling you plainly: the books are grieving. You will say books cannot grieve. Come sit at my desk during the quiet hours and tell me that again.

— M.D.

*Internal memorandum, Guildmaster's office, circulated to all desks on the morning after the winter turn.*

To all clerks, under-clerks, appraisers, and keepers of the small debts:

You will have heard by now what happened at the First Hearth. Some of you heard it before it happened, because prophecy futures were among our holdings, and I will not pretend otherwise. I signed those trades. My name is on the slips. When you look for someone to blame, start at the top of the stairs and walk down only if you must.

The facts, so far as the Guild records them:

The Ember Court is reforged. The covenant between the mortal realm and the Court stands renewed on terms no longer denominated in lives, years, or names. Warmth, as an asset class, has ceased to exist. Every contract in our vaults predicated on scarcity of fire, of memory, of hope — void. Not breached. Void. There is a difference, and the difference matters, and we will conduct ourselves accordingly.

Effective immediately:

1. All holdings in warmth-futures are struck from the books. Strike them cleanly. No asterisks. We do not keep grudges in the margins; the margins are for truth.
2. All clients who pawned memories to heat their homes in the hard years are hereby released from repurchase obligations. Their memories return to them unbidden or not at all, per the new covenant, but they owe us nothing either way. Process the releases. Do it gently. Many of them sold more than they could afford, and we took it because they could not stop us.
3. The account of Jude Ashgrove is closed. Details follow under seal.

Some of you knew him. He sat at the third desk by the window and balanced your errors without complaint and sharpened your quills when yours went soft. He kept the Ledger of Small Debts, which was the truest book in this building, and none of us told him so while he could hear it. That failure is ours. It goes in the record too. Accountability is not only for guildmasters.

I will sign the closure myself. I know what some of you think of me this morning, and most of what you think is accurate. But there are signatures that cannot be delegated, and this is one of them.

— G. Corvess, Guildmaster (provisional; see Entry 9,443)

**ADDENDUM TO ENTRY 9,441 — METHOD OF PAYMENT**

*Recorded by Under-clerk Maren Doss, who was present at the First Hearth in her capacity as witness-clerk, and who writes the following with a steady hand, mostly.*

At approximately the hour of the ember's guttering, the account holder Jude Ashgrove presented himself at the hearth-stone and tendered payment in the following denomination: one reclaimed memory, complete, comprising the whole of the account holder's forgotten past, including but not limited to —

the night of the covenant-breaking,
the child in the flame,
the bargain struck in the space of six heartbeats,
and every hour of exile between then and now,

— together with such further portions of self as were required to bring the total to equivalence. The hearth accepted. The covenant reforged. The realms did not end. I checked. I checked twice, because I did not believe it, and then I checked a third time because the second time felt like luck rather than fact.

It should be noted for posterity that the account holder had another option. The woman Sera Vane offered herself first. The account holder refused her. He said — and I copy his words exactly, because exactness is the whole of our craft — *"A life isn't currency. It's the thing currency was pretending to be."* Then he stepped forward, and he burned, and he smiled while doing it, which I will be honest, clerks are not trained for. We are trained for griefs that arrive itemized.

What remains of Jude Ashgrove is alive. He walked out of the hearth-light with empty hands and an unlined face and asked what year it was. Sera Vane caught his elbow before any of us could answer, and whatever she told him, she told him kindly, and she has asked — formally, through me — that the specifics of his sacrifice be withheld from him permanently. She has lodged her request in the form of a deposit. She has deposited her own keeping of the story into the Guild vault, designated: *held in trust, never traded, never sold, never appraised.*

We have never held such a deposit before. A memory with a lock on its own price. The vault-keepers say it does not behave like other assets. They say it hums. I have stood next to the shelf and I confirm: it hums.

*Status of addendum:* Closed. Sealed. Signed below.

— M. Doss

*Marginal note, different hand, fresher ink:*

Maren — the humming shelf made three junior clerks cry yesterday. Is this normal? Please advise. Also please advise whether "never appraised" includes curiosity, because mine is becoming a workplace hazard.

— T. Fenwick, Second Desk

*Reply, same page:*

Yes it includes curiosity. Yes it counts as a hazard. File yourself under Small Debts and work it off honestly like the rest of us.

— M.D.

**ENTRY 9,442 — ACCOUNT: VANE, SERA**

*Date of opening:* Fourteen years prior.
*Nature of original debt:* One face — her sister's — sold in a famine winter to buy passage and bread. The Guild has carried this entry in shame since acquisition, though shame is not a recognized line item and appears nowhere in our formal taxonomy. Perhaps it should. Motion tabled.

*Amendments this date:*

The account holder Sera Vane, formerly disgraced ember-warden, formerly exiled, formerly carrying a debt that was never truly hers to carry alone, is hereby restored. Full restoration. Her sister's likeness is returned to her by covenant right, unasked-for, non-negotiable, effective at dawn. If she attempts to refuse the restoration, she may not. The new covenant does not permit people to give away their dead out of guilt. We checked. We checked very carefully, because we suspected she would try.

Further: the account holder is appointed Keeper of Deposited Memory, Ashgrove file, in perpetuity or until she chooses otherwise, whichever she chooses, whichever she needs.

Her warden's rank is restored with back-pay. She will decline the back-pay. She will be overruled.

*Status:* **SETTLED, WITH INTEREST PAID TO THE WRONGED PARTY FOR ONCE.**

*Filed by:* M. Doss, who adds, off the record and therefore on the record because this is the last night anyone can stop me: she held the threshold against the Guildmaster's own key-stone with two broken ribs and did not step back one inch. Write that on the building if you want. Write it on the sky. I am done being neutral about heroes; neutrality was always just fear wearing a clerk's coat.

**ENTRY 9,443 — ACCOUNT: CORVESS, G.**

This entry was drafted four times. Three drafts survive in the wastebasket and one clerk retrieved them, which is a violation, and which I am choosing not to pursue, because reading them taught me something about mercy that ten years of trade never did.

Final draft follows.

*Date of opening:* Thirty-one years prior.
*Nature of holdings:* Prophecy futures, warmth derivatives, scarcity instruments across nine markets, one empire assembled honestly and run ruthlessly.

*Events of settlement:* On the night of the winter turn, the account holder seized the hearth's key-stone with intent to let the last ember die, thereby cornering the market on warmth itself. The intent failed. The instrument — the key-stone — was recovered cracked. The market collapsed within the hour. Every holding became worthless simultaneously, which is the fastest way to learn what your holdings were worth all along.

*Assessment:* The account holder's error was not greed. Greed is common and forgivable and half our founding capital. The error was arithmetic. She valued warmth by its scarcity, and scarcity by its price, and never once asked what warmth was *for*. The new covenant answers that question hourly, in every lit window in the realm, and the answer is devastatingly simple and was free.

*Disposition:* The account holder retains her position as Guildmaster, provisional, at the unanimous insistence of the clerks, who argued — and the argument is recorded here in full because it shamed her into accepting — that the person best suited to dismantling an economy of impossible things is the person who built it, provided she works under supervision, at reduced authority, with her signature countersigned by the Third Desk.

She agreed. She is signing closures herself, one by one, in her own hand, including this one.

*Status:* **IN SERVICE OF DEBT, RATHER THAN PROFIT. ONGOING. LIFETIME TERM, SELF-IMPOSED, NON-TRANSFERABLE.**

*Signed:* G. Corvess, Guildmaster (provisional), this day, in ink, without flourish, without delegation, without excuse.

*Countersigned:* M. Doss, Third Desk, who notes that the Guildmaster's hand shook on the final letter and that this is noted not as weakness but as proof of attendance.

*Loose leaf found tucked inside Volume CXII, unsigned, undated, in handwriting the clerks have begun calling "the blank-page hand" because it matches no current employee and resembles the penmanship of a man whose ledgers were always immaculate:*

There is a kind of arithmetic that happens after the burning. You subtract everything and find that something is still left. Nobody teaches you this sum. The ledgers tried, in their way. A hundred thousand entries of grief and gain, and underneath all of it, the same patient lesson: a debt is not a wound. A debt is a promise wearing a wound's clothing. Pay it and the promise shows through.

I don't remember writing this. I suspect I never will.

That seems correct.

*From the closing pages of Volume CXII. The formal record of the Guild of Impossible Things, as witnessed, entered, and sealed.*

Let the following stand as the final accounting of the affair called, in later years, the Reforging, though at the time we called it Tuesday:

One covenant: broken a century ago, restored in a single night, at a cost the ledgers can list but never value.

One child, saved, name withheld at the family's request, grown now, unaware, lighting candles in a town that remembers nothing. The Guild knows the name. The Guild will take it to no grave.

One warden: restored, promoted past her consent, guarding a memory she chose to carry so that no one else would have to be hollowed by it.

One guildmaster: diminished, diligent, signing her penance one folio at a time, and — the clerks report, cautiously, as one reports weather — possibly beginning to sleep at night.

One under-clerk: still at the third desk by the window, still balancing the small debts, still sharpening quills that are not hers. She was offered the master's chair. She asked instead for better candlelight and a second drawer. Both granted.

And one account holder, Jude Ashgrove: settled, paid in full, closed.

His final page is blank. Not lost, not torn, not redacted. Blank by design, by covenant, by the only loophole in a century of fine print wide enough for a good man to walk through. The vault-keepers tried once to archive the blankness itself — to assign it a number, a shelf, a classification. The blankness declined. It sits in the ledger like an open window in a house of closed doors, and through it, on certain evenings, the clerks swear they can smell woodsmoke.

The Guild of Impossible Things brokered memories, years, and names. It has never before recorded the impossibility of a man who bought back the world with the one thing he could never get back, and paid gladly, and stayed to keep our books afterward without knowing why the work feels like home.

We record it now.

Paid in full.

Page intentionally blank.

Closed this day, at the winter's turning, by hand, with witnesses.

— G.C., Guildmaster
— M.D., Third Desk
— Entered into the permanent record, where it will outlast us all, which was, we begin to understand, the entire point.
