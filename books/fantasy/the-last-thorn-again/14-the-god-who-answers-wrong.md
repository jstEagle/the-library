## 14. The God Who Answers Wrong

The shard was gone. Veyra's agents had taken it over the ridge on a mule's back, wrapped in oilcloth like a thing of no consequence, and Cyrus had watched it go because she had chosen otherwise—chosen to drag Sefa out from under an avalanche of crossbow bolts instead of diving for the satchel.

Three days later, she was still paying for it.

Not in coin. In the particular silence that follows a bad decision made for good reasons. Sefa's arm was bound and splinted, the bolt having gone through meat rather than bone, and she'd stopped talking about it, which from Sefa was worse than complaining. Maera prayed constantly now, quietly, the way other people chewed their nails. And Cyrus sat apart at every fire, doing arithmetic that never came out even.

*You saved one life and lost a kingdom's worth of warmth.* That was what Veyra would say, if Veyra ever learned. *One smuggler with a hole in her against every hearth between here and the sea.*

The worst part—the part she couldn't say aloud, couldn't even shape properly in her own skull—was that some small, poisoned corner of her agreed.

They camped that night in a hollow below a ridge the maps called the Widow's Teeth, a spine of black rock where the wind came down like something hunting. The fire was small because Ashvane was weak and large fires drew eyes, and the dragon lay half-buried in snow-shadow at the hollow's mouth, her scales dull as old coins, breathing in long slow intervals that might have been sleep or might have been the last of something.

Cyrus took first watch because she didn't trust herself to sleep.

She sat with her back to a boulder, the antidote kit open on her knees out of pure habit—checking, counting, cataloguing—and found her hands shaking. Not cold. Not fear, exactly. Something with fear's shape but wearing grief's clothes.

"Talk to yourself again," Sefa said from the dark, "and I'm charging you rent."

"You're supposed to be sleeping."

"I'm supposed to be many things." She lowered herself onto the stone beside Cyrus, cradling her splinted arm, and stared into the little fire. Her face was drawn and gray around the mouth. "You know what the boys are saying? Down the caravan. They're saying you chose us over the shiny rock and they can't figure out why. Brann thinks you're simple. Toma thinks you're in love with me."

"What do you think?"

"I think," Sefa said slowly, "that my mother told me once there was a woman on the Thorn road who could taste poison in a stew from across a room, and that woman owed nobody nothing, and that was how she stayed alive." She looked at Cyrus sideways. "And I think that woman just spent ten years' worth of alive on a debt that wasn't hers, and she doesn't know yet whether she regrets it, and that's the most dangerous kind of person to camp with."

Cyrus almost laughed. It came out as breath. "That's very perceptive for someone who got shot."

"I got shot *distractedly*," Sefa said with dignity. "Different thing entirely."

The wind screamed down off the Teeth. Somewhere above them, loose stone chattered down the slope. And Cyrus, watching the fire gutter, felt the last of her composure fray through, and heard herself say—not to Sefa, not really, not to anyone—

"I did everything right. My whole life, I did everything right. I was careful, and quiet, and useful, and invisible, and none of it mattered. They built me for this before I could walk. They wrote it in a ledger like I was livestock. And now the one thing that was actually mine—" her fist closed around the antidote vial at her belt, "—I traded away for one person. One. And I don't even know if it was worth it, and that makes me a fool either way, doesn't it? A fool or a monster, pick one."

Sefa said nothing. Which was its own answer.

So Cyrus did the thing she had never once done in thirty-one years of keeping herself alive by trusting nothing.

She prayed.

It wasn't eloquent. It wasn't even a request, not exactly. It was more of a complaint, flung upward at the waking dark: *If you're listening—if any of you are actually listening—then look at what your covenant cost. Look at what was done to me in its name. If there's justice anywhere in the old blood, let Veyra choke on what she's stolen. Let her feel it. Let someone pay.*

The fire went still.

Not out. Still. Every flame stood up straight as a soldier, and the wind died mid-scream, and the snow stopped falling so completely that the flakes hung in the air like stars pinned in place. The silence had texture. It pressed against her eardrums.

"Cyrus," Sefa whispered. Her hand had found her knife. "Cyrus, the fire—"

"I see it."

The light bent. That was the only way she could ever describe it afterward. The firelight gathered itself, folded, and stood up out of the hearth-ring as a figure—or the suggestion of one, a shape the eye kept sliding off of, tall and robed in ember-color, with a face like the space just after lightning strikes when you can't remember what you saw.

It did not speak aloud. It spoke *in*, directly behind her eyes, in a voice like coals settling.

***LITTLE VESSEL.***

Her whole body went rigid. Every scar—the dozens of them, the map of her poisoning regimen written across her arms and ribs—ignited at once, warm and singing, and beneath the pain was something worse: recognition. The sensation of being *known*. Of being seen all the way down, past the invisibility, past the armor, into whatever the gods had spent twenty years building inside her.

***YOU ASKED FOR JUSTICE.***

"Yes," she managed. Her voice sounded small and far away.

***WE HAVE WATCHED YOU, HEIR OF THE FIRST HEARTH. THROUGH THE NEEDLES AND THE LEDGERS AND THE LONELY YEARS. YOU DID NOT BREAK. FEWER MORTALS THAN YOU IMAGINE WOULD HAVE HELD.*** The presence inclined its headless head, and the gesture was almost tender. ***THE WOMAN VEYRA HAS STOLEN WHAT IS YOURS. SHE WILL USE IT TO CHAIN US AS THE OLD KINGS CHAINED US—NOT WITH IRON BUT WITH LAW, NOT WITH ALTARS BUT WITH ACCOUNTS. WE KNOW HER KIND. WE WERE BOUND BY HER KIND ONCE.***

"Then take it back," Cyrus said. "Take the shard back. You're—you're a god. Take it."

***WE CANNOT REACH IT. IT IS COLD-WRAPPED AND IRON-BOUND AND CARRIED IN FAITHLESS HANDS, AND OUR REACH STOPS WHERE SUCH THINGS BEGIN.*** A pause, and then, gently, terribly: ***BUT YOURS DOES NOT.***

The hanging snow began to move. It swirled inward, spiraling around Cyrus where she sat, each flake catching firelight, until she sat inside a column of drifting embers.

***THE COVENANT MUST BE REMADE AT THE FIRST HEARTH, AND IT MUST BE MADE BY CONSENT—THIS IS TRUE, AND THE DRAGON WILL TEACH IT TO YOU IF SHE HAS NOT ALREADY. BUT CONSENT HAS A SHAPE, LITTLE VESSEL, AND SHAPES CAN BE SIMPLE OR SHAPES CAN BE COMPLEX. ONE VOICE SPEAKING FOR ALL REALMS IS ALSO CONSENT. ONE VOICE, UNDIVIDED, UNOPPOSED.***

The meaning arrived a heartbeat behind the words, the way thunder trails the flash.

***TAKE THE COVENANT WHOLE. SPEAK FOR ALL THREE VOICES YOURSELF. MORTAL AND DIVINE AND BURNING, GATHERED INTO ONE THRONE THAT IS ALREADY INSIDE YOUR BLOOD. NO MORE PARLIAMENTS OF GODS. NO MORE PRIESTESS WHO CAN BE BRIBED. NO MORE KINGS TO POISON. ONLY YOU, AND THE FIRE ANSWERING YOU DIRECTLY, FOREVER.***

"And in exchange?"

***VEYRA.*** The name came out like a log splitting. ***SAY YES, AND BY MORNING SHE WILL TASTE EVERY POISON SHE EVER COMMISSIONED. SHE WILL CHOKE ON ARSENIC IN A ROOM FULL OF PEOPLE WHO LOVE HER, AND NONE OF THEM WILL BE ABLE TO HELP, AND SHE WILL DIE KNOWING EXACTLY WHAT IT IS, BECAUSE YOU ARE A TASTER, HEIR, AND WE WILL MAKE HER DEATH A *TASTING*.*** The presence leaned closer, and the heat of it should have been unbearable and instead was the most comforting warmth Cyrus had ever felt, which frightened her more than anything else. ***AND NO ONE WILL EVER BUILD YOU AGAIN. NO LEDGER. NO REGIMEN. NO HAND IN YOUR BODY BUT YOUR OWN. THIS WE SWEAR BY THE FIRST FLAME.***

The fire held its breath. The snow hung. Sefa was staring at her with wide eyes and her knife half-drawn, clearly able to see the light but not hear the words.

And Cyrus Dravenmoor—who had been poisoned on purpose for twenty years, who had been falsified into existence, whose body had been a project belonging to strangers—who had just lost the relic and gained nothing but a splinted friend and a mountain of doubt—felt the offer slide into the exact hollow carved for it.

That was the horror of it. That was the thing she would carry for the rest of her life, however long it turned out to be: *it fit perfectly.* Every ache she had, the god had a balm. Every theft, a restitution. Vengeance served hot, power served whole, solitude sanctified at last—no more belonging required, no more choosing people over duty, no more sitting in the dark wondering if she was a fool. Just her, and the flame, forever.

Easy. Clean. Paid for with everyone else's freedom, which had never once appeared on any ledger she'd been forced to keep.

*This is the trap,* she thought, with terrible clarity. *This has always been the trap. They trained me to survive by taking the offered thing. Every dose they fed me was practice for this. Say yes, little vessel. Drink deep.*

Maera had taken the bribe too. All those years ago. Believing it was mercy, believing she was preventing a war—and the gods had gone silent for a generation because one frightened priestess said yes to the crown's gold.

Cyrus looked at Sefa—at the splinted arm, the gray face, the woman who owed her nothing anymore and was sitting here anyway, in the cold, keeping watch beside her. At the fire where Ashvane breathed her long dying breaths. At the whole ragged company asleep under wool in the lee of the rocks, smugglers and heretics and a broken priestess, none of whom had consented to be ruled by anyone, least of all by a food-taster with a god whispering in her ear.

"No," Cyrus said.

The word came out cracked and human and hers.

***CONSIDER—***

"No." Louder. "I've considered. My whole life was consideration. You want to hand me a throne made of everyone else's silence, and I—" her voice shook, and she let it, "—I spent twenty years learning poisons, and that's the most poisonous thing anyone's ever offered me. You dressed it up as justice and it's just another cage, and the fact that it's *my size* doesn't make it less of a cage. The covenant broke because somebody decided one voice was simpler than three. I won't be your somebody."

The warmth withdrew. All of it, at once, like a tide going out—and left her shivering in true cold for the first time since the presence had arrived.

***THEN YOU WILL DO IT THE HARD WAY,*** the god said, and there was something in the coal-voice now that might have been disappointment and might have been, incredibly, respect. ***AS IT WAS ALWAYS MEANT TO BE DONE. FREELY.*** The figure of ember-light began to fold itself back down into the flames. ***KNOW THIS, HEIR OF THE FIRST HEARTH. THE OTHERS ARE NOT SO PATIENT AS I. WHEN THEY COME OFFERING—AND THEY WILL COME, FOR YOU HAVE PRAYED ALOUD NOW, AND PRAYER IS A DOOR—***

The world snapped back.

Wind hit like a slap. Snow resumed falling. The fire leapt and crackled and behaved like ordinary fire, and Sefa was on her feet with her knife out, staring at Cyrus like she'd grown a second head.

"What," Sefa said, "in the name of every dead saint, was *that*."

"A door I shouldn't have opened," Cyrus said. She was still holding the antidote vial. Her knuckles were white around it. "A conversation I—"

She never finished. Because the god had said *the others are not so patient as I*, and it hadn't finished warning her before it left—and the darkness beyond the firelight answered for it.

It came down the slope from the Widow's Teeth without sound. Later, Cyrus would struggle to describe it: a pressure, a wrongness, the sense of something vast moving through the night the way a whale moves under a boat. Not one of the patient ones. Not a negotiator. Whatever this was, it had heard her prayer too—heard *vengeance* asked for in a mortal voice—and it had come to collect the debt regardless of her answer.

Sefa saw her face change and did the single bravest thing Cyrus had ever witnessed. She didn't run. She stepped *between*—between Cyrus and the dark, splinted arm and all, knife raised against something that predated knives—and shouted up the slope, "ASHVANE!"

The dragon's eyes opened. Two coals relighting in a dead hearth.

What happened next happened in perhaps three seconds. The dark thing struck—a blow of pure cold that Cyrus felt in her teeth—and Sefa met it with a body and a blade and absolutely no chance at all. The knife shattered. The cold took her shoulder and spun her like a doll, and she hit the boulder Cyrus had been leaning against hard enough that Cyrus heard something crack that wasn't stone.

Then fire filled the world.

Ashvane moved faster than anything that size had any right to move, dragging her failing body across the snow, and breathed a pillar of flame that turned night to noon and drove the dark thing back up the ridge shrieking in a voice like ice calving. It went reluctantly. It went looking back. But it went.

Silence again—but ordinary silence this time, wind and snow and Sefa groaning on the ground.

Cyrus was on her knees beside her before she knew she'd moved. Blood on the snow, black in the firelight. The cold-burn had frostbitten the skin around Sefa's shoulder in a perfect handprint, five fingers, and underneath it the old wound had torn open and started bleeding fresh.

"Sefa. Sefa, look at me."

"Did we win?" Sefa rasped. Her grin was white and awful. "Because from down here it looked like a draw."

"Hold still." Cyrus's hands were already moving, tearing open the kit, and her heart was breaking in a way she understood at last: this was the cost. This was what resisting easy power *cost*, in real coin, in real blood, paid by people who hadn't been asked. The god's bargain would have spared her this. Would have spared everyone this. And it would have damned them all far worse, and she knew it, and knowing it didn't stop the guilt from tasting like copper.

She packed the wound. Sefa hissed and cursed in three languages and gripped Cyrus's wrist hard enough to bruise.

"For the record," Sefa said through her teeth, "whatever that was—whatever it *offered* you—I saw your face when you said no." Her grip tightened. "Nobody says no like that unless it was worth a lot. So whatever it costs next... split it with me. That's how debts work on the Thorn road. You don't carry them alone."

Above them, Ashvane settled back into the snow, her flame dimming again, her breath shorter than before. She had spent something she could not afford tonight, and they all knew it, and no one said it.

Cyrus looked east, toward the pass, toward the capital where Veyra slept with a stolen shard and a clear conscience, and then north, toward the First Hearth, three weeks' climb through killing country with a wounded smuggler, a dying dragon, a priestess full of returning memories, and gods who had now heard her voice twice.

The patient one had warned her: *prayer is a door.* Well. She'd slammed this one. But doors, she was learning, swung both ways—and somewhere behind her, in the dark of the Widow's Teeth, something had taken a good look at her face.

And remembered it.

* * *

They broke camp before dawn, moving fast and quiet, because Maera came back from scouting the ridge white-faced and said only one sentence:

"There are soldiers in the pass ahead of us. A lot of them. And they're carrying the Hearthstone."
