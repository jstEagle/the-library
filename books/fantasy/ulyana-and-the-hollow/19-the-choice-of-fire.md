## 19. The Choice of Fire

The hollow king did not walk down the last stretch of the caldera. It poured.

That was the only word Ulyana could find for it, watching from the shore of black glass at the flame's edge. The shape that had been a man—the crown, the cloak, the courteous tilt of the head—came apart as it descended and reassembled with each step, like water remembering it had once been a river. Where its boots touched the stone, frost bloomed and died in the same heartbeat, summer and winter arguing over ground that belonged to neither. Behind it, in the tunnel mouth, Dominic stood alone with a borrowed spear and a body that had been broken twice already tonight, holding the line against the Hollow Guard because there was no one else to hold it.

She wanted to look at him. She made herself not look. If she looked, she would spend her last strength on fear for him instead of on what was coming, and what was coming deserved every scrap of her attention, the way a storm deserves the sailor's whole sky.

*It comes to bargain,* Emberlast had said. And here it was, stopping ten paces away, close enough that Ulyana could see what two thousand years of hunger had left of the king's face. Not much. The outline of a jaw. The suggestion of eyes, deep-set and dark as wells. The crown sat where a crown should sit, but it had sunk into the flesh—or the flesh had grown up around it, the way a tree swallows an iron nail—and when the thing inclined its head, the metal moved with it like bone.

"Ulyana." Her own name in the king's voice, warm as a hearth, wrong as a hearth in a drowning house. "You are bleeding."

She was. She had torn her palms open on the glass reaching for the flame, and the blood ran thin and bright down her wrists. She said nothing.

"You have carried their voices since you were a child," the hollow went on, gentle, patient, the tone of a physician delivering a diagnosis it has delivered ten thousand times. "You know what I know, then. You know how they *beg*. All day, all night, every god in the crown crying out through you like wind through a cracked window. You were going mad by inches before you ever met the gatekeeper. Don't lie to yourself about that. I watched you sleep in the waystation. I heard what you heard."

"You were in the waystation?" The words came out smaller than she wanted.

"I am everywhere prayer goes." It spread its hands—an old man's gesture, a merchant's gesture, *be reasonable*. "And so I offer you what no one else can. An end to the noise. Not silence—" it raised one finger before she could speak, and the finger was the king's finger, once, a hand that had signed pardons and death warrants both, "—not the poor gray nothing the Choir bought with their burning. *Silence.* A single voice where there were hundreds. Yours. Take the fire into yourself, Ulyana of Nowhere. Become the vessel. Every prayer in the world will come to you and you will answer or refuse as you choose, and no god will ever again use your mouth without asking. You will be kind. I know you will be kind—you fed tollmen you could have robbed, you healed a child in the Pass of Teeth when it cost you a fever week, you listened to an old woman who had nothing to give you. Rule kindly. End hunger. End the *hunger*, which is the oldest cruelty of all, older than kings." Its well-eyes gleamed. "I would do it myself, but I am—"

It paused. For a moment something flickered across the ruined face, something almost like grief.

"—I am what I am. But you could be better than any of us. You could be the first honest god the world has ever had."

The candle-flame behind Ulyana guttered low. In its dying light she felt them stir—not the gods' voices, those were nearly spent now, burned down to embers in her skull—but the *memory* of them, the shape of what they'd offered her all her life: importance, purpose, a self so large it could hold the world's suffering without spilling.

And beneath that memory, quieter, a different one. Wind on a mountainside. Bad singing, cheerfully off-key. A ledger with her name written in careful letters. *Ulyana, no family, no god.*

She hadn't written that yet. But she had seen it. Somewhere ahead of her, in the place visions come from, she had already signed it.

"No," she said.

The hollow tilted its head. "You haven't heard the terms."

"I don't need the terms. I've been hearing offers my whole life." Her voice steadied as she went, the way a rope steadies when you put your weight on it. "Every god that ever spoke through me started polite. Started with *I only want to help*. You know what happened? I woke up with frost in July and strangers kneeling in my dreams and no idea which thoughts were mine. You're offering me the same thing with better furniture." She took a breath that tasted of sulfur and cold stone. "And you're lying about the kindness. I've felt you through the crown, thing. There is no kindness in you. There's just hunger wearing a dead man's manners."

For the first time, the warmth left the king's face. What remained was worse than rage—it was *attention*, vast and patient, the attention of something that had eaten centuries.

"The dragon's offer is no kinder," it said. "Ask it what it wants. Ask it what the fire costs."

*

Emberlast's eye opened fully then, grey and enormous, filling half the cavern's dark. When the dragon spoke, the floor hummed under Ulyana's boots.

*The girl knows my price. I told her true, hollow thing, though you never learned to tell anything true. The fire goes into her. All of it. What remains of me, and what remains of them, and what remains of the door between the world and the divine—all of it burns through her mortal blood and out of the world forever. No more gods. No more vessels. No more crowned hunger, because there will be nothing left to hunger FOR. She will be emptied. She will be ordinary. She will live perhaps sixty more years and die of something small and human, and no one will build temples to her, and that is the gift.*

"And if she refuses?" The hollow's voice had gone soft again, and softness from it was more frightening than thunder. "If she simply steps aside? I take the flame. I take the gods. I wear divinity openly, as I was always meant to. The world gets a god who *acts*, dragon, instead of your long grey mercy of absence. Prayers answered. Crops in flood and drought alike. Wars ended by decree. You call it hunger. I call it *stewardship*."

*I call it a cage with the door painted open,* Emberlast rumbled. *I have watched two thousand years, little theft. I watched the binders fuse the gods into your crown to stop a war, and I watched the fusion rot, and I watched you learn to smile. Everything you steward, you consume. You consumed the courtier Vess. You consumed the captain whose face you wear. You will consume her too, and call it partnership while you swallow.*

Ulyana looked at the flame. It was very small now—a candle, truly, trembling in a cavern built to hold cathedrals, and she understood suddenly that this was what two millennia of vigilance had dwindled to: a wick, and an old grey dragon coiled around it like a cupped hand around a match in wind.

"What does it cost me," she asked quietly. "Truly. Not the poetry. The truth."

*The truth.* Emberlast's eye regarded her, and in it she saw reflected a woman small as an ant, standing at the edge of everything. *The voices leave. All of them. You will never hear a prayer again—not theirs, not anyone's. The part of you that was a conduit becomes ash, and ash does not regrow. You will stand beside people in pain and be able to offer them nothing but your hands and your hours. Some days that will feel like poverty. Some nights you will grieve the voices, even the cruel ones, because they were yours and they made you large. And the burning itself—* the dragon's great head lowered until its breath stirred her hair, and its voice gentled into something almost tender, *—the burning itself will hurt, child, in a way that has no word in any tongue I have spoken. It is the pain of becoming small. Nothing survives it unchanged. Many would not survive it at all.*

"And if I take the hollow's bargain?"

*Then you survive. Larger. Lonelier. Forever.* A pause, and then, dry as old paper: *Forever is longer than it sounds. I would know.*

*

Somewhere behind her, steel rang on stone. Dominic. She heard him shout something wordless and defiant, heard the crack of the Hollow Guard pressing in, and her heart lurched toward the sound like a compass needle.

And then, through the din, his voice found her—not shouting now, but carrying anyway, the way certain voices carry, pitched to cross a distance and land exactly where they're meant to.

"Ulyana!" Ragged. Certain. "Hey—smuggler's daughter! Listen to me!"

She turned. At the tunnel mouth he was bleeding from the temple, spear braced against his shoulder, three Guard shapes circling him like wolves deciding. He wasn't looking at them. He was looking at her, and his eyes were the most solid thing in the cavern.

"Whatever they're offering—" he called, "—remember the village! Remember the unanswered ones!"

The Village of the Unanswered. The old woman with the crooked hands who had thanked her anyway. *Thank you for listening. Nobody listens. Listening is enough.*

"You don't need to be a god to matter!" His voice cracked and he didn't care. "You never needed it! The whole time I've known you, the best things you did, you did *empty-handed*! The maps you stopped selling! The kid in the pass! Me! You saved ME, and you didn't have a drop of fire in you that day—you just stayed! That's the whole miracle, Ulyana! Staying!"

The hollow moved then—fast, faster than a king should move, closing the ten paces with its hand extended, and its voice poured honey directly into her ear, into the space behind her eyes where the voices used to live:

"They will forget you. Mortals always forget. Sixty years and dust, and no one praying at your grave. But as a god—"

"As a god," Ulyana said, "I'd never be *with* anyone again. Only above them." She looked at the extended hand, the crown sunken in dead flesh, the well-eyes full of two thousand years of appetite dressed up as care. "You know what I figured out, in the collapsing dark, talking to the threshold god? You don't actually want worship. Worship is just food. What you want is what every hungry thing wants—to stop being empty. And there's only one cure for that, and it isn't power. It's letting something in. And you *can't*. You fused yourself shut two thousand years ago and there's no room left in you for anything but taking."

She stepped back. Toward the flame. Toward the dragon.

"So no. Not your vessel. Not your partner. Not your meal." She turned to Emberlast, and her voice did not shake, which surprised her, and would surprise her for the rest of her life whenever she remembered this moment. "Old one. Give me the fire."

*Child.* The dragon's eye was very close, and very old, and very sad. *Once it begins, it cannot be stopped or shared or undone. You will burn alone.*

"I've been burning alone since I was six years old," Ulyana said. "At least this time it's for something."

Behind her, the hollow screamed—not the king's voice anymore, but the sound underneath it, the sound of a door being kicked in—and lunged.

And Dominic Lockridge, keeper of the gate that should stay shut, threw himself out of the tunnel mouth and into its path with a broken spear and nothing else, roaring her name like a ward against the dark—

—and Ulyana stepped into the flame.

*

It did not feel like fire.

It felt like *leaving*.

The light went up through her boots, her knees, her opened hands, and every god still smoldering in the crown's long shadow came with it—she felt them pass through her like travelers through a doorway, thousands upon thousands, each one pausing for a heartbeat to touch her hair or press something wordless into her palm, gratitude or apology or simply *goodbye*—and the hollow's reaching hand struck the light and recoiled with a shriek that peeled frost off the cavern walls, because there was nothing left for it to grip, nothing left to hunger for, the pantry of heaven emptying itself through one smuggler's daughter straight out of the world—

It hurt the way Emberlast had promised. It hurt like becoming small. She felt herself shrinking, the vast conduit-self peeling away layer by layer, the voices thinning, thinning, going quiet, going *quiet*—

And in the last instant, before the final silence closed over her like snowfall, she heard two things.

One was Emberlast, sighing, uncoiling at last after two thousand years, its great body beginning already to crumble into warm grey ash, and the old dragon was—she was almost sure—*smiling*.

The other was Dominic, on his knees in the settling dust, the Hollow Guard collapsed around him like cut marionettes, the hollow king staggering back formless and shrieking with nowhere left to pour itself—Dominic, alive, weeping without shame, saying her name over and over, not as a prayer, because there was no one left to pray to, but as a plain human calling across a room: *Ulyana. Ulyana. Ulyana.*

And it was the sweetest sound she had ever heard, because for the first time in her life, it was the only voice in her head.

The light went out.

The silence came down like kindness.

And in the dark, gaunt and mortal and utterly empty, Ulyana began—very quietly, very shakily—to laugh.
