## 6. Buyer Unknown

There is a particular kind of shame in being the only man alive who cannot remember his own crime. Everyone else in this unraveling country has lost something—a village, a name, a mother's face. I have lost the one thing that might have explained why they all lost theirs.

Dominic doesn't say it aloud, but she thinks it. I can tell by the way she watches me when she thinks I'm cataloging maps: that look like she's reading a ledger entry with a smudge on it, trying to decide whether to trust the ink.

We travel east along what used to be the trade-road to Vessia, following ledger-logic, which is its own strange discipline. The Ledgerkeepers taught me this much before I was expelled from their company—every transaction leaves a wake. You don't find a buyer by asking who bought; you find them by asking what was *paid*, and then you walk backward along the payment until it runs dry. Debts are rivers. They always flow somewhere.

The road itself is half-gone. Not destroyed—*unwritten*. For a mile at a stretch we'll be walking on honest cobblestone, and then the world goes soft and gray and slightly damp, like walking through the memory of a road rather than the road itself, and my boots make no sound because sound requires certainty. Dominic carries her master's brass scale in her satchel, and every few hours she weighs something small against something smaller—a coin against a button, a word against a silence—and writes down which is heavier. It's how she measures how much reality is left in a place. In the unwritten stretches, everything balances perfectly. Nothing has weight because nothing has history.

"That's cheerful," I said the first time she told me.

"It's not meant to be cheerful," she said. "It's meant to be precise."

That's Dominic entire. She has never once in her life confused comfort with truth, and I loved her for it before I understood it would be the death of us both.

* * *

The trade-routes of memory are not metaphorical. This is the part nobody believes until they've walked one. When the Ledgerkeepers brokered impossible things, they didn't move goods—they moved *certainty*. A bottled echo is a certainty about a voice. A key to nowhere is a certainty about a door. And a country's true name is the largest certainty there is: the sum total of everyone's agreement that this place exists.

Certainty moves along routes the way water moves downhill. There are stations. There are tolls. There are, God help me, *brokers*.

I know the routes because I drew them. Twenty years ago, before the disgrace, before the map vaults, I was the royal cartographer, and my finest work was never a map of land at all—it was a chart of the memory-trades, inked on vellum so fine the king himself unrolled it across his knees. I remember drawing it. I remember the pride. What I do not remember is what happened three days later, and that gap in me has a shape, and the shape is a wound, and I have carried it so long I mistook it for a character trait.

At the third station—an old toll-house standing in fog so thick the bell above its door rings without being struck—we found the first real record. Dominic opened her traveling ledger and read aloud, and I felt each word land on me like a stone dropped down a well:

*"Item: one (1) sovereign existence, kingdom of [NAME WITHHELD]. Condition: used, borders fraying. Buyer: the Vessian Imperium. Price: see attached schedule. Broker: R. Calloway."*

"Attached schedule," I repeated.

She turned the page. Her face did something complicated and then went very still, which is what her face does instead of crying.

"The price," she said, "wasn't gold."

* * *

It wasn't gold. It was never going to be gold. Empires don't buy existence with currency; currency is what you use when you can't get anything better. The Vessian Imperium paid in futures—in obligations, in years, in a debt structured so intricately that tracing it took us four days and nearly killed us both, because the debt had been designed, deliberately, to be untraceable. Layered through shell-accounts of memory. Routed through dead men's recollections. One link in the chain was a promise made by a woman who had been Unwritten before she finished speaking it.

But debts are rivers, and rivers reach the sea.

The sea, in this case, was a night twenty-two years ago. A fire. The old Ledgerhouse annex, before your time, before mine ended—I'll come to that. A fire in the winter, and a girl trapped on the second floor, and an apprentice cartographer who happened to be delivering charts that evening, who heard her screaming, and went up the stairs anyway.

I remember it now. That's the horror of it. The memory came back the way floodwater comes back through a broken dam—all at once, in the toll-house, while Dominic held the final page of the schedule under the lamp and read out the last line item.

*"Final consideration: one (1) memory, held in perpetuity by the seller. Subject: the act of brokerage."*

And beneath it, in my own handwriting—the handwriting I'd know anywhere, that slanting, impatient script with the crossed sevens—

*I accept this price willingly.*

The dam broke. Twenty-two years fell on me in the space of a breath.

I remembered the fire. I remembered carrying her out—she was eleven, light as a bundle of kindling, coughing smoke into my coat. I remembered the fever afterward, the doctors shaking their heads, the Ledgerkeepers' physicians saying her lungs were scarred past saving, that no medicine in the country could pull her through. I remembered going to the brokers myself. I remembered offering everything I had, and being told it wasn't enough, and being offered instead a trade: the Imperium would supply the remedy—one of theirs, a thing grown rather than made, a certainty about a living girl that her body would believe absolutely—but the price was a service rendered. Someone had to broker the larger transaction. Someone with access to the crown's chart-rooms. Someone trusted.

They didn't ask me to steal the name. They asked me to *introduce* the buyer to the seller. To draw the route and open the toll-gates and stand witness while the old king, drowning in debts of his own, signed away the one asset he didn't know he had. They asked me to be the riverbed.

And I said yes. I sat at that table with my hands steady and my heart full of one thought only—*she lives, she lives, she lives*—and I signed, and I walked out, and the price collected itself from me on the threshold like a toll-taker, reaching into my skull and taking the whole evening, the whole decision, the whole sin, leaving behind a wound-shaped hole that I spent two decades calling melancholy.

"Dominic," I said, and my voice came out wrong, scraped hollow. "The medicine that saved you. It was Vessian."

She looked at me. The lamp guttered between us.

"I know," she said quietly. "I read the schedule. Page nine. *One (1) life restored, subject: D. Thackeray.*"

"You knew?"

"I've known since the sealed ledger." She folded the page closed with terrible gentleness. "I've known since the entry dated tomorrow. I just couldn't prove you knew. And I needed to know if you knew—because if you chose it knowing, that's one thing, Riven. If you were used—" She stopped. She pressed her thumb flat against the ledger's cover the way she presses things flat when she wants them to stop moving. "You weren't used. You chose. You just weren't allowed to remember choosing."

Loyalty and love, it turns out, are the same wound seen from different sides. I cut my country to save her. She was saved by the cutting. Every morning since, the crown has sung the song that holds the borders together, and every note of it has been sung over the fact of what I did, and neither of us knew, and both of us carried it, and the country unraveled anyway, because that is what bargains with empires do—they collect slowly, at the edges first, so you have time to forget what you owed.

* * *

We walked out of the toll-house into the fog, and I told her everything, all of it, in order, because order was the only penance I had left. The fire. The fever. The table where I signed. The moment on the threshold when the memory was taken and I felt it go—not as pain but as *lightness*, which should have warned me, because nothing that leaves you that easily was ever yours to keep.

She listened the way she does everything: completely, without flinching, taking notes. Actual notes. At one point she wrote down my confession verbatim and asked me to initial it, and I laughed—a cracked, awful laugh—and said, "You're building a case against me."

"I'm building a record," she said. "There's a difference. A case needs a verdict. A record just needs to be true." Then she added, in smaller script, underneath: *He chose her. He would choose her again. This is not an excuse. It is a fact, and facts are heavier than excuses.*

I wanted to argue. I wanted to say something large and self-flagellating and useless. Instead I said the true thing, which was smaller:

"I'd do it again."

"I know," she said. "That's what frightens me. Not what you did. That you'd do it again, and mean it, and pay the same price, and stand there tomorrow not remembering, and I'd have to lose you twice." She looked east, toward the fog-line, toward the Imperium. "So we're not going to let tomorrow happen the same way twice. We're going to find the buyer's envoy, and we're going to renegotiate."

"You can't renegotiate with an empire."

"You can if you hold collateral." She tapped the ledger against her palm. "The contract says the name returns if it's sung whole by a living voice. It doesn't say whose voice. And it doesn't say what happens if the singer refuses to stop singing."

The fog thinned ahead of us, and through it we could see the border-stones of Vessia, and beyond them, impossibly, lights—a city burning bright and certain in the dark, lit with all the existence it had purchased, while behind us our own country dimmed toward gray.

And on the road between, walking toward us out of the fog, came figures. Dozens of them. Blank-faced, silent, humming.

The Unwritten were moving too. And they were humming a tune I almost recognized—something about it snagging at the hole in me, at the wound-shape where my sin used to be, like a key testing a lock.

Dominic heard it before I named it. She went white.

"That's my mother's lullaby," she said.
