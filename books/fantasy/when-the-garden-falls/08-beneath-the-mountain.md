## 8. Beneath the Mountain

The tunnel drank light.

Sebastian had known darkness before — cellars, pantries, the space beneath a floorboard where he'd hidden as a boy while his father argued with the tax collector. But this was different. This darkness had weight. It pressed against his eyelids, settled in the hollows of his collarbones, tasted of limestone and old iron and something sweeter underneath, like honey left too long in a cracked jar.

Wren walked half a pace ahead, her hand on the wall. Her fingers didn't tremble. She moved like someone who had never learned to fear the dark, or had learned too well to show it.

"Odile said the smugglers' roads go deeper than any map," she murmured, her voice swallowed almost instantly by the stone. "She said the mountain remembers every foot that walks it."

"Odile said a lot of things." Sebastian's boots found purchase on slick rock. The air grew warmer with each descent, not colder — a fever heat rising from the earth's bones. "She also said not to trust doors. Which suggests she expected us to find them."

"Did she expect us to survive them?"

He didn't answer. The tunnel narrowed, forcing them single-file, and the walls began to change. Rough-hewn gave way to fitted stone, joints so tight a knife blade couldn't find purchase. The tool marks were old, centuries old perhaps, but the craft was unmistakable. This wasn't smugglers' work. Smugglers hacked and blasted. This had been *carved*.

Ahead, the darkness thinned. Not lightened — thinned, as though the dark itself had been stretched until it became translucent, shot through with veins of something that might have been bioluminescence or might have been memory made visible.

Wren stopped. Her head tilted, listening to something only she could hear.

"Birds," she whispered. "But not birds. They're singing in a language that doesn't exist."

"Can you understand it?"

"No. But I know what it *feels* like." She turned, her face pale in the strange half-light. "It feels like hunger. Not for food. For *stories*."

The tunnel opened into a chamber.

Sebastian's breath caught.

He had imagined many things beneath the mountain: dungeons, laboratories, the squalid lairs of whatever creatures fed on the gifted. He had not imagined a ballroom.

The ceiling vaulted fifty feet overhead, ribbed with something that caught the veined light and threw it back in fractured rainbows — not crystal, not glass, something organic, grown. The floor was black marble veined with gold, worn smooth by centuries of dancing feet. Along the walls stood tables, not of wood but of something pale and translucent, each laden with objects that made no sense: a child's wooden horse, a wedding ring bent out of true, a lock of hair tied with blue ribbon, a letter sealed with red wax, a single chess piece — a white queen, her crown chipped.

And the guests.

They stood in clusters, tall and impossibly slender, their skin the color of milk spilled on porcelain. Their eyes were wrong — too large, pupils dilated until the iris was a thin ring of silver or gold or violet. They wore clothes that seemed to shift at the edges, silk becoming velvet becoming shadow becoming something like living smoke. No two wore the same fashion; some draped in styles Sebastian recognized from portraits three hundred years old, others in cuts that had never existed in any human court.

They did not eat. They *tasted*.

A noblewoman lifted a teacup — porcelain painted with forget-me-nots — and pressed it to her lips. Her throat worked. Her eyes fluttered shut. A sound escaped her, half-sigh, half-moan, and the air around her shimmered with the ghost-image of a garden in spring, a young woman laughing, a promise spoken beneath a cherry tree: *I will wait for you. I will always wait.*

The noblewoman swallowed. The image dissolved. She set down the cup and reached for a pocket watch, frozen at 4:17.

*Memory,* Sebastian realized, his gift waking on his tongue like a struck bell. *They're eating memories. Not metaphorically. Literally.*

Beside him, Wren made a small, strangled sound. Her hands flew to her ears, though the chamber was silent as a tomb.

"They're screaming," she breathed. "All of them. The memories — they're *screaming*."

Sebastian's hand found her wrist, grounding her. "Breathe. Wren, breathe. You're not the one being consumed."

"Am I not?" Her eyes met his, wide and wet. "Every gifted child they took — every soul they harvested — their memories are *here*. Being passed around like canapés. My mother's lullaby. My father's hands teaching me to mend nets. The taste of blackberries in July." Her voice cracked. "They're eating *us*."

A figure detached itself from a cluster near the far wall. Tall, broader than the others, wearing a coat of midnight velvet over a waistcoat stitched with thread that caught the light like trapped stars. His face was handsome in the way a blade is handsome — precise, cold, purposeful. His eyes were entirely pupil, no white at all.

He moved toward them without walking, the distance simply ceasing to exist between one heartbeat and the next.

*Inquisitor,* Sebastian's gift whispered, tasting the air the figure displaced. *But not Maren Voss. Older. Deeper. The thing that made her.*

"Sebastian Kavanagh." The voice resonated in the chest, not the ears. "The crown's taster. The poison-drinker. The truth-tongued." A smile curved lips too pale to be human. "We have anticipated you for a very long time."

Sebastian's fingers brushed the vials at his belt. Twelve bottles, each a different poison, each a different key. "I go by Sebastian. My friends call me worse."

"Your friends." The noble's head tilted. "The woman Odile. The king you served. The girl who hears birdsong." He spoke the words like he was tasting them, rolling each syllable on a tongue that had forgotten what honesty tasted like. "You carry them all inside you. A pantry of stolen lives."

"I carry what I've earned."

"Ah, *earned*." The noble laughed, a sound like crystal breaking. "Such a human concept. As if anything is earned in this world. As if the king's death in your arms was anything but a transaction — his final breath for your silence, his secret for your survival."

Sebastian went still. *The king's death. The memory I've never spoken. The taste of aconite in the royal physic, and the king's hand on my wrist, and the words whispered through blood-flecked lips: "The queen... she sold her... find the mountain... break the—"*

*He knows. He tasted it from me. But when? How?*

"Three trials," the noble said, as though Sebastian hadn't thought at all. "Three doors. Three truths offered freely. Pass them, and you may seek what you came for. Fail, and you become part of the vintage." His smile widened. "We *do* so love a vintage with... *character*."

"And if I refuse?"

"Then you leave. The way you came." The noble gestured, and the tunnel behind them was simply *gone* — sealed by a fall of stone that made no sound. "But the mountain does not release what enters it willingly. You would wander until your mind unspooled, and then we would harvest you anyway. Slowly. Painfully. With great appreciation for the flavor of despair."

Wren's hand found Sebastian's. Cold fingers, crushing grip.

"Three trials," Sebastian said. "Name them."

***

The first door stood at the end of a corridor lined with mirrors.

Not glass mirrors. Pools of mercury set in frames of bone, each reflecting not the corridor but a different scene: a battlefield at dawn, a nursery empty of children, a ship sinking beneath black waves, a crown melting in a furnace.

The door itself was ordinary oak, banded with iron, bearing a single word carved deep: **REGRET**.

"Open it," the noble's voice came from everywhere and nowhere. "Taste what lies beyond. Offer a regret true enough to match the door's weight. Anything less, and the door consumes you."

Sebastian approached. The mercury mirrors rippled as he passed. In one, he saw himself at sixteen, kneeling beside his father's cooling body, the tax collector's ledger open to a page that would have saved their shop — if Sebastian had delivered it in time. In another, he saw the king's face, contorted by poison, reaching for him with fingers that dissolved into mist.

He stopped before the door. His hand hovered over the latch.

*Regret. True regret. Not the performative kind, not the kind you tell yourself at night to feel noble. The kind that burns.*

He thought of Odile. Her blunt fingers wrapping his knuckles after a beating. Her voice, rough with whiskey and truth: *You're a stupid boy, Seb. But you're *my* stupid boy. And I don't let mine break.*

He thought of the night she died. The false trail she'd given Voss. The way she'd looked at him through the bars of the cage — not afraid. *Proud.*

*I should have gone back. I should have burned the city down to reach her. I should have—*

The door swung inward before he touched it.

Beyond lay not a room but a moment.

Odile's kitchen. The smell of rosemary bread and pipe smoke. Odile at her table, writing a letter by lamplight — the letter she'd never sent, the one Sebastian had found in her coat pocket after.

*To the boy who tastes poison,* it read. *If you're reading this, I'm dead. Don't mourn me. I lived longer than a smuggler has any right to, and I spent my last years watching you become something the world wasn't ready for. That's not nothing. That's everything. Don't you dare waste it on grief. Use it. You've always been good at using things. — O.*

Sebastian stood in the doorway, and the memory washed over him — not visual, but *tasted*. Rosin and ink. Old paper. The particular salt of tears he'd refused to shed. The bitter-sweet of pride that wasn't his own but had been gifted to him anyway.

He stepped across the threshold.

The memory shattered like glass, each fragment dissolving on his tongue: *pride, fierce and tender / fear, not for herself but for him / love, the kind that asks nothing in return / regret — only one: that she wouldn't see what he became.*

The door slammed shut behind him.

"One," the noble's voice echoed. "Two remain."

***

The second door waited in a garden that grew upside down.

Trees rooted in the ceiling, their branches brushing the floor. Flowers bloomed in reverse, petals folding inward toward stems. A fountain fell *upward*, water rising from a basin in the floor to vanish into darkness above. The air smelled of ozone and jasmine and something copper-sweet.

The door was white birch, carved with branching patterns that moved when not looked at directly. A single word: **BETRAYAL**.

"Offer a betrayal," the noble's voice instructed. "One you committed. Not one done to you. The door distinguishes."

Sebastian's jaw tightened. *Betrayal. I have a pantry of those.*

He thought of the chancellor's wine, years ago. The arsenic he'd tasted and not warned against — not because he wanted the chancellor dead, but because the man's thoughts had been so loud with grief and certainty that the king *deserved* death, and Sebastian had been young and tired and *curious* what that certainty tasted like on the tongue of a dying man.

He thought of the queen's sister. Elira. The soul he'd never met but carried now like a stone in his pocket. The bargain he'd made with the archivist: *I'll descend. I'll retrieve her. But I won't promise what happens after.*

He thought of Wren, walking beside him into this nightmare, trusting him to lead her out again. The betrayal not yet committed but already forming in his chest: *I would sacrifice her to finish this. I would. I hate that I would. But I would.*

The door opened.

A throne room. The king — *his* king, the one who'd died in his arms — sat not on the throne but on the steps before it, head in hands. The crown lay beside him, dented. The great hall was empty save for them.

*Sebastian,* the king's voice, not spoken but *tasted* — wine turned to vinegar, honey curdled, the metallic tang of blood. *You knew. You tasted it in the physic. You knew it was aconite. You knew I was dying. And you let me speak my last words to you instead of calling the physicers. Why?*

*Because I wanted to know,* the memory-Sebastian answered, and the taste of it was ash and salt. *Because I wanted to hear what a dying king whispers to a food-taster. Because I was tired of being the vessel for everyone else's secrets. Because I wanted one secret of my own.*

The king looked up. His face was not angry. It was *disappointed*. That was worse.

*You were the only honest thing in this court,* the king tasted. *And you broke yourself to keep a secret that wasn't yours to keep. That is the betrayal, boy. Not that you let me die. That you let yourself become a liar to do it.*

Sebastian fell to his knees in the memory, and the taste of it flooded him: *shame, hot and acid / grief, delayed and compounded / the terrible knowledge that he would do it again, every time, because some truths are too expensive to speak.*

The birch door closed.

"Two," the noble intoned. "One remains."

***

The third door was not a door.

It was Wren.

She stood in a circular chamber of white stone, barefoot in a white shift, her dark hair loose around her shoulders. She did not move. Her eyes were closed. Around her wrists, ankles, throat — thin chains of a metal that drank the light, anchoring her to the floor, the ceiling, the walls.

The noble stood beside her, one long finger tracing the line of her jaw.

"Wren of the Thornfells," he murmured. "Who hears truth in birdsong. Who sings the memories of the stolen." His finger pressed, drawing a bead of blood that did not fall but *hovered*, a perfect red sphere. "The final trial is simple, Sebastian Kavanagh. Offer a memory of her. A true memory. One that proves you value her more than your mission. More than the soul you seek. More than your own survival."

Sebastian's hand went to his belt. To the vials. To the poison he'd brewed for the court's hospitality — a tincture of his own blood, concentrated, capable of killing anything that drank it.

*If I use it now, she dies too. The chains, the noble, the court — all connected. One vintage.*

*If I don't, I must give them a memory. A true one. The most true.*

Wren's eyes opened. They were not afraid. They were *knowing*.

*She knew,* Sebastian tasted in the air between them. *She knew this was the trial. She walked into it willingly. She trusted me to choose.*

*Or she trusted me to refuse.*

The noble smiled. "Time flows differently here, taster. You have the span of three heartbeats. Choose."

Sebastian closed his eyes.

He did not reach for a vial.

He reached for a memory.

Not the first time they'd met — her in a cage, singing to a bird that wasn't there, her voice weaving a map of the Thornfells that no human hand had drawn.

Not the night by the fire when she'd pressed her palm to his and let him taste the birdsong-memory of her mother: *hands shaping dough, flour in dark hair, a lullaby about a girl who walked into the wind and became a storm.*

Not even the moment two days ago when she'd woken screaming from a nightmare of the mountain, and he'd held her until the shaking stopped, neither of them speaking the truth that he'd been shaking too.

He chose the memory from this morning.

*Wren at the tunnel's mouth. The mountain's heartbeat thrumming through the stone. She'd turned to him, face pale in the veined light, and said: "I'm not afraid to die. I'm afraid to die *without purpose*. If this ends the winter, if this frees the stolen souls — then my life had meaning. That's all any of us get, isn't it? Meaning?"

*And he'd wanted to say: Your life has meaning *to me*. You have meaning *to me*. But the words were too large, too dangerous, too much like a promise he couldn't keep.*

*So he'd said: "We'll both walk out. That's the plan."*

*And she'd smiled — a real smile, small and crooked — and said: "You're a terrible liar, Sebastian Kavanagh. But I'll follow you anyway."*

The memory tasted of: *cold stone / warm fingers / the taste of a lie told for the right reason / the taste of a truth heard anyway / trust, fragile and fierce / love, unnamed and unspoken / the terrifying certainty that he would burn the world to keep her breathing.*

He opened his mouth and *spoke* the memory into the air — not words, but the thing itself, exhaled like breath on glass.

The white stone chamber drank it.

The chains binding Wren did not break. They *dissolved*, each link turning to dust that drifted upward like snow.

Wren fell forward — and Sebastian was there, catching her, his arms full of living weight and warm breath and the scent of pine needles and rain.

"Show-off," she whispered against his collarbone. "You couldn't just *think* it like a normal person?"

"I'm not normal. You knew that."

"Neither am I." She pulled back, eyes bright. "The birds are singing again. Different song this time. Victory. Or —" She frowned. "Or something that *feels* like victory. But underneath..." Her head tilted. "Underneath there's a new note. Sharp. Urgent. *Warning*."

The noble stood where he had been, unmoved. But something had changed. The perfect stillness of his face had cracked — not into anger, but into something that might have been *interest*.

"Three trials passed," he said. "The taster has paid his toll in truth. The girl has paid hers in trust." He inclined his head. "Welcome to the Court of the Long Winter, Sebastian Kavanagh. You may now seek the Chalice of Frozen Wine. It lies in the Heart-Chamber, three corridors down, through the Gallery of Unspoken Words."

"And the queen's sister?" Sebastian asked, supporting Wren as she found her footing. "Elira. Her soul."

The noble's smile returned, colder than before. "The soul is *in* the chalice. The chalice *is* the soul. To free her, you must drink the wine. All of it. And survive what it contains."

"Poison?"

"Memories. Nine hundred years of them. Every soul the court has ever consumed, compressed into a single vintage." The noble's eyes — all pupil, no white — fixed on Sebastian with terrible clarity. "You have tasted poisons all your life, taster. You have built immunity to death. But have you built immunity to *grief*? To *regret*? To the weight of every stolen life this court has ever feasted upon?"

Sebastian's hand tightened on Wren's. His gift hummed on his tongue, tasting the noble's words — and beneath them, something else. A thread of *fear*. Not for himself. For *them*.

*He's afraid of what I'll become if I drink. He's afraid I'll survive.*

"Three corridors," Sebastian repeated. "Gallery of Unspoken Words. Heart-Chamber. Chalice of Frozen Wine."

"Correct. But know this, truth-tongued one: the Gallery does not permit silence. Every unspoken word you have ever swallowed will demand utterance. Every truth you bit back. Every lie you let live. They will *all* be spoken before you reach the Heart."

The noble faded — not walked away, *faded*, like breath on a mirror.

"Charming," Wren muttered. "Three corridors of my own personal hell. Let's go."

They walked.

***

The Gallery of Unspoken Words was a corridor of mouths.

Not carved. *Grown*. Thousands of them, lining the walls from floor to ceiling, each different — child's mouth, old woman's mouth, king's mouth, beggar's mouth. They did not speak. They *breathed*, a susurrus of exhalation that raised the hair on Sebastian's arms.

As they passed, the mouths began to whisper.

*I love you — said to a dying father who never heard.*

*I didn't mean it — the lie told to a wife about her husband's last words.*

*She's not mine — the thought held when looking at a daughter with another man's eyes.*

*I poisoned the king — the truth Sebastian had swallowed, choked down, buried beneath duty and fear and the terrible knowledge that speaking it would change nothing and cost everything.*

Wren flinched at each one. Her gift made her hear them not as whispers but as birdsong — each unspoken word a different species, a different melody, a cacophony of silences given voice.
