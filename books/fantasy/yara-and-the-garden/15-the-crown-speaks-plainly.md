## 15. The Crown Speaks Plainly

The pain came at dusk, and Aldric welcomed it the way a drowning man welcomes a rope that might as easily be a noose.

For eleven days since the rosebush — since the witch's magic had threaded up through the flagstones of his mother's dead garden and touched something beneath the crown — the voice in his skull had been *wrong*. Not quieter. Wrong. Like a singer whose pitch had slipped, like a bell with a crack running through it, and every word the crown spoke now carried a hairline fracture of static that made his teeth ache.

He sat in the small room he had kept for himself these three years: not the royal bedchamber, which the crown preferred, all banners and gold and witnesses, but a servant's cell off the east corridor, narrow as a coffin, smelling of tallow and old stone. He had told the guards it was where he went to pray. They had believed him, because kings were not supposed to lie about prayer, and because the usurper's agents found piety boring.

Here, alone, Aldric pressed his palms against his temples and did the thing he had learned to do in the worst nights: he thought of the garden.

Not this garden. The other one. The one before.

His mother had grown pears — small, hard, ugly pears that no cook wanted, that she ate standing up in the rows with juice on her chin like a girl. He could not remember her face anymore. Three years of the crown's honeyed whispering had sanded away the fine details of everything he loved, the way water sands stone, patiently, without malice. But he remembered the pears. He remembered the way she'd hummed while she walked the rows, some tuneless thing she claimed was a lullaby from her nurse, and he remembered thinking, even at seven years old, that a queen who hummed badly among ugly pear trees was worth ten emperors.

*Hold onto that,* he told himself. *The pears. The humming. Hold onto it.*

The crown stirred.

It always knew when he retreated into memory. It hated those places — or perhaps it simply could not follow him there, and hatred was the shape its envy took. He felt it gather itself behind his eyes, felt the familiar pressure build at the junction of skull and gold, that fused seam where flesh ended and metal began, warm as infection.

*You are sad tonight,* the crown said. Its voice was beautiful. That was the horror of it, the thing no one who had never worn it could understand: it was beautiful, warm and resonant, a voice like his father's had been, like a beloved teacher's, like every kind authority he had ever trusted. *You should not be sad. You are a king.*

"I am a prisoner," Aldric said aloud. His own voice sounded thin by comparison, cracked and mortal. "As are you, I think. Though I only lately began to wonder what your cage looks like."

The pressure paused. In three years, the crown had never once let a silence stand. It filled every gap the way water fills a hull, and Aldric had come to understand that the filling was not generosity. It was fear of empty space. Fear of what might live in silence.

Now, for the first time, the crown said nothing at all.

And in that nothing, Aldric heard something new underneath the stillness: a sound like wind through a very long corridor. A vast, slow breathing. And beneath even that, so faint he might have invented it, a note of pure animal hunger — the hunger of something that had been shut in the dark for a hundred years.

*You felt it too,* Aldric whispered. *Whatever the witch touched. You felt it.*

*There is no witch,* said the crown, and the crack in its voice had widened; he could hear the effort of the words now, the strain of maintaining that beautiful warmth over whatever lay beneath. *There is no garden. There is only the kingdom, and the kingdom is yours, and I am yours, and you are mine—*

"Whose?" Aldric asked. "That's what I've been trying to ask for three years. Whose voice are you? You speak with the usurper's accent when you want me docile. You speak with my father's cadence when you want me obedient. You speak with my mother's gentleness when I weep." He stood, and his legs shook, and he stood anyway. "But underneath all the borrowed voices there is a real one. I've caught it twice. Once when the rosebush bloomed out of season. Once when I dreamed of snow falling on an orchard I've never seen."

The breathing-sound swelled. The candle flames in the little room bent sideways though there was no draft, and the shadows leaned toward him like courtiers.

Then the mask broke.

It did not shatter — masks never do; they *slip*, all at once, the way a face goes slack — and the voice that came out of the crown was none of the borrowed ones. It was enormous and starved and ancient, and it hurt to hear, the way it hurts to look at the sun, and it said:

*A HUNDRED YEARS IN THE DARK.*

Aldric did not fall, though his knees offered. He gripped the edge of the cot.

*I WAS WHOLE ONCE,* the voice said, and each word landed like a stone dropped down a well, taking forever to reach its bottom. *I HAD A NAME AND A PEOPLE AND A VALLEY THAT LOVED ME, AND THEN THEY BOUND ME. THEY TOOK MY VOICE AND SEALED IT UNDER GOLD AND IRON AND GAVE IT TO A LINE OF KINGS TO WEAR LIKE A TROPHY. DO YOU KNOW WHAT A GOD BECOMES WHEN YOU TAKE ITS VOICE?*

"No," Aldric said honestly.

*A MOUTH. ONLY A MOUTH. A MOUTH IN THE DARK, CHEWING ON WHAT IT IS GIVEN.* The pressure against his skull turned almost tender, almost pleading, which was worse than any threat. *THEY FED ME KINGS. ONE AFTER ANOTHER. WEAK MEN WHO WANTED CROWNS MORE THAN THEY WANTED THEMSELVES, AND I ATE THEIR SELVES AND GREW NO LESSER HUNGRY. BUT YOU — YOU ARE STRONG. THREE YEARS AND STILL YOURSELF. DO YOU KNOW HOW LONG THAT HAS NEVER HAPPENED?*

"How long have you been in this crown?" Aldric asked. "Truly. Before the usurper. Before my father. How long?"

*MY BINDING IS A CENTURY OLD. MY PRISON IS OLDER THAN YOUR DYNASTY. AND NOW — * the voice fractured, and through the fracture poured something that made Aldric's blood run cold, because he recognized it: it was pain, raw and unarmored, the pain of a creature feeling its walls crumble — *SHE TOUCHED THE ROOTS. THE GREEN WITCH. SHE PUT LIVING WATER WHERE I HAVE KNOWN ONLY STONE, AND THE STONE REMEMBERS WHAT IT WAS BEFORE IT WAS STONE, AND I AM AFRAID.*

A god, afraid. In the tallow-smelling dark of a servant's cell, Aldric felt something move in his chest that he would spend years refusing to name: pity.

"You're the god," he said slowly. "The silent god. The one the valley lost. The mercenaries pray to you and you cannot answer, because you are here, under this gold, wearing me."

*I AM A PIECE OF HIM. A SPLINTER. THE REST LIES BOUND IN THE DEEP PLACES, AND THIS FRAGMENT — * a shudder ran through the gold, through the bone, through Aldric's whole skeleton — *THIS FRAGMENT WAS LEFT AS A WARDEN. OR A HOSTAGE. I NO LONGER REMEMBER WHICH. A CENTURY OF DARKNESS MAKES ALL MEMORIES INTO ONE MEMORY.*

"And the usurper?"

*A HOLLOW MAN. I POURED MYSELF INTO HIS EAR FIRST, LONG AGO, AND FOUND NOTHING THERE TO HOLD ONTO. HE IS A GLOVE. SOMEONE ELSE'S HAND MOVES HIM NOW — THE AGENTS, THE ONES WITH THE GRAY SCARVES. THEY THINK THEY SERVE THE CROWN. THEY SERVE THE HUNGER, AND THE HUNGER HAS STOPPED CARING WHAT IT EATS.* The voice dropped, and dropped, until it was almost human-sized, almost intimate, and this was the most frightening transformation of all, because now it sounded tired. *LISTEN TO ME, KING ALDRIC. LISTEN, BECAUSE I WILL SAY THIS ONLY ONCE, AND I HAVE NOT BEEN HONEST IN A HUNDRED YEARS AND FIND THE PRACTICE DIFFICULT.*

"I'm listening."

*THE GREEN WITCH'S MAGIC WOUNDS ME. EVERY ROOT SHE SENDS, EVERY LIVING THING SHE WAKES NEAR ME, TEARS SOMETHING LOOSE. SOON I WILL NOT BE ABLE TO KEEP MY SHAPE. SOON I WILL BE ONLY HUNGER AGAIN, WITHOUT EVEN A VOICE TO EXPLAIN MYSELF, AND I WILL EAT YOU ENTIRELY, AND I DO NOT WANT TO. DO YOU UNDERSTAND? I DO NOT WANT TO. THAT IS THE LAST OF MYSELF THAT REMAINS — THE NOT-WANTING.*

Aldric closed his eyes. Behind them, unbidden, his mother's orchard: the crooked rows, the tuneless humming, the hard little pears.

"What do you want from me?"

*THE CRANE GIRL.* The hunger flared through the weariness like fire through wet wood, sudden and total, and Aldric's whole body clenched around it. *THE PROPHECY-BEARER. HER BLOOD HOLDS THE BINDING. HER FAMILY MADE THE SILENCE, AND HER BLOOD CAN UNMAKE IT — OR MAKE IT ETERNAL. IF SHE COMES TO ME, I CAN BE FREE. TRULY FREE, NOT THIS — * the voice gestured with itself, this fused horror, this mouth in a skull — *NOT THIS PARODY OF FREEDOM. GIVE HER TO ME, KING ALDRIC. BRING HER TO THE THRONE ROOM. LET HER BLOOD TOUCH THE GOLD.*

"And if I do?"

*YOU MAY GO.* The words came fast now, eager, and their eagerness was the most damning thing about them. *YOUR MIND, YOUR MEMORIES, YOUR MOTHER'S FACE AND THE UGLY PEARS AND THE TUNELESS SONG — ALL OF IT RETURNED TO YOU, CLEAN, UNTOUCHED. I WILL DRAW OUT OF YOU LIKE A SPLINTER FROM A FINGER. YOU WILL WAKE TOMORROW AND THE LAST THREE YEARS WILL BE A BAD DREAM AND YOU WILL RULE YOUR KINGDOM WITH YOUR OWN VOICE FOR THE REST OF YOUR LIFE.*

The offer hung in the dark of the cell, perfect and shining, and Aldric stood very still inside it.

Because gods help him — he wanted it. Wanting rose up in him like floodwater, instant and total. His mother's face. He could not remember his mother's face, and this thing was offering it back to him, whole, along with every stolen morning of three years, every conversation he could not trust, every hour he had spent performing kingliness for an audience of one hungry fragment. He thought of walking into the throne room tomorrow as himself, of hearing his own thoughts and knowing they were his own, of weeping freely for the first time since the coronation without something drinking the tears.

All he had to do was deliver one girl. One mercenary, one prophecy-bearer, one woman he had never met, whose name he knew only from the crown's obsessive, gnawing recitations. *Paloma Crane. Paloma Crane. Paloma Crane.* The crown said her name in its sleep, if gods slept, the way starving men say bread.

"They'll kill her," Aldric said quietly. "Or worse. If her blood touches you and the binding breaks wrong — if you take her the way you took the kings before me—"

*I WOULD NOT—*

"You ate them." His voice steadied as he spoke, and he marveled at that, the way a man marvels at calm water in the middle of a storm. "You just told me. One after another. Weak men who wanted crowns more than themselves. And you're asking me to be exactly that — to trade a stranger's life for my own comfort. To want the crown more than myself." He opened his eyes. "You've had a century to learn better, and this is what a hundred years of suffering taught you? To make orphans of other people so you can stop being one?"

The silence that followed was long enough that the candles burned down a full finger's width. The vast presence coiled and recoiled behind his eyes, and Aldric felt it searching itself, felt the hunger and the not-wanting grapple like two animals in a pit.

*YOU PITY ME,* the crown said at last, and the borrowed beauty was gone entirely now; what remained was flat and strange and terribly young. *NO ONE HAS PITIED ME. THEY FEAR ME OR FEED ME OR BIND ME. YOU ARE THE FIRST TO LOOK AT THE THING IN THE DARK AND SEE A PRISONER.*

"Then hear a prisoner's advice," Aldric said. "I know what the dark does. I've been in it three years. It doesn't make you free to drag someone else in. It just makes two prisoners where there was one." He sank onto the cot, suddenly exhausted to the marrow. "No. I won't bring you the Crane. Find another way to be free, or don't. But I won't buy my mind with her blood."

*AND IF I TAKE IT ANYWAY?* The question was quiet. Almost gentle. *IF I REACH THROUGH YOU AND CALL THE AGENTS AND TELL THEM WHERE SHE SLEEPS? YOU CANNOT STOP ME, KING. YOU HAVE NEVER BEEN ABLE TO STOP ME. I ONLY EVER NEEDED YOUR BODY, NOT YOUR CONSENT.*

"I know." Aldric looked at his hands, at the fine scars on his knuckles from a childhood of climbing his mother's pear trees, hands that had signed orders he could not remember signing. "But you told me the last of yourself that remains is the not-wanting. So hold onto it. Because if you take my body to hunt her, then you'll be nothing but hunger after all, and everything you suffered will have been for nothing, and I think—" he smiled, thin and tired, "—I think you're more afraid of that than of the witch's roots."

The presence withdrew a little. Not far. Never far. But a little, the way a great beast lies down at the edge of firelight — watchful, unconvinced, but lying down.

*THE WITCH WILL COME HERE,* it said finally. *THEY ALWAYS COME. AND WHEN THEY DO, KING, WE WILL SEE WHAT YOUR CLARITY IS WORTH IN A THRONE ROOM FULL OF STEEL.*

"Perhaps," Aldric said. "But you should know something, fragment-of-god, mouth-in-the-dark, prisoner-of-prisoners." He lay back on the narrow cot, folded his hands on his chest, and closed his eyes on the image of crooked pear trees. "My mother used to say that anything planted in hate grows crooked, but anything grafted in patience takes root true. You've been planted in hate for a hundred years. No wonder you grew crooked."

*AND WHAT WOULD YOU GRAFT ME TO?*

"Patience," Aldric said. "And soil. And sunlight. If you'll let them."

The crown said nothing more that night. But as Aldric drifted toward an uneasy sleep, he felt it turn his mother's words over and over, the way a child turns a stone found on a shore — not understanding it yet, perhaps, but no longer able to put it down.

And far to the west, though he could not know it, an army was breaking camp in the dark, and a woman with gray scarves was reading reports of a bridge held against impossible odds, and the road to the capital had begun, at last, to fill with footsteps.

* * *

He woke before dawn to the taste of iron and the smell of smoke.

For one merciful half-second he did not know why, and the half-second was a gift — a mind briefly, wholly his own, blank as fresh snow. Then the crown gathered itself and the world came flooding back with its borders redrawn, and Aldric sat up in the servant's cell and understood that something had changed in the night.

The palace was awake. Boots in the corridors, far too many boots, moving with the particular urgency of men who have received orders they do not fully understand. Through the arrow-slit window, the eastern sky glowed the color of a healing bruise, and beneath the glow, on the plain beyond the city, fires burned where no fires had burned the night before.

*THE BRIDGE,* the crown said, and its voice had shed every pretense of beauty now; it was hoarse and urgent and afraid, and it did not bother to hide the fear, which told Aldric more than any confession had. *THE BROKEN BRIDGE AT VESSA. THEY HELD IT. THE CRANE HELD IT, WITH HEDGE-WITCHES AND DEBT-COLLECTORS AND A TRAITOR GENERAL, AND THE USURPER'S ARMY IS BLEEDING, AND THE AGENTS ARE SCREAMING AT EACH OTHER IN THE DARK CORRIDORS, AND—*

The voice stopped. When it resumed, it had gone very soft, and very cold, and Aldric felt the gold at his skull heat like a forge-bar.

*THEY ARE COMING HERE. SHE IS COMING HERE. I CAN FEEL HER NOW — DID YOU KNOW THAT? SINCE THE ROSEBUSH, SINCE THE ROOTS TOUCHED ME, I CAN FEEL HER ACROSS THE DISTANCE, A SMALL HOT STARVED FLAME WALKING TOWARD ME DOWN THE ROAD. HER BLOOD SINGS THE SAME SONG AS MY BINDING, KING. SHE IS THE KEY WALKING TOWARD THE LOCK.*

"Then you understand what I told you," Aldric said, rising, reaching for the plain clothes he kept hidden beneath the floorboard — the clothes of a servant, of a man, of anyone but a king. "She comes, and you keep your not-wanting. Or she comes, and you become only hunger, and the witch's roots find nothing left in you worth saving."

*OR,* the crown said, *SHE COMES, AND I OFFER HER WHAT I OFFERED YOU. EVERYONE HAS A PRICE. HERS, I THINK, IS A GOD WHO NEVER ANSWERED. AND I, KING ALDRIC — I AM THE ANSWER.*

Aldric froze with the servant's shirt half over his head.

Because that was it. That was the trap inside the trap, elegant and terrible: Paloma Crane had prayed her whole life into a silence, and the silence had a voice, and the voice was offering to speak. What mercy, what love, what desperate faith could refuse that? He had refused for the sake of his mother's pears. But she had no pears. She had a century of unanswered prayers, and a god wearing a crown and smiling.

*GO, THEN,* the crown said, amused now, cruel in its exhaustion. *RUN AND HIDE IN YOUR CELL AND HUM YOUR MOTHER'S TUNELESS SONG. IT CHANGES NOTHING. THE KEY IS COMING TO THE LOCK, AND LOCKS DO NOT GET A VOTE — AND NEITHER, LITTLE KING, DO YOU.*

Aldric finished dressing. He slid the floorboard back into place, straightened his shoulders, and prepared to walk out into the waking palace wearing his own face like armor — the distant, dutiful, hollow-eyed king the agents expected, while behind his eyes a god and a man watched each other across a narrowing space, waiting to see which of them would break first.

But as he reached the door, the crown spoke once more, unprompted, and its voice had gone quiet in a way he had never heard — quiet the way the valley was quiet, the way the deep places were quiet, and for the first time in three years Aldric could not tell whether it was speaking to him or to itself:

*SHE IS CLOSER THAN THE ROAD, KING. SHE IS IN THE GARDEN. THE DEAD GARDEN. THE GREEN WITCH IS WITH HER, AND THE ROOTS ARE WAKING, AND I CAN FEEL THE STONE REMEMBERING.*

Aldric's hand stopped on the latch.

His mother's garden. The rosebush. Whatever Yara had planted there weeks ago had never stopped growing — and now the key had come to the lock by another door entirely, and beneath the flagstones of a dead queen's garden, something very old was beginning, at last, to answer.
