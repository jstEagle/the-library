## 19. What the Margins Hold

*Found folded into the false back of Edmund Hollis's desk, wrapped in oilcloth against the damp, and addressed in his careful surveyor's hand:*

*For Margaret. For the boy. For whoever outlives me.*

---

**The first letter — dated the ninth of March, 1848**

Margaret,

You will say I have taken to writing you letters when we sleep under the same roof, and you will be right, and you will also understand before the end of this page why I could not say these things aloud over supper. The spoken word evaporates. Ink stays where it is put. That has been my whole creed for forty years, and lately it has become my whole danger, so let me put it down plainly while my hand still obeys me.

I have begun keeping a list.

Not a survey, not an atlas proper — those are honest trades. A list. In the margins of the great work, in a cipher of my own devising that would embarrass a first-year apprentice if he ever caught it, which he must not, not yet, because Rosalind is a good boy with a straight spine and I love him too much to make him complicit before I know whether complicity will mean the gallows or merely ruin.

Here is what I know, written once and never again aloud: the displacements are not accidents of progress. Three towns now — Marrowgate, Ashcote Fen, and the hamlets along the Tern that no longer appear on any Ordnance sheet printed after 1844. I mapped Marrowgate myself in '41. Four hundred souls, a church, a school, a herring fleet worth more than half of Vellamouth. It was *there*, Margaret. I stood on its quay. And then it was gone from the record as though the sea had been asked politely to swallow it, and the men who asked were not the sea but the Crown's own commissioners, and the land it stood on is now leased to parties whose names I am still gathering, one at a time, like a man picking shingle off a beach.

The refugees come through Vellamouth because we are the last port before the marsh roads end. They arrive with nothing but their memories, and memories are the one cargo no requisition officer knows how to weigh. So they tell me their family names, their neighbours' names, the numbers of their hearths, the dates the soldiers came, and I write it all down in the margins where only another mapmaker would think to look — in the flourishes of coastlines, in the stippling of dunes, in the little compass roses that decorate the corners of plates. A compass rose with thirty-two points can hold thirty-two letters if you know the convention. I have invented conventions enough to hold a whole county of ghosts.

Why am I telling you this in a letter instead of in our bed?

Because if they come for me, they will search the house for papers and find none, because there are none to find — the atlas will be where no man thinks to look, and this letter, this plain honest letter, will tell you where the key to the convention lives: in your wedding ring, love. Sixteen letters of my name and yours, in the order we spoke our vows. You always said the marriage had made us one text. Now it has literally done so, and you may laugh at me across the kitchen table tomorrow and I will deny everything.

But do not laugh until you have read this twice. And do not let anyone read it who does not already carry our whole life in their chest.

Your E.

---

* * *

**The second letter — undated, water-stained**

M.,

A woman came to the door tonight with a child asleep against her shoulder and frost in her hair, and I want to set down what she said before sleep dissolves it, though sleep will not dissolve it, I suspect, for forty years.

She was off one of the Tern hamlets — Brindle Staithe, the one the new charts show as a sandbank. She said: "They didn't burn us out, sir. Burning leaves evidence." She said they came with papers instead. Notices of sanitary necessity. Fever grounds declared, dwellings condemned, compensation offered at figures so low they were insults wearing arithmetic. And when folk refused, there was no fire, no violence, nothing so useful to a man like me. There was simply a second notice, and a third, and then one morning the ferry timetable had changed and the market charter had lapsed and the parish register had been removed to a county office two days' ride away, and within a season a town is not destroyed but merely *forgotten*, which is cheaper and cleaner and leaves no inquest to sit upon.

The child woke while she talked and looked about my study with eyes too old for its face, and I thought: there is the whole matter in miniature. That child has been subtracted. Not killed — subtracted. Removed from the ledger as one strikes a misprinted figure, and the sum of the realm continues without it, balanced and tidy.

I gave the woman bread and coin and took her testimony by candlelight, every name she could remember, and when she had gone I sat a long while looking at the atlas open on its stand, and I understood something I have been circling these many weeks:

Memory itself is resistance. Nothing grander than that.

They cannot hang a man for remembering, not yet, not openly. So long as one copy of the truth exists anywhere — in ink, in margins, in the mouth of a fisherman's widow reciting her neighbours like a rosary — then the erasure is incomplete, and an incomplete erasure can be reversed. A town struck from the record is dead only if no one records the striking. What I am making is not a book. It is a wound kept open on purpose, so that the body politic cannot pretend it never bled.

I am too old to run and too stubborn to hide. But I can write small, and I can write true, and I can die slow. Every man dies at his own pace if he refuses to hurry.

Do not fear for me yet. Fear is for afterwards, and afterwards has not arrived, whatever my cough says to the contrary.

E.

---

* * *

**The third letter — dated the twenty-first of April**

Margaret,

Rosalind nearly caught me tonight.

He came in for the tide tables and found the atlas open to the Ashcote Fen plate, and he leaned over my shoulder the way he does, all that young attention bent like light through a lens, and he said: "Master, your dune stippling is wrong here. Too regular. It reads like writing."

It reads like writing, Margaret, because it *is* writing, and my apprentice has the eye I prayed he'd have and the innocence I prayed he'd keep, both at once, and God help me I lied to him. I told him my hand grows unsteady and repeats itself. He believed me because he loves me, which is the most dangerous currency in the world and the only one I trade in anymore.

Should I tell him?

I turn it over nightly like a stone, looking for what lives underneath. If I tell him and they take me, he becomes a witness, and witnesses to this sort of thing do not die of old age. If I do not tell him and they take me, the atlas may pass to him unremarked — margins and all — and he will discover it later, alone, grieving, angry. Anger is a fuel that burns the vessel holding it. But grief plus purpose, that is a different fire. That is a forge fire. It shapes things.

I have watched him trace coastlines by candlelight these three years and I know what is in him. His people crossed an ocean, Margaret — you told me yourself what his mother carried from her own country, how she taught the children that a person who loses their stories has been robbed twice. He understands, in his bones, that a map is never neutral. Every map is an argument about what deserves to exist. The Crown's maps argue that some places deserve to vanish. Mine argues back.

No. Not mine. Ours. I have begun to think of it as ours whether he knows it or not, the way a father thinks of a son's inheritance before the son is grown.

I will not tell him yet. But I will begin leaving the door of this room unlocked, and I will stop hiding the key in the flour bin like a man with something to conceal, and I will let chance and his own sharp eye do what my cowardice will not. If he finds it, he finds it. If he asks, I will answer. I find I lack the courage for anything more deliberate, and I confess it here because a letter is a confessional that keeps its records.

E.

---

* * *

**The fourth letter — dated the third of May**

M.,

They came today. Not soldiers — worse. Gentlemen.

Two of them, from the Office of Works, very civil, boots wiped carefully at the door. They admired the atlas. They said the Crown wished to acquire it for the national collection, and named a price that would have bought us the house twice over, and smiled the way men smile when the outcome is already decided and the negotiation is merely a politeness owed to the form of things.

I told them the atlas was promised to my heirs.

One of them stopped smiling, just slightly, just at the corner, and looked around my study the way a buyer looks around a shop whose stock he intends to acquire more cheaply later. He looked at the chart drawers. He looked at the binding of the atlas on its stand. I watched him *read the room*, Margaret, the way I read a coastline — noting depths, hazards, channels of approach — and I understood that the visit had not been an offer. It had been a survey. They were mapping me.

After they left I sat down and did the bravest thing I have ever done, which was nothing. I made tea. I traced a plate of the Dorset coast that has needed finishing since 1838. I let the evening be ordinary, because ordinary is camouflage now, and the moment this household stops being ordinary we are all in the open.

But I will tell you what I did not tell you at supper, because you were tired and the stew had taken three hours and I would not spoil it: the gentleman at the door said a thing as he left. He said, "Mr. Hollis, history happens elsewhere, to other people. Assist it, and it will happen kindly."

Elsewhere. Other people. As if Marrowgate were elsewhere. As if the four hundred souls of it were other people. As if the woman from Brindle Staithe and her frost-haired child were not sleeping tonight in some barn not ten miles from our bed, breathing the same fog.

That is the empire's whole theology in one sentence, love: history happens elsewhere. And that is the whole lie, because history is a tide, and tides do not consult harbours before they rise. It is happening here now. It has been happening here for years, and I am done pretending the water is someone else's weather.

If they take the atlas they take the names. If they take the names, the towns stay dead forever. So the atlas must leave this room before they return, and soon, and I must seem to remain, sitting quietly, tracing my Dorset coast, an old man with a cough and an unsteady hand.

Keep the ring on your finger. Do not polish it. Fingerprints in the wax of memory are exactly what we want.

E.

**The fifth letter — dated the seventeenth of May, six days before his death**

Margaret,

Short tonight. The hand is bad and the hour is late and there is a man watching the lane from the churchyard wall who has been there three evenings running, and one does not write long letters under observation even in cipher.

Three things.

First: Finch. Tobias writes from the marsh that his press survived the seizure of his shop — the frame buried in a peat boat, type in barrels under his sister's floor. He can print, given clean copy and time. He asks, delicately, whether the work is far enough advanced to be worth the risk of his neck coming back within reach of the assizes. I have written back: *soon*. Whether soon means a fortnight or a season depends on how many more refugees find this door, and on how fast my traitor hand can hold its line.

Second: the boy. I have decided, and you will not talk me out of it, so save your breath for colder nights. When it is time, the atlas goes where no searcher looks and no thief steals, which is into plain sight — into the hands of my apprentice, openly, as a master's legacy to be finished. Let them watch me hand my work to my student. What crime is there in that? An old man completing his atlas through his heir. The convention will travel inside his skill rather than beside it; he will finish plates in my style, add margins in my method, and never know until the day comes — or know only dimly, the way one knows a thing in the hands before the head admits it. I am gambling that Bray's kind look at craftsmanship and see only craftsmanship. They see what they expect: a trade, not a testimony.

Third: I have been thinking about what I will have left undone, and I find the list is short, and none of it regrets. Forty years of coastlines, Margaret, and I never once drew a lie into a chart, not even a flattering one, not even for a harbourmaster's fee. I have measured honestly, recorded faithfully, and now, at the end, I am doing the only truly original thing of my career: I am making a map that argues. If the price of the argument is that I shall not see it win — well. The herring I ate tonight swam in seas older than every grievance in this kingdom, and it did not concern itself with outcomes either.

Tell me tomorrow that the garden wants turning, and that my cough sounds better, and that I am a foolish old man. Lie to me kindly, wife. Tomorrow I shall need the strength that only being loved foolishly provides.

Ever yours,
E.

P.S. — The watching man has moved to the quay. Note that in your memory, Margaret, and keep it. Whatever story they build afterward, you will know the shape of its foundations, because you saw the scaffolders first.

**The sixth letter — begun the twenty-third of May, unfinished**

M. —

They have asked me to attend the fort tomorrow. "To advise on coastal surveys," says the note, in a secretary's fair hand, which is how power signs itself when it wishes to remain deniable.

I am not so old as to mistake a summons for an invitation, nor so vain as to believe my advice is wanted. What is wanted is my submission, and failing that, my absence — permanent, quiet, medically explicable. An old man with a cough. Men of my age die conveniently and often, and the times are such that no coroner will look hard at a natural death in a season when half the county is said to be feverish anyway.

So. Last pages, then, and I shall spend them as I spent the first ones: on instruction, because a teacher's last lesson should be his best.

Hear me, whoever holds this — boy, wife, stranger, magistrate, God forgive me, even the King's own men if the wheel turns so far:

You will be told that records are kept to preserve order. This is true the way a cage is built to preserve birds. Every ledger, every census, every fair-handed chart is also an instrument of forgetting, because to record some things is necessarily to crowd out others, and the men who decide what fills the page decide what the future will believe existed. They burned libraries once, when burning was fashionable. Now they only edit. The result is identical and the hands stay clean.

Therefore: write. Write in margins, in codes, in the white space between printed lines, in the patience of a widow's memory and the stubbornness of an apprentice's hand. Write names — above all write *names*, for a number can be reissued but a name, once spoken into permanence, belongs to someone, and someone can be grieved, and grief is the one force no commission can levy against.

They will call it sedition. Let them. Sedition against what? Against a fiction — the fiction that history happens elsewhere. The truth is that history happens *here*, in this room, at this desk, under this hand, and it is happening to *us*, and the only question any of us answers in this life is whether we were present for it or merely nearby.

I have been present. It has cost me my sleep, my breath, and shortly, I suspect, considerably more. I do not regret a stroke of it. The margins hold, Margaret. Everything that matters has always lived in the margins — the poor, the drowned, the forgotten, the wives, the apprentices, the names too small for the official plates. And margins, unlike empires, do not fall. They are simply waiting for someone to read them.

Finish the—

*The sentence breaks off mid-word, the penstroke trailing into a blot, as though the writer were interrupted. Beneath it, in a later hand — smaller, steadier, and not his own:*

*Rosalind came at seven o'clock the next morning and found the study door locked from within, which it had never once been in thirty-one years.*

---

* * *

Margaret Hollis sat in the darkened parlour with six letters spread across her knees like a hand of cards she had been dealt by a dead man, and outside, far down toward the harbour, the troop transports rode at anchor with their lamps lit, patient as creditors.

She read the last unfinished line again. *Finish the—*

Then she folded the letters along their old creases, put them where her husband had trusted her to keep them, and went to the window to watch the ship that meant to carry the town away — and began, very calmly, to plan what she would tell Rosalind in the morning, and how much of it, and whether the boy was ready to learn what his master had died believing: that the margin was waiting for him now, and the pen with it.
