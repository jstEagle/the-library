## 17. Crossing Fire  

The bridge stretched ahead like a jagged tooth of iron and flame, the wooden planks already singed where the garrison’s muzzle‑flash had caught the rigging. Petra could feel the heat on her skin even before the first shot cracked the air, a thin, sharp sting that made the back of her neck prickle. She tightened her grip on the dispatch case, its brass seals still warm from the night’s journey, and pressed it tighter against her chest.  

The toll house loomed like a gutted beast, its roof a collapsed honeycomb of charred timber and ash. Smoke curled from its broken windows, thick enough to obscure the sky above the estuary, turning the heavens into a bruised violet. The garrison’s flag—blood‑red with the republic’s emblem—flapped limply in the wind, a parody of authority.  

Petra’s eyes flicked to Sera’s shoulder, where the Vane daughter stood, her own coat dark with soot, her hands clenched around a small, weather‑worn journal. Sera’s gaze was fixed on the bridge’s far edge, where the garrison’s sentries leaned over the railing, their rifles trained like predators.  

“Ready?” Petra whispered, though the words sounded hollow against the crackling fire.  

Sera nodded, a thin line of determination tracing her jaw. “We’ve come too far for this to end now.”  

Petra forced a smile, the muscles in her face burning from the effort. “Then we move.”  

She turned back to the case, feeling the weight of its sealed contents pressing against her spine. The dispatches had been the purpose of every step she’d taken—delivered from the capital’s halls to the enemy’s daughter’s estate, now to be taken across this bridge and beyond the Cape Route. The promise she’d made to Aldric Vane, now a ghost of a man, still thrummed beneath her ribs, a quiet drum that matched the beating of her heart.  

The garrison’s commander, a broad‑shouldered man with a scar across his left cheek, stepped forward, his voice reverberating over the river’s roar. “Halt! Who goes there?” he bellowed, his rifle’s barrel already trained on the bridge’s midpoint.  

Petra’s breath caught. The garrison had been expecting them, the decoy half‑working as Ivo Marsh had arranged. The decoy—Marsh himself—had moved ahead, using his census authority to claim the toll house was being evacuated. He’d been ordered to draw the garrison’s fire, to create a window for Petra and Sera to slip through. The plan had been delicate, a dance of lies and timing, but now the music was breaking.  

Marsh appeared on the far side of the toll house, his silhouette stark against the orange glow. He raised his hands, the census ledger he carried clutched to his chest, and shouted, “Ministry orders! The bridge is to be cleared for the convoy!”  

The garrison’s commander barked an order, his men’s rifles clicking as they trained on Marsh. A single shot cracked, the bullet slicing the air just above Marsh’s head, a spray of gravel and smoke erupting where it struck the stone. The garrison opened fire, a barrage of rounds that sprayed the bridge like a storm of sparks.  

Petra felt the bridge shudder beneath her boots, the wooden planks groaning under the weight of the onslaught. The case banged against her side, a steady thud that matched the rhythm of her racing heart. She could see the garrison’s fire blooming in arcs, each flash a brief seizure of light before the smoke swallowed it. The bridge itself seemed to inhale and exhale with the barrage, the fire licking at its edges, turning the iron cables into rivers of molten gold.  

She turned to Sera, whose eyes were wide but steady. “We have to keep moving,” she said, voice strained.  

Sera’s jaw tightened. “We can’t leave the case. It’s everything.”  

Petra nodded, though she felt the case’s weight pulling her down, each step a negotiation with gravity and fear. The bridge’s railing was slick with blood and ash from previous skirmishes, and the garrison’s fire had turned the air into a furnace of heat and debris. She could smell burnt gunpowder, the acrid tang of fire, and the faint, metallic scent of rain on salt—still present despite the inferno.  

She took a breath, the cool night air of the estuary rushing in through the smoke, and forced herself to focus on the rhythm of her steps. Each footfall was a deliberate strike against the chaos, a small assertion of control in a world that was crumbling around her.  

The garrison’s fire intensified, the bridge now a living thing, thrashing under the barrage. Petra felt the case’s leather edges heating, the brass seals beginning to glow with an inner fire. She could have sworn the dispatches were breathing, the weight of truth pressing against the pages as if they were alive.  

She glanced at Marsh, who stood defiantly, his ledger raised as if to shield himself from the bullets. He shouted something over the din, his voice cracking like ice. “The census! The names! They’ll be erased if you don’t stop!”  

Petra’s heart sank. The census, the list of the disappeared, the weaponized bureaucracy that had turned her own life into a ledger of lies. She could feel the weight of those names pressing against her ribs, each one a silent witness to the republic’s decay.  

She forced herself to push past the terror, to remember why she was here. The dispatches, the truth about the ministry’s fabrication of Vane’s treason, the proof that would dismantle the faction that had turned her country into a ghost town. She could not let that be lost in the flames.  

“Keep moving,” she hissed to Sera, pulling the case tighter. “We have to get to the other side.”  

Sera nodded, her journal clutched tighter, her eyes scanning the bridge for any sign of a safe passage. The garrison’s fire was a relentless tide, but there was a gap—a section of the bridge that had already been weakened by previous bombardments, a wooden span that sagged under the weight of the fire but still held.  

Petra vaulted over the gap, her boots finding the cracked planks, the case swinging dangerously but staying attached to her shoulder. The impact of the jump sent a shock through her body, but she forced herself forward, the adrenaline overriding the pain.  

Sera followed, her stride shorter but determined, her journal slipping from her grasp as she clutched the case. The book fell with a soft thud onto the bridge’s edge, its pages fluttering like moths caught in a sudden gust.  

Petra turned, half‑expecting a bullet to tear through the air, but the garrison’s fire was now focused on the toll house, the decoy’s sacrifice having drawn the majority of the shots. The bridge’s midpoint was momentarily clear, a narrow window of opportunity.  

She reached the other side, the case still intact, the dispatches still sealed, the truth still untouched. The garrison’s fire continued to rage, the bridge now a river of fire and smoke, the toll house collapsing in a final, spectacular burst of orange and black.  

Petra looked back, the bridge behind her a monstrous inferno, the garrison’s flag now a blackened rag flapping in the wind. She could see Marsh’s silhouette, his ledger still raised, his face a mask of resolve. He had held the line, buying them precious seconds, his own life likely forfeit.  

She turned to Sera, who stood trembling but unbroken, the journal now lost among the ashes. “We made it,” Petra breathed, her voice cracking with relief and grief.  

Sera nodded, tears streaming down her cheeks, her grief mingled with the exhaustion of the journey. “We did,” she whispered, her voice barely audible over the roar of flames.  

Petra felt the case’s weight shift, the dispatches now fully within her grasp, the promise finally within reach. She placed a hand on Sera’s shoulder, feeling the tremor of her sorrow, and felt a strange calm settle over her. The adrenaline that had fueled her flight now softened, replaced by a quiet certainty.  

The garrison’s fire began to wane as the bridge’s structure gave way, the final shots sputtering out as the wooden span collapsed into the river below, a dark plume of smoke rising like a funeral pyre. The toll house, the garrison’s flag, the entire outpost vanished in a burst of ash and sound, leaving only the echo of their footsteps and the distant cries of the fallen.  

Petra stood on the far bank, the dispatch case now a solitary beacon against the dying sky. The sun, already setting, painted the clouds in shades of bruised purple and gold, as if the heavens themselves were mourning the fall of the republic.  

She looked out over the water, the bridge’s remnants smoldering in the distance, the fire now a distant memory. The weight of the dispatches still pressed against her, but it was a different kind of weight now—one of purpose rather than burden.  

Petra turned her gaze back toward the estate where Sera had lived, the coastal home now a silhouette against the fading light. She could hear the distant sound of the ministry’s sweep, the relentless counting and confiscating that had haunted her every step. She knew that the next chapter would be one of exposure and retribution, that the truth she carried would ignite a fire far beyond the bridge’s collapse.  

She felt a sudden, sharp pang of survivor's guilt as she thought of Marsh, of Corven, of the countless names erased from the census ledger. Their sacrifices were now part of the weight she bore, a burden she could not set down.  

Petra took a deep breath, the salt air filling her lungs, and felt the weight of the case settle into a steady rhythm with her heartbeat. She knew that the bridge she had just crossed was not merely a physical structure but a metaphor for the path she had chosen—a path that would lead her into exile, into the Cape Route, into the unknown future of the truth she had carried.  

She turned, her eyes meeting Sera’s, both women standing at the edge of the water, the dispatch case now a shared burden, a shared promise. The fire had burned the bridge behind them, but it had also burned away the lies that had held them captive.  

Petra extended her hand, not in greeting but in a silent acknowledgment of what they had both endured. “We’ll see this through,” she said, her voice soft but resolute.  

Sera’s eyes glistened, and she nodded, a small smile forming on her lips. “Together,” she replied, the words carrying the weight of all the lives they had touched.  

The bridge behind them now a blackened scar on the coastline, the fire already dying to embers, Petra felt a surge of purpose. The next step would be to deliver the dispatches to the ports beyond, to ensure the truth would not be buried with the republic’s ashes.  

She turned her back on the dying bridge, the horizon stretching before her, the Cape Route beckoning, the unknown ahead waiting. The fire had been a crucible, but now the real trial began.  

Petra tightened her grip on the case, feeling the sealed dispatches pulse against her palm, a promise kept and a truth about to be unveiled.  

She took the first step onto the path that would lead her into exile, the weight of the case now a beacon rather than a burden, the bridge’s flames a distant echo of a world that was falling.  

The next chapter would be the toll house’s fall, the ministry’s sweep, and the final reckoning that would decide whether the republic’s secrets would be laid bare or buried forever.
