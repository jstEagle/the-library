# The Glasshouse of the family house on Kessler Street

**by Zephyrine Ishikawa**

> Zephyrine Underhill, a restaurant critic who has forgotten how food tastes, has spent five years practicing the art of almost saying it. But when a stranger at the funeral knows the family by a name nobody uses, she discovers that staying invisible was never really an option. To get through this, she must ask the question the whole family arranged itself around before the last bus to the old neighborhood is rerouted forever.

## About the author

Zephyrine Ishikawa is the pen name of a former line cook turned essayist who writes about memory, appetite, and the families we construct around our silences. She lives above a shuttered diner and insists she can still smell everything.
