# The Late Aftermath

**by Odile Ishikawa**

> Lena Okafor, a translator losing a childhood language one borrowed word at a time, has spent twelve years practicing the art of almost saying it. Then a song on the radio is one that was written and never recorded — and she is pulled straight into the middle of it. Now she must forgive the stranger in the old photographs, and time is already running out.

## About the author

Odile Ishikawa is a former interpreter who grew up between three languages and two continents, and whose essays on translation have appeared in several literary quarterlies. The Late Aftermath is her second novel, written over four years in Lagos, London, and a rented room above a record shop in Ibadan.
