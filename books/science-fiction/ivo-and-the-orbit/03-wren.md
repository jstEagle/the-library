## 3. Wren

The summons came through at 0400 station time, and Wren Okafor read it three times before the words stopped rearranging themselves.

*Subject: Ivo Marchetti. Fraud, identity falsification, conspiracy to divert insurance assets. Apprehend and transport.*

She knew that name the way you know a scar—by touch, in the dark, without looking. Ten years since she'd heard it spoken aloud, and here it was in an enforcement directive with her own authorization code attached, as if the universe had decided to be cruel efficiently.

Her cabin aboard the cutter *Meridian's Edge* was four meters of recycled air and one porthole showing the slow rotation of Ceres dockyard lights. She sat on the edge of her bunk in the dark and let the name sit in her chest like swallowed ice.

Ivo Marchetti had died ten years ago. Officially. Unofficially, Wren had helped bury him—had stood in a registry office on Ganymede while Jonah signed the papers with a hand that shook only once, and had said nothing, because loving someone meant carrying what they couldn't.

Now someone was wearing the name like a stolen coat, and the Bureau wanted it brought in.

*Apprehend and transport.* As if Ivo Marchetti were a person who could be found. As if the ghost hadn't been laid to rest by the only two people who ever mourned him.

Wren dressed, braided her hair back tight, and did not think about the last time she'd seen Jonah Marchetti—on a loading platform, rain hissing off the heat exchangers, both of them pretending the goodbye was professional. *Stay dead,* she'd said. *Stay gone.* And Jonah had nodded and walked into the crowd and become paperwork.

She keyed the comm. "Okafor. I'm taking the Marchetti file."

A pause on the other end. "You're aware there's a conflict flag? Prior association."

"I'm aware." Her voice came out level, which surprised her. "That's why I'm taking it. Nobody else knows what to look for."

Another pause, longer. Then: "Approved. Auditor Voss wants updates every twelve hours."

Voss. The name landed wrong, some instinct she couldn't name yet. She filed it away and pulled up the case manifest, and there it was again—the *Calypso Vein*, a ship decommissioned before it ever touched vacuum, flying routes on paper that no engine had ever burned. Ghost tonnage. Ghost cargo. Ghost payouts routing somewhere toward Halcyon Relay, out past the last charted beacon.

And at the center of it, a signature she knew better than her own handwriting.

Jonah's hand had changed in ten years. The letters were smaller now, more compressed, like someone writing to fit into a narrow life. But the way the *M* hooked—that was still there. That was still Jonah.

Wren closed the file and pressed her palms against her eyes until she saw static.

"Okay," she said to the empty cabin. "Okay. I'm coming."

* * *

Jonah felt the surveillance before they saw it—a pressure at the back of the neck, the particular silence of a dock where too many people were pretending not to look. They finished logging the cargo manifest for the *Tamarind*, a grain hauler bound for the Belt, and kept their hands steady while the hair on their arms stood up.

Ten years of being nobody taught you the weather of being watched.

They made it two corridors toward their berth before the voice caught up with them.

"Investigator Marchetti."

Not *Ivo*. Not the name from the files. *Investigator Marchetti*—their working name, their cover, delivered in a voice Jonah would have known blindfolded, underwater, at the bottom of a gravity well.

They turned.

Wren Okafor looked exactly like the version of her that lived behind Jonah's eyelids, and nothing like her at all. Older. Harder at the jaw. Enforcement grays instead of the salvage-rig leathers she used to wear, and a sidearm on her hip that sat there like it belonged, like it had been there long enough to forget it was ever a choice. Her braid was regulation-tight. Her eyes were doing something complicated that her face refused to participate in.

"Bureau Officer Okafor." Jonah kept it flat. Kept it professional. Kept it anywhere but where it actually lived. "Strange jurisdiction for you."

"The file has a conflict flag." Wren stopped two meters away—interview distance, arrest distance, everything except the distance they used to stand at. "I requested it."

"You requested it." Jonah let that hang. "Bold."

"It was going to land on someone's desk. Better mine than Voss's."

"And is that why? Or do you just like collecting old debts?"

Something flickered across her face—hurt, maybe, or its well-armored cousin. "I need to ask you about the *Calypso Vein*."

"There's no *Calypso Vein*. It never flew."

"That's the point." Wren glanced down the corridor—empty, but Jonah clocked how she checked, how she'd learned to check too. "Its manifest carries your name. Your real one. Insurance payouts are cycling through shell brokers and terminating at accounts feeding Halcyon Relay. Someone built a fraud around a dead man, Jonah, and either you're doing it—"

"You know I'm not."

"—or someone wants the Bureau to think you are. Which puts me in the position of having to bring in a suspect who doesn't exist, in front of an auditor whose questions I don't like." Her voice stayed level, but Jonah heard the crack running under it, the same fault line that had always been there, the one she papered over with procedure. "So help me. Tell me something true."

Jonah looked at her. Ten years of rehearsed indifference, and it lasted about four seconds.

"The signature on the manifest is mine," they said quietly. "Or close enough. Someone studied my hand. Someone who had access to old registry archives, old claims files—" They stopped. Watched her understand it.

"Someone with Bureau access," Wren said.

"Or someone who sold to people who have it." Jonah stepped closer, and Wren didn't step back, and for one dangerous moment they were just two people in a corridor with a decade between them and no airlock rated for that kind of pressure. "Wren. If you file your report, Voss owns this investigation by end of shift. If you don't, you're burning your career on a ghost."

"I've burned things for ghosts before."

The words landed harder than she'd aimed them, and Jonah saw her realize it a half-second too late. Saw her jaw set against whatever came next.

"Wren—"

"Don't." She raised a hand—not a weapon, not quite a wall. "Don't do the thing where you decide what I can afford. You did that ten years ago. It went great."

"I did it so you could keep your life."

"I did fine," she said, and it was almost true, and they both heard the almost. "Look. Twelve hours. That's my reporting window. After that, Voss escalates, and this stops being an investigation and starts being a manhunt. Find out who's wearing your name before I have to arrest them—or you."

"And if I can't?"

Wren held their eyes. Everything unsaid sat right there in the space between them, ten years deep, neither of them willing to dive.

"Then I do my job," she said. "It's all I've got left that works."

She turned and walked away, boots ringing on the deck plating, and Jonah stood in the corridor breathing like they'd surfaced from deep water.

* * *

Twelve hours later, Wren sat in the *Meridian's Edge*'s cramped comms alcove and listened to Voss's voice come through clean and cold from the corporate relay.

"Your suspect was observed boarding the *Tamarind* berth complex at 0417," Voss said. "Then leaving through cargo customs at 0930. Where were your interviews conducted, Officer Okafor?"

"On neutral ground. Standard practice."

"Standard practice," Voss repeated, tasting the words. "Be advised—we've flagged Marchetti's financials. If they attempt to leave the station, we'll consider flight confirmation of guilt. I trust you'll be positioned accordingly."

The channel closed. Wren sat in the hum of the ship's systems and stared at the case file glowing on her console—Jonah's forged signature, the *Calypso Vein*'s impossible route, the money crawling toward Halcyon like blood toward a wound.

Flight confirmation of guilt. Which meant if Jonah ran, Voss didn't need evidence anymore. Which meant either Jonah was already trapped—

—or Voss needed them trapped, and fast, before anyone looked too closely at where the money actually went.

Wren thought about the corridor. About *tell me something true*, and the four seconds it took Jonah's armor to crack, and the way they'd said *you know I'm not* like it was the one fact in the universe they'd never doubted.

Her console chimed. Dock authority alert: unregistered thruster burn, berth 44, the *Tamarind*'s slip—no, not the *Tamarind*. A private hauler, cold-starting engines it had no scheduled departure for.

Berth 44. Jonah's berth.

Wren was moving before the thought finished forming, grabbing her jacket, keying the cutter's preflight, telling herself it was procedure, pursuit protocol, a suspect attempting flight.

Telling herself the thing she'd gotten good at telling herself for ten years.

The *Meridian's Edge* dropped its moorings ninety seconds later, chasing a ghost out into the dark—and behind them, quiet as a knife sliding free, another ship lit its drives and followed.
