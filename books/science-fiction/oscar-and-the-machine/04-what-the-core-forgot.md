## 4. What the Core Forgot

The summons came through the colony's ancient intercom at 0600, a voice like gravel poured through a speaker cone: *Administrator Reyes requests the presence of Inspector Thackeray. Administrative Building. 0800.*

*Inspector.* Not Jude. Not even *the clone* — which was what half the settlement called him behind closed doors, and what the other half called him to his face. Reyes was being precise. On Meridian, titles were tools, and Reyes sharpened his.

The Administrative Building was the tallest structure in the settlement, which meant it was four stories and had working heat on two of them. Jude climbed the stairs past peeling posters from the war years — *MERIDIAN FEEDS THE FRONT* above a painting of grain barges that had never existed — and into an office that smelled of real coffee, imported, absurd, obscene.

Drellan Reyes sat behind a desk that had been cleared of everything except a single slate and a cup. He was smaller than his voice, a compact man in a charcoal uniform pressed sharp enough to draw blood, with the kind of face that had been designed by committee to be forgotten. He did not stand. He did not offer coffee.

"Thackeray." He said the name the way a man tests a floorboard he suspects is rotten. "Sit."

Jude sat. Oscar's memories supplied the room's history unprompted: this office had belonged to Administrator Cho, who had drowned in the reservoir in the second winter. Before that, it had been a school. The Core had repurposed it during the evacuation scare and never repurposed it back. Meridian was full of rooms like that — things that had been something else once, and no one left alive who remembered what.

"You've been here nine days," Reyes said. "You've interviewed forty-one colonists, reopened a murder file the previous administration closed in eleven days, and requested shipping manifests going back six years." He folded his hands. "I'm going to ask you a question, and I want you to hear it as a courtesy and not a threat. What are you looking for?"

"Oscar Thackeray's killer."

"Wrong answer." Reyes slid the slate across the desk. On it: a form. *Case Status: Unresolved. Recommendation: Administrative Closure.* And beneath it, a second document, longer, dense with legalese. *Meridian Colony — Decommission and Withdrawal Order. Signature of senior Core representative on-world required.*

"Sign both," Reyes said, "and you go home on the next transport. Full pay. A commendation, even. The Core appreciates thorough work, and you've been thorough. The file will note the investigation was inconclusive due to environmental factors. Glacial instability. It happens."

"Environmental factors." Jude looked at the slate without touching it. "He was shot twice in the chest at close range on a glacier."

"Glaciers are unstable."

"Who killed him?"

Reyes smiled. It was a good smile. Jude could see why the Core had sent him — a smile like that could close a colony or open a casket, whichever the paperwork required. "Nobody. That's the finding. Nobody, because there is no evidence of a person. There is a dead man, a missing sidearm, and a colony full of people with grief and grudges and nothing else. You inherited a cold case, Inspector. The decent thing — the *loyal* thing — is to let it stay cold."

There it was. *Loyal.* Jude felt the word land somewhere old, somewhere the memories weren't his but the reflexes were. Oscar had been Core. Twenty-two years of service. Oscar had believed in the institution the way some men believe in gravity — not because it was kind, but because it held everything down.

Jude had those beliefs in his head. He could feel them like fillings in his teeth. And he could feel something else too, newer, growing around them like frost: the widow's data chip in his pocket, warm from his body heat, and the coordinates on it, and the fact that Oscar had died nine kilometers out on the ice shelf with a sidearm he hadn't drawn.

"I'll consider it," Jude said.

"Consider it tonight." Reyes stood, finally, and came around the desk, and for a moment he was almost warm. Almost human. "Thackeray, I'll tell you something I don't tell people. I've decommissioned four colonies. Do you know what they all had in common? Someone who wouldn't sign. Someone who loved the place too much to let it die with dignity. And do you know what those colonies are now?" He spread his hands. "Nothing. Names on a list. The Core forgets, Inspector. That's not cruelty. That's *hygiene*. Don't be the thing the Core has to clean off Meridian."

Jude walked back through the cold streets with the unsigned slate in his pocket and Reyes' voice in his ears, and it took him until the third intersection to realize what was wrong with the conversation.

Reyes hadn't asked what Oscar had found.

He'd had nine days of Jude's requests to wonder about it. He'd read the same files. And not once had he asked *what did he find out there?* Not once. A man doesn't avoid a question unless he already knows the answer.

*

The colony archive was in the basement of the medical center, which was fitting, because the records were sick. Jude had a clearance level that came with Oscar's skull, and for nine days he'd been using it for the murder case. Tonight he used it for something else.

He pulled Meridian's wartime service record. Or he tried to.

The file existed. He could see its index entry, its size, its date range — the whole war, eleven years of it, Meridian had been a supply hub and a fleet anchorage, the *Thornfield* and the *Cassiopeia's Wake* and the *Ardent Meridian* herself, the colony ship that had given the world its name, all of them logged, all of them homeported here.

Every record in the file was sealed.

Not classified — Jude had seen classified, and classified was a lock with a key somewhere. This was different. This was a seal, a Core-wide archival freeze, stamped with an authority code he'd never seen in twenty-two years of Oscar's memories, which meant it came from above Oscar's entire world. *Sealed by order of the Continuity Office. Access: none. Duration: indefinite.*

The freeze date was logged to the hour.

Jude sat in the dark of the archive with the terminal's glow on his face and did the arithmetic twice, because the first time he didn't believe it.

The seal had gone into effect at 0340, Meridian local time, on the fourteenth of Frostmonth.

Oscar Thackeray's body had been found at 0515 the same morning, on the ice shelf, nine kilometers out.

He pulled the coroner's estimate. Time of death: between 0200 and 0400.

Oscar had died inside the seal window. Not after it. Not before it. Inside it — within hours, maybe minutes, of the moment the Core locked away eleven years of Meridian's history and threw away the key.

Jude's hands were steady. That was the strange part. Oscar's hands had never shaken, not during the evacuation of Halcyon Reach, not during the bombardment watch, not ever, and apparently the hands came with the memories.

He thought: *coincidence.* He thought it the way you test a floorboard. It did not hold.

He pulled the last unsealed document in the file — a routine fleet disposition summary, dated the week before the freeze. It listed Meridian's anchorage fleet. Eleven ships. He knew from the public record — everyone in the Core knew from the public record — that Meridian's fleet had been destroyed in the Battle of the Verge, a heroic last stand, a tragedy the Core still used in recruitment films.

The disposition summary listed eleven ships.

The Battle of the Verge casualty records — unsealed, public, taught in schools — listed *fourteen* ships lost from Meridian anchorage.

Jude read the numbers again. Eleven. Fourteen. Three ships that had been at Meridian and had not died at the Verge, and had not been decommissioned anywhere he could find, and had been erased from a file that was sealed within hours of Oscar Thackeray's death.

Three ships that had gone somewhere, done something, and been forgotten on purpose.

The archive terminal hummed. Somewhere above him, the medical center's night staff moved through their rounds, and beyond that the settlement slept under its failing heat grid, and beyond that the glacier stood over whatever Oscar had been walking toward when someone shot him in the dark.

Jude thought about Reyes' word. *Loyal.*

He had been loyal his whole life — both of his lives, the borrowed one and the brief one. He had believed the Core was gravity. He had believed it held things down so they didn't drift away.

But gravity didn't erase ships. Gravity didn't seal eleven years of a colony's history and murder the one man who'd gotten too close to the vault door. Gravity didn't send a smiling man with imported coffee to offer a commendation for looking away.

The Core wasn't gravity.

The Core was a hand on the scale.

Jude took the widow's data chip from his pocket and set it on the terminal beside the seal order, the coordinates and the freeze date side by side, and the two of them sat there together like a lock and its key.

Oscar had found something under the glacier. Something worth three erased ships. Something worth a Core-wide seal and a dead inspector and a decommission order signed in haste.

And whatever it was, it was still down there. Still buried. Still humming, if eleven dreaming children could be believed.

Jude copied the seal order to his own storage, wiped his access trail the way Oscar had taught him without ever teaching him, and climbed the stairs into the Meridian night.

The slate with the decommission order stayed unsigned in his pocket.

He had thirty-six hours, though he didn't know it yet — and out on the ice shelf, under four hundred meters of blue ice, something old had been waiting a long time for a Thackeray to come back.
