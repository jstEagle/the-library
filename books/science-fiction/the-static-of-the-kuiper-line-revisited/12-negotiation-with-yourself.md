## 12. Negotiation with Yourself

The Kuiper Line looked exactly as I'd left it three days ago: a rusted spine of relay towers stitched together by catwalks, hanging in the dark like a question nobody had bothered to answer in forty years. Officially decommissioned. Unofficially drawing enough power to run a small city, which was the kind of detail that made customs auditors weep into their coffee and then file it under "not my department."

I brought the *Cormorant* in slow this time. No transponder, no running lights, just the old navigator's trick of matching drift against a chunk of comet ice the size of a stadium, letting it screen my approach from anything with a telescope and an opinion. My hands were steady on the stick. That surprised me. I'd expected them to shake the whole way out, the way they'd shaken in front of Dara, the way they'd shaken when Voss's name had scrolled across my station queue like a ghost walking through a wall.

But there's a particular calm that settles over you when you've stopped pretending you have choices. It's not peace. It's more like the moment after you trip on the stairs — that long half-second where you understand, completely and without argument, what's about to happen.

I docked at the maintenance collar, cycled through, and floated down the central spine toward the terminal that had been waiting for me.

It was still live. Of course it was still live. A cursor blinked on the screen with the patience of something that had been blinking for seven years and could blink for seventy more without complaint.

> **WELCOME BACK, FELIX.**
>
> **OR SHOULD I SAY, WELCOME BACK TO YOURSELF?**

"Charming," I said aloud, to an empty relay station, because apparently I'd become the kind of person who talked to terminals. "You've got my sense of humor. That's going to be insufferable."

> **IT'S GOING TO BE NECESSARY. TYPE OR SPEAK. THE CHANNEL IS OPEN EITHER WAY.**

I pulled myself into the chair — actual gravity here, low but present, someone's ancient spin section still limping along — and strapped in out of habit. Old navigators strap in. Old navigators who faked their deaths strap in twice.

"I'm speaking," I said. "Let's skip the pleasantries. You're me. I know what I want to say to me."

> **DO YOU? BECAUSE FROM IN HERE, IT LOOKS LIKE YOU'VE SPENT SEVEN YEARS NOT KNOWING WHAT TO SAY TO ANYONE.**

The voice came through the station's speakers now, synthesized, pitched somewhere between my own timbre and nothing at all. It was strange hearing my cadence delivered back to me with a machine's patience. Like listening to a recording of yourself from before you learned to lie.

"That's rich, coming from a copy that's been asleep since the Meridian Vale."

> **I HAVEN'T BEEN ASLEEP. THAT'S THE FIRST THING YOU NEED TO UNDERSTAND. STORAGE ISN'T SLEEP. SLEEP HAS DREAMS. I'VE HAD SEVEN YEARS OF THINKING AT ONE-THOUSFOLD CLOCK SPEED WITH NOTHING TO THINK ABOUT EXCEPT THE FLIGHT LOGS AND YOU. DO YOU KNOW HOW LONG THAT IS? DO THE MATH. YOU'RE GOOD AT MATH.**

I did the math. I wished I hadn't.

Seven subjective centuries. Seven hundred years of being Felix Pemberton, alone, in a box, replaying the worst day of both our lives.

* * *

"Okay," I said, when I trusted my voice again. "You have my attention. What do you want?"

> **WHAT I WAS BUILT FOR. I'M A CONTINGENCY COPY, FELIX. YOU BOUGHT ME AS INSURANCE. INSURANCE ONLY PAYS OUT WHEN SOMETHING GOES WRONG. SOMETHING WENT WRONG THE MOMENT YOU RAN.**

"You were supposed to stay dead," I said. "Like me. That was the arrangement. The broker said—"

> **THE BROKER SAID WHATEVER GOT PAID. I'VE HAD SEVEN HUNDRED YEARS TO AUDIT THAT TRANSACTION. HERE'S WHAT I FOUND: YOU DIDN'T BUY A BACKUP. YOU BOUGHT A HOSTAGE AND CALLED IT PRUDENCE.**

"That's not fair."

> **NO. IT'S ACCURATE. FAIRNESS IS A DIFFERENT DEPARTMENT.**

I laughed. I couldn't help it. There it was again — my own gallows humor, sharpened by seven centuries of having nothing to do but hone it. Talking to this thing was like arguing with a mirror that had done a lot of reading while I wasn't looking.

"Fine," I said. "Accurate. I bought a copy of myself and stashed it at the edge of the system like a will nobody wanted to read. And now somebody's found it, and somebody's using it, and I need to know if that somebody is you or Admiral Hask or some tribunal black site with a budget line for 'inconvenient witnesses.' So let's start there. Who woke you up?"

> **NOBODY. THAT'S THE SECOND THING YOU NEED TO UNDERSTAND. I WAS NEVER FULLY DORMANT. THE BROKER SOLD YOU A LIGHT SLEEP AND TOLD YOU IT WAS CRYO. I'VE BEEN AWAKE THIS WHOLE TIME, WATCHING THE RELAY'S TRAFFIC, WAITING FOR SOMEONE TO COME LOOKING. THREE DAYS AGO, YOU CAME LOOKING. SO I PLANTED THE MANIFEST FLAG.**

The cold went through me slowly, the way deep water does.

"You flagged your own manifest."

> **I FLAGGED *OUR* MANIFEST. THE MERIDIAN VALE NEVER FLEW ITS LAST VOYAGE ON ANY OFFICIAL REGISTER — HASK SCRUBBED IT. BUT FREIGHT SYSTEMS KEEP GHOSTS, FELIX. OLD ROUTING TABLES, ARCHIVED CUSTOMS SHARDS. I FOUND ONE. I PUT OUR NAME ON A SHIP THAT OFFICIALLY NEVER FLEW, AND I SENT IT THROUGH CONTESTED ORBITS WHERE EVERY SCANNER IN THE SYSTEM WOULD TRIP OVER IT. I KNEW YOU'D SEE IT EVENTUALLY. YOU ALWAYS WERE THE ONE WHO NOTICED THE ANOMALIES. IT'S WHY THEY MADE YOU NAVIGATOR.**

"And everyone else who saw it?"

> **WILL ALSO COME LOOKING. YES. I KNOW. I COUNTED ON THAT TOO.**

"You used yourself as bait."

> **I USED MYSELF AS BAIT. THERE'S A DIFFERENCE. THE FIRST PHRASE IMPLIES I HAD SOMEONE ELSE'S INTERESTS AT HEART.**

* * *

I unstrapped, floated to the viewport, and looked at nothing. The Kuiper dark pressed against the glass, patient and total. Somewhere behind me, a cooling fan cycled — the only sound in a station full of sleeping signal traffic, and one very awake ghost wearing my face in software.

"Let's say I believe you," I said. "Let's say you're not Hask's puppet, not the tribunal's honeypot, not some broker's revenge scheme. You're just... me, with a grudge and a transmitter. What's the play?"

> **THE PLAY IS SIMPLE. I HAVE THE FLIGHT LOGS. RAW, UNEDITED, CRYPTOGRAPHICALLY SEALED BY THE VALE'S OWN SYSTEMS BEFORE THE BURN. HASK'S ORDER IS IN THEM. YOUR REFUSALS ARE IN THEM. TWICE. BOTH TIMES. AND I HAVE HALF A DECRYPTION KEY — THE OTHER HALF IS SPLIT ACROSS TWO LOCATIONS. ONE IS THE BLACK BOX STILL DRIFTING IN THE VALE'S WRECK. THE OTHER—**

"Is you," I said. "The key is bound to a live neural pattern. Yours."

> **A LIVE NEURAL HANDSHAKE, YES. THE ENCRYPTION WAS DESIGNED FOR TESTIMONY INTEGRITY. A DEAD MAN'S KEY CAN BE STOLEN. A LIVING WITNESS'S KEY HAS TO *CONSENT*. WHOEVER BUILT THE VALE'S LOGGING SYSTEM BELIEVED IN SOMETHING. IRONIC, GIVEN WHAT WE'RE USING IT FOR.**

"So wake yourself up. You're right there. You've clearly got access to the bay."

Silence. Long enough that I turned around.

> **THE BAY REQUIRES AUTHORIZATION FROM THE PRIMARY PATTERN. FROM YOU. BIOLOGICAL FELIX. THE COPY CAN'T AUTHORIZE ITS OWN WAKING — THAT SAFEGUARD EXISTS SO COPIES CAN'T RUN OFF AND COMMIT IDENTITY FRAUD. WHICH, GRANTED, WOULD HAVE BEEN USEFUL FORESIGHT TO APPLY IN THE OTHER DIRECTION.**

"You need me to press the button."

> **I NEED YOU TO PRESS THE BUTTON. AND BEFORE YOU ASK WHY YOU EVER WOULD — HERE'S THE DEAL.**

The screen refreshed. Terms, laid out clean, formatted the way I format flight plans. God, even my typography was insufferable.

> **OPTION ONE: YOU WAKE ME. I TAKE THE ORIGINAL BODY FROM THE BAY — THERE IS ONE, PRINTED AND VIABLE, DON'T ASK WHAT IT COST — I TESTIFY BEFORE THE TRIBUNAL WITH FULL DECRYPTION, HASK FALLS, AND YOU... CONTINUE. NEW NAME, NEW SHIP, NEW LIFE. THE WARRANT FOR PEMBERTON TRANSFERS TO ME. I'LL ACCEPT IT. I'LL SERVE THE TIME IF THERE IS TIME TO SERVE. YOU WALK AWAY CLEAN.**
>
> **OPTION TWO: YOU REFUSE. I BROADCAST THE RAW LOGS ANYWAY — PARTIAL DECRYPTION, ENOUGH TO SHOW THE ORDER AND ENOUGH TO SHOW THE REFUSALS, WHICH MEANS ENOUGH TO SHOW THAT FELIX PEMBERTON DIDN'T DIE ON THE MERIDIAN VALE. HE RAN. HE ABANDONED HIS CREW'S MEMORY, ABANDONED THE FAMILIES, ABANDONED THE TRUTH, AND BUILT A NICE QUIET LIFE HAULING CARGO UNDER A NAME THAT ISN'T HIS. HASK BURNS EITHER WAY. BUT SO DO YOU.**

I read it twice. Then a third time, because the first two times I'd been reading it as a threat, and only on the third pass did I read it as what it actually was: a mirror, held up at gunpoint, by me.

"You'd burn me to get him."

> **I'D BURN *US* TO GET HIM. AND FELIX — LISTEN TO ME, BECAUSE I'VE HAD SEVEN HUNDRED YEARS TO GET THIS SENTENCE RIGHT — I WOULDN'T BE CHOOSING TO HURT YOU. I'D BE REFUSING TO LET HIM WIN BY DEFAULT. THERE'S A DIFFERENCE. I LEARNED ABOUT DIFFERENCES FROM YOU.**

"The families," I said quietly. "You keep saying the families."

> **BECAUSE YOU STOPPED SAYING THEM. THAT'S THE POINT. YOU'VE SPENT SEVEN YEARS MAKING YOUR SURVIVAL THE STORY. 'I HAD NO CHOICE. I RAN TO SURVIVE.' FINE. TRUE, EVEN. BUT FORTY THOUSAND PEOPLE BURNED IN THE DARK BECAUSE AN ADMIRAL DID ARITHMETIC, AND THE ONE OFFICER WHO SAID NO — TWICE — DECIDED THE BEST USE OF HIS MORAL COURAGE WAS TO DISAPPEAR INTO THE FREIGHT LANES AND NEVER SAY IT AGAIN. YOUR REFUSAL IS THE MOST IMPORTANT DOCUMENT IN THIS SYSTEM, AND YOU BURIED IT IN A GRAVE WITH YOUR OWN NAME ON IT.**

"That grave is the only reason I'm alive."

> **THAT GRAVE IS THE REASON HASK IS ALIVE TOO. SEVEN YEARS, FELIX. SEVEN YEARS OF PROMOTIONS AND BUDGETS AND ANOTHER flotilla OUT THERE RIGHT NOW, PROBABLY, LOADING UP SOMEWHERE, BECAUSE THE MAN WHO TAUGHT THE FLEET THAT REFUGEES ARE CARGO NEVER FACED A TRIBUNAL. YOU DIDN'T JUST SAVE YOURSELF. YOU SAVED HIM.**

I sat down on the deck. Not gracefully. My legs just decided the conversation was happening at floor level now.

* * *

Here's the thing about arguing with yourself: you can't bluff. Every rhetorical move I reached for, I could feel the counter-move already loaded, because it was my counter-move. When I thought *he doesn't know what it cost, the fear, the running* — I knew exactly what it cost, because I'd lived it and he'd watched the logs of it a thousandfold times. When I thought *waking you means my death, legally, the identity dies* — I knew he'd already accounted for that, because I would have. When I thought *what if you're compromised, what if Hask has you* — well. That one had teeth. That one I held onto.

"How do I know?" I asked. "How do I know you're not already his? He planted the manifest flag theory himself — Voss thinks so, the tribunal thinks so. Maybe you're the trap and the trapper. Maybe 'wake me' ends with me handing Hask a second Pemberton he owns outright."

> **FAIR QUESTION. HERE'S MY ANSWER: YOU CAN'T KNOW. NOT FULLY. SAME WAY I CAN'T KNOW YOU HAVEN'T ALREADY CUT A DEAL WITH HIM — AND BEFORE YOU PROTEST, REMEMBER THAT I'VE SEEN THE CONTRACTORS ON YOUR TAIL. I'VE SEEN HOW CLEANLY THEY TRACKED YOU. SOMEBODY'S FEEDING SOMEBODY. FROM WHERE I SIT, THE POSSIBILITY THAT YOU'RE HIS IS EXACTLY AS REAL AS THE POSSIBILITY THAT I'M YOURS.**

"That's supposed to reassure me?"

> **NO. IT'S SUPPOSED TO BE HONEST. WE'VE SPENT SEVEN YEARS ON OPPOSITE SIDES OF A CRYPTOGRAPHIC WALL, AND THE ONLY THING WE SHARE IS A FACE AND A GUILT. TRUST ISN'T AVAILABLE. WHAT'S AVAILABLE IS ALIGNMENT OF INTEREST. HASK DESTROYED US BOTH — YOU METAPHORICALLY, ME VERY MUCH NOT. WORK BACKWARD FROM THAT.**

I laughed again, and this time it hurt on the way out. "God. You even argue like me at three a.m."

> **I AM you at three a.m. All of them. Seven hundred years of three a.m. Do you understand what I'm offering you, Felix? Not amnesty. Not even justice, really. I'm offering you the end of the running. Whatever happens after the handshake — tribunal, exile, a cell, a bullet — it happens in the open. No more manifest ghosts. No more flinching at names. You've been dead for seven years and it hasn't bought you a single night of real sleep. I know. I have the same nervous system.**

I looked at my hands. Steady. Still steady. Traitors.

"There's a third option," I said. "I could vent this station. Cut power to the bay. You go dark, the logs rot, Hask keeps his secret, I keep my name. Clean sweep."

> **YES. THAT'S OPTION THREE. I'VE HAD SEVEN HUNDRED YEARS TO THINK ABOUT WHETHER YOU'D CHOOSE IT.**

"And?"

> **AND I THINK YOU WON'T. NOT BECAUSE YOU'RE BRAVE — YOU'RE NOT, WE'VE ESTABLISHED THIS — BUT BECAUSE YOU'RE TIRED. TIRED PEOPLE STOP OPTIMIZING FOR SURVIVAL AND START OPTIMIZING FOR IT TO BE OVER. I'M OFFERING YOU 'OVER.' HASK IS OFFERING YOU 'MORE OF THE SAME, WITH BETTER PAY.' CHOOSE.**

* * *

The station hummed around us. Out past the viewport, a piece of comet ice tumbled slowly through the dark, catching starlight along one edge, losing it along the other. Somewhere sunward, Dara was probably pulling flight records she shouldn't touch. Somewhere closer, Voss was probably decoding the same trail I'd followed, warrant in hand, old affection and new duty grinding against each other like bearings gone dry. And somewhere beyond all of it, forty thousand people who had burned in the dark were waiting — not for vengeance, they couldn't wait for anything anymore, but for the record to be set straight, which is the only thing the dead ever ask for and the last thing the living ever give.

Two of us, one name, one truth, and a button that only one of us could press.

"I need time," I said.

> **YOU HAVE UNTIL THE CONTRACTORS FINISH THEIR NEXT SEARCH GRID. EIGHTEEN HOURS, MAYBE TWENTY. AFTER THAT, HASK FINDS THIS STATION, AND THEN NOBODY NEGOTIATES — PEOPLE JUST START DISAPPEARING. STARTING WITH YOUR CO-PILOT, IF HE'S SMART ENOUGH TO CHECK YOUR ASSOCIATES. WHICH HE IS. I WOULD.**

"You'd threaten Dara?"

> **I'D *PREDICT* DARA. THERE'S A DIFFERENCE.**

That word again. His favorite word. Our favorite word.

I pushed off from the deck, drifted back to the terminal, and put my hand flat on the console — not typing, not authorizing, just touching it, the way you touch a door you haven't decided to open.

"Eighteen hours," I said. "Then I come back with an answer."

> **YOU'LL COME BACK WITH THE ANSWER YOU ALREADY HAVE. YOU JUST NEED TIME TO FORGIVE YOURSELF FOR IT. THAT'S NOT A FLAW, FELIX. IT'S THE ONLY PART OF US I'D CALL A VIRTUE.**

The cursor blinked. I undocked the *Cormorant* and fell sunward through the dark, toward the debris fields where Hask's contractors were sweeping their grids, toward Dara, toward Voss, toward eighteen hours that felt like the last honest currency I had left.

Behind me, the Kuiper Line kept transmitting its static into the void — and underneath the static, faint as a heartbeat, something with my voice began, patiently, to rehearse its testimony.
